MECHANISM: A set of coupled accumulating quantities and the rates that move mass between them is specified to represent interacting subsystems, with feedback links drawn as a signed influence network. Each accumulator's time derivative is written as the algebraic difference of its inflow and outflow rates, where rates are functions of other quantities, fixed coefficients, and auxiliary variables, yielding a closed system of coupled ordinary differential equations. The equations are integrated forward in discrete annual time steps from specified initial values to produce long-horizon trajectories of every quantity. A subset of coefficients that cannot be measured is calibrated by an evolutionary search: a population of candidate coefficient vectors is iterated through selection, recombination, and mutation over many generations to minimize a fitness equal to the root-mean-square deviation between model-implied importance scores and externally elicited target weights across a fixed number of factors, subject to bounded ranges. The calibrated coefficients are inserted back into the system. Structural and behavioral checks confirm the trajectories reproduce expected baseline patterns. A local sensitivity analysis perturbs selected coefficients by a fixed percentage above and below baseline and records the resulting change in output trajectories to rank leverage. Finally, several intervention scenarios are defined by setting control coefficients to discrete intensity levels, each is simulated over the horizon from a common start delay, and the resulting output trajectories are compared against the unperturbed baseline to assess relative effect and threshold-like nonlinearities.
