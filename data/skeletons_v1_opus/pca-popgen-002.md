MECHANISM: The goal is to recover the number of latent groups underlying a large rectangular array of integer-valued observations on entities by examining the spectrum of its second-moment matrix. Each entry is modeled as a bounded random count whose mean reflects a group-specific latent rate, so the data array decomposes as a deterministic low-rank mean part plus a zero-mean bounded-variance noise part. The uncentered cross-product matrix over entities is formed and its eigenvalues are taken in decreasing order. The analysis treats this as a finite-rank perturbation of a random matrix: the low-rank mean contributes a number of large singular values equal to the number of well-separated groups, governed by a small group-by-group moment matrix, while the noise part has an operator norm bounded by a constant times the square root of the dimensions. Perturbation bounds for singular values then show that, with probability approaching one, exactly that many eigenvalues exceed a dimension-free threshold after rescaling, while the remaining eigenvalues collapse into a bounded bulk whose shape follows the limiting law for noise covariance. Counting the eigenvalues above the threshold yields the estimate of the number of groups. The leading large eigenvalues are predicted in closed form from the eigenvalues of the small moment matrix and the two array dimensions, giving a quantitative resolution condition. The analysis shows the smallest separating eigenvalue scales with the number of entities, so power depends more on the number of entities than on the number of features. The output is an integer count plus conditions under which it is exact.
