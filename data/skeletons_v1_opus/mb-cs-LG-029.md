MECHANISM: A generator that builds discrete objects incrementally is modeled as a distribution over paths in a directed acyclic graph, with the goal that terminal objects are produced with probability proportional to a given nonnegative score. Because the normalizing constant is intractable, training is posed as matching a forward transition distribution to a backward target distribution. A scalar potential is attached to each node, and a balance identity is imposed: over any partial path between two nodes, the expected combination of the forward step-probabilities and the endpoint potentials must equal the corresponding reverse combination. The key result proves that the potential satisfying this partial-path balance, for a fixed forward transition rule, coincides exactly with a directed divergence between forward and backward path distributions from that node, turning the potential into a consistent evaluator of policy mismatch. A squared-residual objective over sampled partial paths of varying length, with length-dependent weights, is minimized by stochastic gradient steps to fit the evaluator. The transition rule is then improved by a gradient ascent step using an advantage formed from accumulated step-rewards and potential differences, with a discount-like knob trading bias against variance. Forward and backward transition rules and their evaluators can be updated jointly in a single phase, and the same residual form supports off-distribution data collection. Analogous identities and proofs are derived for the reverse direction.
