MECHANISM: A sequence of observations is modeled as governed by an unobserved discrete-valued latent variable that evolves as a first-order Markov chain over a finite set of states, characterized by a transition matrix whose rows are probability vectors. Conditional on the current latent state and exogenous inputs, each observation is drawn from a state-specific emission distribution. The stationary state distribution is obtained as the fixed point of the transition operator. A forward recursion integrates out the latent states: at each step the predictive density combines the emission likelihood with forecasted state probabilities, which decompose into the product of transition probabilities and the previously filtered state probabilities, yielding filtered probabilities by normalization and the one-step-ahead observation density. The total likelihood is the product over time of these conditional predictive densities. Parameters are estimated either by maximizing this likelihood through an iterative expectation-maximization scheme that alternates a filtering-smoothing pass with a parameter update, or by a Bayesian data-augmentation scheme that samples states and parameters from full conditionals via a forward-filtering backward-sampling pass within a Markov chain. Structured restrictions on the transition matrix are imposed through a linear parameterization with fixed selector matrices, encompassing mixtures, change-point (absorbing-state) chains, and tensor-product compositions of independent chains. Time-varying transition probabilities are parameterized by a multinomial-logistic link on covariates. An infinite-state extension replaces the finite chain with a nonparametric prior over an unbounded number of states, estimated via truncation or sampling. An endogenous variant couples the latent driving process to the observation noise through a threshold discretization of a correlated continuous autoregressive factor.
