MECHANISM: A target scalar quantity can in principle be recovered by several competing structural specifications, each producing its own estimating functional that equals the target only if that specification's assumptions hold. For each specification a scalar diagnostic parameter is defined from the observed distribution that is exactly zero when a testable implied constraint holds, providing data-driven evidence of validity. Rather than selecting one specification, the method forms a smooth weighted average of the specification-specific functionals, where each weight is a normalized Gaussian kernel of its diagnostic parameter, so specifications whose constraints appear satisfied receive larger weight. The bandwidth controls sharpness; as it shrinks the weighting approaches hard selection. It is proven that the distance between this aggregated functional and the true target is bounded by the worst bias among invalid specifications divided by a discrimination factor that grows when valid specifications have smaller diagnostics than invalid ones, and the bound vanishes in a limit when at least one specification is valid and detectable. For estimation, each functional and each diagnostic is estimated by an asymptotically linear estimator with a known influence function, possibly using flexible nuisance estimators. Joint asymptotic normality of the stacked estimators follows, and the delta method with the gradient of the weighting map gives the limiting variance of the aggregate. A sample covariance of influence functions plus the plug-in gradient yields a variance estimate and confidence intervals; resampling alternatives are given when influence functions are unavailable. A small stabilizing term guards against a near-zero normalizer.
