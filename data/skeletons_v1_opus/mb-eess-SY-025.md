MECHANISM: A collection of entities is placed at random positions in a bounded planar region, and a connectivity relation links any two entities whose pairwise Euclidean distance falls below a fixed radius, producing a random spatial graph over which multi-hop paths are sought. For each link a received-signal quantity is drawn from a distance-dependent attenuation model plus an additive random term, and a derived signal-to-noise quantity is computed; from it a per-link success probability is obtained as a function raised to a length exponent. A scalar carrier-selection index is advanced step by step through a one-dimensional nonlinear recurrence whose parameter places it in a chaotic regime, generating a non-periodic sequence used to pick the operating band each step. A feedback update rule adjusts each transmitter's power up or down by a fixed increment to hold the signal quantity near a target threshold. Route choice is made locally by assigning each candidate link a composite scalar cost that linearly combines its unreliability and the inverse of the source node's residual budget, and forwarding along least-cost links. When a transmission fails, a neighbor that overheard it is recruited with fixed probability and the relay with the best instantaneous channel is selected, adding spatial diversity. The whole system is advanced over many discrete epochs in which channel realizations, hopping, power adaptation, routing, and relaying are recomputed, with an adversarial interference term injected over a window of epochs. Outcomes such as delivered-fraction, channel quality, and end-to-end delay are accumulated, and the entire run is repeated over several independent random seeds and averaged. The procedure is a stochastic discrete-event simulation rather than a closed-form derivation.
