MECHANISM: The study assesses how local feedback rules at distributed injection nodes interact across a connected network of conductors. It first derives a closed-form sensitivity relation: from the complex-valued node-balance relation linking injected real and imaginary power, node states, and branch impedances, the deviation of a node state from its source value is expanded into real and imaginary parts, and partial derivatives of the state-magnitude deviation with respect to the two power components are taken, showing each is proportional to a corresponding impedance component divided by the node magnitude. This yields the regime distinction in which the state magnitude is dominated by one or the other power component depending on the resistance-to-reactance ratio of the branch. Each controllable node is governed by a piecewise-linear feedback curve mapping its measured local state into a commanded power component through a dead-band, a proportional droop slope, and saturation limits. Two such laws are considered, one acting on the imaginary-power component and one curtailing the real-power component. The dynamic behavior of the full network, with grouped nodes each running an assigned feedback law, is then integrated forward in real time through a sequence of scripted disturbance events that step the input resource level and the source state up and down. Responses of real power, imaginary power, the power ratio, and node states at the coupling points of each group are recorded and compared across combinations of feedback laws and across two branch-impedance configurations to quantify cross-group influence transmitted through the shared impedance.
