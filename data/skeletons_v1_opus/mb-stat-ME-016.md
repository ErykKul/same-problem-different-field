MECHANISM: Each entity carries a categorical-valued trajectory over a bounded interval, observed at one of many planar locations. The trajectories are first reduced to fixed-dimensional real vectors by an optimal-scaling step that solves an eigenproblem: indicator functions of each category are encoded by deterministic functions, and one finds orthogonal projections maximizing a between-state variance ratio integrated over the interval, which reduces to an integral eigen-equation whose leading eigenvectors and eigenvalues give the coordinates, retaining enough components to explain most variance. Over the encoded vectors a moving-window search is run: candidate regions are nested discs centered at one location and reaching another, each containing at most half the entities. For each candidate a nonparametric rank-based statistic is evaluated; multivariate spatial signs are transformed so the resulting ranks are spherically distributed, and a concentration index contrasts the mean ranks inside versus outside the window. The scan statistic is the maximum of this index over all candidate windows, and the maximizing window is reported as the most likely region. Because the null distribution of the maximum is intractable, significance is assessed by repeatedly permuting the location labels, recomputing the maximized statistic, and computing an empirical exceedance proportion as the p-value. The region is declared significant if its p-value falls below a fixed level. Performance is assessed on simulated label assignments by power, true/false positive rates, and predictive value.
