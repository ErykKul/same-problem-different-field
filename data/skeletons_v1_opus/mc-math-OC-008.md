MECHANISM: The problem is to maximize a nonnegative, non-monotone objective with a relaxed diminishing-returns property over a downward-closed convex region of the unit box. The method is projection-free and uses only first-order information: at each step it solves a linear maximization of the gradient over the feasible region to obtain an ascent direction, and advances by a measured fractional update whose schedule is tuned by the relaxation parameter. A complementary bracketing routine maintains a lower and an upper candidate and adjusts coordinates in opposing directions with parameter-aware thresholds, yielding a one-sided value guarantee. Key inequalities relating the objective at a point to its values at the coordinatewise join and meet are derived in asymmetric, parameter-weighted form, since the relaxation breaks the symmetry exploited in the strict case. Each subroutine emits a certified lower bound on the achieved value as a linear combination of the optimum's value and auxiliary terms; a convex mixture of the two certificates is then optimized over mixing and schedule parameters to give the best provable factor. The driver applies these subroutines recursively, passing forward an approximate-local-optimum seed along a designated chain when a step fails its large-value criterion, and a potential that strictly decreases each level bounds the recursion depth. The analysis proves the resulting factor interpolates smoothly in the relaxation parameter, matches the strict-case constant at the boundary, and the algorithm runs in time polynomial in the dimension and inverse discretization.
