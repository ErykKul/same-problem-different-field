MECHANISM: The aim is to model the conditional mean of a continuous outcome as a flexible function of two sets of observed covariates attached to two interacting classes of entities, then to decompose the variance of the outcome into interpretable additive parts. First, the outcome is regressed on each entity's covariates with a recursively partitioning tree, and the terminal leaves define discrete cells that group entities of each side by similar predicted outcomes. Second, the full conditional mean function is fit nonparametrically with a boosted ensemble of additive trees that captures nonlinear interactions, using a blocked cross-fitting scheme that splits both entity indices into disjoint blocks so each observation is predicted by a model trained excluding its own two blocks, yielding unbiased held-out predictions despite two-sided dependence. The number of cells on each side is selected by minimizing held-out prediction error. Third, cell means are computed and projected by a weighted least-squares onto additive indicators for the two sides, giving orthogonal main-effect terms plus an interaction remainder; the outcome variance then splits exactly into a term per side, a cross-covariance sorting term, an interaction term, and a residual. Marginal-effect summaries are produced by averaging the fitted function over the distribution of the remaining covariates, and a correlation-respecting local-accumulation variant integrates binned local derivatives to avoid extrapolation. Out-of-sample fit is benchmarked against linear baselines on a held-out subset of entities. The decomposition isolates how much dispersion comes from each side, from assortative matching, and from non-additive complementarity.
