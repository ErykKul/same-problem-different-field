MECHANISM: The task is to assign each input image to one of several categories while emitting a transparent record of which measured properties drove the decision. A raw input is first converted into many interpretable numeric descriptors at several scales (per-object morphology, texture, intensity, position, and aggregate compositional and spatial statistics), which are stored as rows of relational tables. Language-driven agents, given the table schema and the candidate categories, compose declarative aggregation queries that select, filter, group, and arithmetically combine the stored descriptors into summary findings; the queries are validated for schema and syntax before execution and act as the explicit reasoning trace. A comparison step evaluates each candidate category by checking the queried summaries against reference value ranges (some from background knowledge, some empirical from training data) and converts categorical goodness-of-fit judgments into numeric weights aggregated into a calibrated confidence per category. In parallel, a separately trained convolutional predictor maps the raw input to category probabilities. A final fusion step arbitrates between the two confidence sources to output a category and a rationale; agreement reinforces the result while disagreement flags review. Evaluation uses a held-out split with top-one accuracy and ablations isolating the contribution of the structured-query reasoning, the visual predictor, and their fusion. The genuinely computed components are the learned visual classifier and the rule-based confidence aggregation and fusion; the rest is orchestration of query generation and knowledge comparison.
