MECHANISM: A multi-channel grid-structured input is mapped through a deep layered nonlinear function to a single continuous scalar output, trained by minimizing a bounded saturating loss between predicted and target scalars over a large labeled collection. A base predictor is first fit on a large, lower-quality labeled collection. It is then adapted to a smaller, higher-quality labeled collection in three ways that are compared: freezing the bulk of the parameters and retraining only the input and output sub-blocks; inserting low-rank additive correction factors into every linear and convolutional weight so that only a small fraction of parameters is updated; and retraining the whole function on the merged collection. The low-rank correction parameterizes each weight update as a product of two thin matrices of small inner dimension, sharply limiting the number of trainable values. Optimization uses adaptive first-order gradient steps with a plateau-triggered learning-rate reduction and an early-stopping rule based on a smoothed validation loss. Evaluation reports the median signed relative deviation, a robust scaled spread of that deviation, and the fraction of gross outliers, computed on held-out subsets of each collection. A data-fraction sweep measures how performance and training time scale as the adaptation collection is subsampled. The study contrasts adaptation quality, generalization across collections, and compute cost across the three strategies. The core computation is gradient-based fitting of a layered nonlinear regressor with a parameter-efficient adaptation mechanism.
