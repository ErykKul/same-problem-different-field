MECHANISM: A large pretrained sequence model that generates tokens one at a time is augmented with an auxiliary scoring capability that estimates, at any point during generation, the probability that the partially produced sequence is on track to a correct outcome. Low-rank additive corrections are attached in parallel to selected internal linear maps and are switched on only when a special probe token is inserted, so that in the default mode the base model is unchanged. When the probe token is inserted at a position, it attends over the cached intermediate key and value representations accumulated for the preceding sequence, reusing them rather than recomputing, so each query costs a single forward pass. The probe's final-layer representation is mapped by a small stacked-nonlinearity head to one scalar in the unit interval. To train this scalar predictor, each position in a generated sequence is assigned a soft target that interpolates from a neutral value toward the final binary correctness label as the position advances, and the predictor is fit by minimizing mean squared error against these targets. The whole sequence of position-wise scores is computed in one pass by issuing many probe queries simultaneously under a triangular mask that restricts each probe to its own prefix. At deployment, many candidate sequences are generated and the scalar scores are used to weight, prune, or early-stop them, keeping shorter higher-scoring candidates. Design choices such as the corrector rank, the target-shaping rule, and the score-pooling window are varied to study their effect.
