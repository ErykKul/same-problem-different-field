MECHANISM: Consider a quantity that evolves by continuous random fluctuation within a bounded region while a separate two-state indicator switches on and off according to a memoryless jump process. The goal is the joint probability density over the quantity's value and the indicator state, together with the distribution of the first time at which the quantity reaches a boundary while the indicator is in the permitting state. One first writes a forward evolution equation (a diffusion operator plus linear switching terms) with mixed boundary conditions and solves it via an integral transform in time, reducing it to an ordinary differential boundary-value problem whose solution uses an elementary Green's function. An equivalent and more general route decomposes each trajectory into an alternating sequence of bulk excursions and instantaneous boundary contacts, terminated when a contact coincides with the permitting state. This yields an implicit integral (renewal) equation relating the gated density to the simpler density for an always-absorbing boundary, with each contact restarting the process a small offset away to avoid immediate re-contact. Transforming the integral equation and applying the convolution property converts it into an algebraic relation, summed as a geometric series over the number of non-terminating contacts to give a closed expression. Taking the offset to zero recovers the direct solution, evaluated through a limiting (L'Hopital) argument. In higher dimensions the boundary contact becomes a spatial integral, solved by an eigenfunction expansion and a boundary surface-to-flux operator. Survival probabilities and mean first-reach times follow by integrating the resulting densities, and special limits reproduce a partially-permeable boundary condition.
