MECHANISM: Repeated measurements are collected on many entities over several ordered time points, with a response and several time-varying explanatory quantities per entity at each point. The expected response is modeled marginally as a link-transformed linear combination of the contemporaneous explanatory value and its earlier (lagged) values, without conditioning on response history. Rather than fitting one coefficient per lag (a high-dimensional, unstable expansion) or a single pooled coefficient (which hides temporal structure), the lagged coefficients are grouped into a small number of predefined blocks that share a common coefficient, while the contemporaneous coefficient is kept separate. Estimation proceeds by forming moment conditions: for each explanatory quantity, an indicator structure flags which (time-point, lag) combinations yield valid orthogonality conditions between the residual and the explanatory value, based on a classification of how the quantity relates to past and future responses. Valid lag-specific moment functions are summed within each block according to the grouping. The stacked sample moment vector is averaged over entities, and a quadratic form of this vector weighted by a positive-definite matrix is minimized to obtain the estimator; the weighting matrix is taken as an estimate of the inverse long-run moment covariance. For binary responses a logistic mean function supplies the link and the corresponding derivative weights. The nonlinear minimization is solved by a quasi-Newton iterative method that approximates the curvature. Finite-sample behavior is assessed by simulating coverage and interval length under several lag and feedback structures.
