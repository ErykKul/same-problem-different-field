MECHANISM: A collection of unit vectors in a high-dimensional complex space, each carrying a probability weight, is summarized by its order-k moment operator, defined as the weighted expectation of the k-fold tensor power of the rank-one projector onto each vector. Two such collections are compared by the trace norm of the difference of their moment operators, giving a hierarchy of approximation notions indexed by k, with both additive and multiplicative (sandwich) error definitions. A reference maximum-entropy collection consistent with a fixed average operator is defined as the minimizer of an accessible-information functional; its explicit form deforms a uniform measure on the sphere by the square root of the average operator, and its k-th moment is an integral of a rational matrix function over the uniform measure. A key lemma shows that an unnormalized version, obtained by applying the square-root deformation to uniform-measure vectors, has a simple tensor-product moment that approximates the normalized reference's moment to an error controlled by the second Schatten norm (purity) of the average operator. Building on this, three theorems give sufficient conditions for emergence: long-time evolution under a generic Hamiltonian (no spectral resonances) yields an approximate design from random phases; conditioning one factor of a bipartite state drawn from a higher-order design yields a local design; and rotating the conditioning basis by a higher-order random unitary yields a local design for an arbitrary bipartite state. Each theorem carries explicit error bounds in moment order and subsystem dimensions. Numerical experiments construct states by structured circuits, compute reduced operators and k-th moment operators directly, and report the trace-norm distance as a function of system size, doping, and basis complexity.
