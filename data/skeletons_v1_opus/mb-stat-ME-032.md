MECHANISM: Observations are pairs of a possibly truncated outcome time and an indicator, with a covariate vector, and the cumulative intensity of an associated counting process is modeled as a linear combination of covariates with coefficient functions that may vary with time. Each covariate is assigned to one of three disjoint index sets: an unrestricted coefficient function, a constant coefficient, or exclusion, yielding three-to-the-power-of-the-covariate-count candidate submodels. For each submodel a least-squares-type estimator integrates a previsible weight operator against the increments of the counting process, where the weight involves projections built from inverses of accumulated outer-product matrices restricted to the active covariate columns. A scalar focus quantity, a linear functional of the cumulative coefficient functions evaluated at a target covariate vector and time, is targeted, and its estimator's mean squared error is decomposed into a variance term and a squared-bias term. The variance term is estimated by an integral of the squared weight against the observed increments, shown to converge to a limit. The squared-bias term is estimated as the squared difference between the submodel estimate and the full-model estimate, minus an estimate of that difference's variance, truncated at zero. The selection criterion sums the estimated variance and the truncated squared bias, and the submodel minimizing it is chosen. A weighted extension integrates the per-target criterion against a nonrandom or empirical weighting measure over a region of targets. Estimates are then averaged across top submodels with exponential weights, and a resampling scheme drawing synthetic outcomes from the fitted full model is used to approximate the selected estimator's sampling distribution and intervals.
