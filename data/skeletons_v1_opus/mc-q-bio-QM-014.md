MECHANISM: A scalar target quantity is predicted from an entity represented by two parallel feature vectors: a richer, more informative descriptor available only at training time, and a cheaper, weaker descriptor available at both training and deployment. Two feedforward networks are defined, a higher-capacity one consuming the rich descriptor and a lighter one consuming the weak descriptor, each mapping its input through nonlinear layers to a latent representation and a linear scalar output head. The rich-input network is trained by minimizing the squared error between its output and the ground-truth target. The weak-input network is trained by minimizing a convex combination of its own squared error against the target and a transfer term that penalizes the squared difference between its output and the rich network's output; an optional additional term penalizes the squared difference between the two networks' intermediate latent representations. Gradients from the transfer terms flow only into the weak-input network, while the rich network's outputs and representations are treated as fixed targets, so structural information is implicitly transplanted into the weak predictor. Both networks are optimized; at inference only the weak-input network is used, requiring just the cheap descriptor. All feature vectors are standardized to zero mean and unit variance. Performance is assessed by leave-one-entity-out cross-validation, computing correlation and error metrics averaged over repeated runs, with a held-out external set and diagnostic residual, agreement, and error-distribution analyses confirming reduced bias relative to the weak-only baseline.
