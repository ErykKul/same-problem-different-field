MECHANISM: A noisy one-dimensional signal is processed into a labeled feature vector per event so that a simple linear separator can assign each event to one of a few classes. The raw sequence is first passed through a band-limited recursive filter applied forward and backward to remove drift and out-of-band noise without phase shift. Salient landmarks are located by an ensemble of detectors whose votes are weighted by a signal-quality score, and the sequence is cut into fixed-length segments by minimizing a composite per-segment cost trading off entropy, signal-to-noise, and length penalties. From each segment a high-dimensional descriptor is built from time-domain moments, transform-domain band energies computed via a discrete Fourier transform and multiresolution decomposition, and shape attributes. Inter-segment structure is added by building a weighted directed graph whose edge weights combine feature-vector cosine similarity with an exponential decay in index distance; a stationary-importance score is computed by iterating a damped stochastic transition map to a fixed point, and local clustering scores are computed per node. The enlarged descriptor is reduced by ranking features on a mutual-information criterion and recursive elimination, then a low-rank projection onto leading variance directions is appended. Class imbalance is corrected by synthetic minority oversampling with boundary cleaning. A linear classifier is finally fit by minimizing a regularized average loss, and accuracy plus cross-validated confidence intervals are reported. Importance is read off directly from the linear coefficients.
