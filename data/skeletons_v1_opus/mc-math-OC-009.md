MECHANISM: The goal is to minimize a scalar objective functional over a parametrized family of objects, where each object is itself a probability-weighted spread described by a location vector and a positive-definite spread matrix. A population of many such candidate objects is evolved over continuous time. At each instant a consensus candidate is formed as a weighted average of the whole population, with weights given by an exponential of the negative objective so that better candidates dominate. Each candidate is then driven deterministically toward this consensus while also receiving a stochastic perturbation whose magnitude scales with its distance from the consensus, so that distant members explore more. To make the averaging and the noise well defined, the curved parameter space is replaced by a flat inner-product space via a fixed reference object and a transport map, turning the dynamics into an ordinary system in a finite-dimensional Euclidean space. The continuous dynamics are discretized by a simple stochastic time-stepping scheme that requires only evaluations of the objective, with no gradients. Well-posedness is shown under a local Lipschitz condition on the objective, and a large-population limit is taken to reduce the interacting system to a single self-consistent process. Under boundedness and curvature assumptions, the spread of the population is shown to collapse and the consensus is shown to approach a global minimizer at an exponential rate, with the gap controlled by a sharpening parameter. A regularization that clips degenerate spreads guarantees the analysis stays in the valid region.
