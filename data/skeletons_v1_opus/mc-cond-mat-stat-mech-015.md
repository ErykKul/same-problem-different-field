MECHANISM: The expected value of a quantity obtained by clipping a Gaussian variable at zero is computed in closed form as a sum of a mean term scaled by the Gaussian cumulative function and a scale term scaled by the Gaussian density, yielding a single shape function of the standardized mean. This output is fed back as the mean of the next stage, defining a one-dimensional nonlinear recursion whose multiplier is the ratio of scales divided by a fixed constant. Under a recentering that holds the standardized mean at zero, the shape function reduces to a constant and the stage values form a geometric sequence with ratio proportional to the scale parameter. Summing the infinite chain gives a closed-form total that converges if and only if the ratio is below one, fixing a critical scale at the square root of two pi; at the critical value the recursion becomes pure iteration of the shape function and is self-similar. Introducing a survival filter that retains only realized draws exceeding a threshold multiple of the stake replaces the multiplier by a conditional expectation equal to the scale times the ratio of the Gaussian density to the tail probability, lowering the critical scale and giving its limit as the threshold approaches zero. Because the surviving count decays geometrically while each survivor's value grows geometrically, the outcome distribution acquires a power-law tail whose exponent is the negative ratio of the logarithms of survival probability and conditional growth, expressed in closed form. A large-population stochastic simulation iterating the same clipped-Gaussian draw with a continuation rule confirms the transition from a curved to a straight rank-value relation across the threshold.
