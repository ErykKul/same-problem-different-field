MECHANISM: The aim is to model the full conditional distribution of a continuous outcome given covariates when the error structure is non-constant and non-normal, rather than only its mean. In a first stage each outcome value is mapped to the unit interval through a smoothed empirical cumulative-distribution transform, which preserves rank order and removes scale-specific distortion; this transform is shown to be consistent at root-n rate so it perturbs the later estimation only at second order. In a second stage the transformed values, now bounded in the open unit interval, are modeled by a two-parameter distribution on that interval whose variance is a function of its mean, with one submodel linking the mean to covariates through a link function and a separate submodel linking the precision parameter to covariates. The coefficients of both submodels are estimated by maximizing the corresponding log-likelihood, which has no closed form and is solved by iterative numerical optimization with log-transformed parameters to enforce positivity. Predictions and effects are mapped back to the original outcome scale by applying an empirical quantile function to the fitted mean, so coefficients are interpreted as shifts in percentile position and marginal effects vary across observations. Uncertainty is obtained by a nonparametric bootstrap that resamples whole records, recomputes the empirical transform, refits both submodels, and forms prediction intervals on the unit interval that are mapped back to the original scale. Performance is assessed by a Monte-Carlo study scoring distributional discrepancy, interval calibration, and point error against several classical regression baselines, and on one real tabular dataset.
DOMAIN: applied regression with heteroscedastic non-normal errors
STRUCTURE: numerical optimization
DATA_OBJECT: set or table
INFERENCE: maximum likelihood
PROBLEM_FORM: estimation
DISTRIBUTION: continuous; other: beta
COMPLEXITY: convergence rate
