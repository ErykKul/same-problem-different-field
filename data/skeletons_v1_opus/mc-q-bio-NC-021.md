MECHANISM: Many self-propelled agents move on a two-dimensional domain with positions and headings governed by stochastic differential equations, where local speed is modulated by a spatially varying slowness field and heading is driven by a control input plus angular noise. Agents deposit into a shared scalar field that evolves by a reaction-diffusion equation coupling deposition, diffusion, and decay to the agent density, so the field acts as a slowly varying memory sensed through its gradient. A trail-following control aligning heading with the log-gradient of the field is derived as gradient descent of a divergence between agent density and field concentration. An additional steering law is determined by posing a stochastic optimal-control problem: minimize the expectation of a path functional combining accumulated traversal time, a quadratic control-effort penalty, and a terminal constraint, subject to the agent and field dynamics. After reparameterizing trajectories by arc length, the augmented objective is extremized, yielding backward co-state (adjoint) equations whose terminal conditions encode the endpoint constraint and whose solution gives a feedback control proportional to the heading-sensitivity co-state. A separation of fast trajectory traversal and slow field adaptation justifies treating the field as quasi-static within each forward-backward solve. Forward simulation generates candidate paths, backward integration computes sensitivities, the feedback law updates steering, and the field is updated, iterating to convergence. A Lyapunov functional for the coupled slow-fast system certifies monotone decrease of the expected cost and convergence to time-minimizing geodesics of the slowness metric. Connections to eikonal and Hamilton-Jacobi value-function descriptions and to dynamic optimal transport are established analytically and checked by simulation.
