MECHANISM: Start from a symmetric pairwise-association matrix computed between many simultaneously recorded scalar signals. Sweep a scalar threshold from high to low; at each level binarize the matrix by keeping only entries above the threshold, yielding a graph whose edges appear as the threshold drops, a connectivity-percolation probe. At each threshold compute connected-component statistics by component-labeling traversal: the size of the largest component, the second-largest (a susceptibility-like quantity), the number of components, and graph-summary observables such as a Laplacian-spectrum quantity, the degree-distribution spread, and the skewness of the component-size distribution. Track each observable as a function of the threshold; near the percolation point the second-largest-component and variability measures peak and the component-size distribution becomes broad/heavy-tailed, while above it a giant component dominates. Locate a characteristic threshold where these independent signatures converge, and use it as a single intrinsic descriptor replacing arbitrary threshold choice. Validate by rescaling curves to collapse across instances, by relating the descriptor to analytic percolation-density approximations (reciprocal of the leading eigenvalue; a mean-degree ratio), and by comparing against an independent temporal-autocorrelation indicator, for which a closed-form linear-recurrence derivation links the association matrix to the lag-one autocorrelation. Further validate on simulated excitable-network and lattice spin dynamics swept through a continuous transition, confirming the descriptor peaks at the critical control value.
