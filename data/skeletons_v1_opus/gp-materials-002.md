MECHANISM: A scalar target depends on a vector of inputs through an unknown latent function endowed with a prior over functions specified by a covariance kernel. Given observed input-output pairs, the posterior predictive mean and variance at a new input are obtained by conditioning a joint Gaussian, requiring inversion of a regularized kernel matrix. A more expressive variant composes several such latent function layers, so the output of one layer warps the input to the next, and because the resulting marginal is intractable it is fitted by maximizing a variational lower bound using inducing points that summarize each layer. The fitted surrogate predicts several correlated outputs jointly with calibrated uncertainty. An iterative loop then repeatedly fits the surrogate and selects which inputs to evaluate next by maximizing an acquisition score. One acquisition adds a confidence-weighted multiple of the predictive standard deviation; another scores a batch by the expected increase in the volume of the region dominated by the predicted outcomes relative to a reference. Evaluation costs differ across information sources, so candidates are drawn in proportion to a cost-weighting and the acquisition is optimized over a discrete candidate set by a stochastic search. The procedure alternates cheap single-output queries with expensive batched multi-output queries, accumulating evaluations until a budget is spent. A scalar shape descriptor counting how many non-dominated points lie on the convex hull characterizes the geometry of the trade-off surface.
