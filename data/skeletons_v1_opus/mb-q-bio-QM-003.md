MECHANISM: A heterogeneous directed multigraph of typed entities connected by typed relations is assembled, and the task is to score whether an unobserved typed edge between two entities is true. Two model families are compared. The first learns low-dimensional vectors for every entity and relation and scores a candidate triple by a relation-specific geometric function, for example projecting head and tail vectors through a per-relation matrix and measuring a translated distance. The second initializes node features and repeatedly aggregates information from each node's neighbors across relation types over several layers, concatenating a node's own state with a pooled summary of neighbors and applying a learned linear map and nonlinearity, then scores an edge by feeding the two endpoint embeddings through a small predictor. Training uses a discriminative objective contrasting observed edges against corrupted or mined negative edges, either a margin-based ranking loss or a binary classification loss, with negatives filtered to exclude real edges and resampled each step. Evaluation enforces a temporal split so test edges postdate training edges, uses verified hard negatives, and reports area under the precision-recall curve and the fraction of true edges ranked within the top positions. Controlled ablations remove or swap node feature modalities to isolate the contribution of explicit object structure versus pure topological connectivity. Scaling experiments vary parameter count and training-data fraction to separate the effect of capacity from data volume. Top novel predicted edges are checked against external ground truth. The central finding compares topological signal against structural features.
