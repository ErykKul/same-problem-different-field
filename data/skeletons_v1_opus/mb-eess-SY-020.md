MECHANISM: A system of interacting units is represented as a weighted graph, encoded by a symmetric, positive semi-definite operator whose off-diagonal entries are negative pairwise coupling coefficients and whose row sums vanish. A diagonal weighting operator is formed from per-unit scalar attributes computed in closed form from local parameters and the system operating point. A generalized eigenvalue problem of the matrix pencil formed by the coupling operator and the diagonal weighting operator is solved, and the eigenvector associated with the smallest nonzero eigenvalue is extracted and normalized by its largest absolute entry. This eigenvector assigns each unit a scalar score interpreted as its local resistance to propagated perturbations, generalizing the ordinary second-smallest-eigenvalue eigenvector by embedding the per-unit weights. A closed-form spectral expansion expresses each unit's transient response to a localized impulse as a weighted sum over eigenpairs of damped oscillatory modes. Separately, a stochastic forcing term is modeled by a mean-reverting linear stochastic differential equation driven by white noise. Repeated independent realizations of the forcing are simulated forward in time over a fixed horizon with a fixed step, producing per-unit and aggregate response trajectories. Summary statistics, including dispersion of an aggregate weighted-average quantity and an accumulated absolute-deviation functional, are collected across realizations and compared against the spectral score to validate the ranking.
