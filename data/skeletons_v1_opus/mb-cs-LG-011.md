MECHANISM: The goal is to maximize an expensive, noisy, time-varying black-box objective defined over a large structured discrete input space, by querying it sequentially under a fixed budget. Structured inputs are mapped into a continuous low-dimensional latent space by a learned generative decoder whose latent code is an additive sum of covariate-dependent functions, each given a smoothness-inducing prior approximated by a finite linear basis expansion with random coefficients; one of the covariates is the iteration index, making the embedding explicitly time-indexed. The decoder and its prior hyperparameters are fit by maximizing an evidence lower bound with a factorized approximate posterior, optimized by stochastic mini-batch gradient steps. A probabilistic surrogate is placed over the latent-to-value map using a covariance function that factorizes into a latent-space kernel times a time kernel, trained variationally on accumulated (latent, time, value) triples, so the predictive mean and uncertainty depend on the query time. At each round an acquisition rule trades off predicted value against predictive uncertainty, restricted to an adaptively sized local region around the current best point; candidate latent points are scored by drawing posterior function samples, the chosen points are decoded back to structured candidates and evaluated. The generative model is periodically refit and embeddings recomputed to keep latent geometry aligned, with auxiliary regularizers and an inversion step correcting decoder-induced mismatch. Performance is tracked by re-scoring all past candidates under the current objective and accumulating the gap to the per-round optimum.
