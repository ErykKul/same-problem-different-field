MECHANISM: A configuration assigns one of a few discrete states to each node of a regular grid, and an energy is defined as a sum of pairwise terms over neighboring nodes plus single-node terms, weighted by several coupling constants. The central object is the configuration-sum (partition function) over all assignments, with each configuration weighted by an exponential of its negated energy over temperature; thermodynamic quantities follow as derivatives or moments of this sum. Three complementary computations are carried out. A variational approximation replaces the full energy by a tractable single-node trial energy with free parameters, bounds the free energy, and minimizes it, yielding coupled self-consistent fixed-point equations for the mean state and its square that are solved by a Newton iteration. A low-temperature expansion starts from the ground configuration and enumerates, by combinatorial counting, the number and weight of configurations obtained by flipping increasing numbers of nodes, producing the partition function as a power series in the exponential variables, from which order parameter and response series are extracted. A stochastic simulation repeatedly proposes single-node state changes and accepts them with probability one if the energy decreases and otherwise with the exponential of the negated energy change, sampling equilibrium configurations to estimate averages, a normalized fourth-to-second moment crossing statistic, and finite-size scaling exponents. An exact algebraic change of variables maps one special case onto a simpler two-state model.
