MECHANISM: Two approximations of an unknown response are available, a cheap abundant one and an expensive scarce one, and the aim is to fuse them to locate the minimizer of an objective built from the response while quantifying how uncertain that minimizer is. The expensive response is modeled as a scaled version of the cheap response plus an additive discrepancy term, with additive observation noise. Both the cheap response and the discrepancy are given process priors whose finite collections are jointly multivariate-normal with parameterized smooth kernels, which induces a joint normal over all stacked observations with a structured block covariance depending on the scale factor. Estimation proceeds modularly: the cheap process hyperparameters are fit by maximizing a marginal likelihood, then for each candidate scale factor the residual between scarce observations and scaled cheap predictions is treated as discrepancy plus noise and its hyperparameters are fit by likelihood, and the scale factor is chosen by maximizing a penalized marginal likelihood. Uncertainty in the scale factor is approximated by repeatedly refitting it while holding out one scarce observation at a time, yielding an empirical distribution. Prediction at new inputs uses the conditional-normal formulas to give a posterior mean and covariance. For decision-making, many scale-factor draws and many predictive surface draws are generated, the objective minimizer over a space-filling candidate set is recorded for each draw, and the resulting collection of minimizers forms an empirical distribution whose center gives the recommended setting and whose spread quantifies decision uncertainty.
DOMAIN: manufacturing process optimization, computer experiments
STRUCTURE: dense linear algebra
