MECHANISM: The work bounds a worst-case expected-loss functional taken as a supremum over all distributions within a transport-cost ball of an empirical distribution. The central construction reduces the problem from the multivariate loss to univariate growth-rate functions, where the rate at a data point is the largest loss increase achievable within a perturbation radius. For each such rate the least concave majorant and the least star-shaped majorant are formed, and the robust-minus-empirical gap is sandwiched between an average of star-shaped majorants and a concave majorant of the maximal rate; the proof builds an explicit perturbation for the lower bound and uses concavity with Jensen's inequality for the upper bound. Corollaries establish monotonicity of the bounds in the cost exponent and a dichotomy in which the certificate is either finite for all radii or infinite for all radii. The same majorant machinery bounds a complexity functional, the expected supremum over a hypothesis class of noise-correlated averages, showing the adversarial-minus-empirical complexity gap is controlled by the complexity of the rate class. A tractable surrogate score with compositional and product calculus rules is introduced for layered predictors, and the classical Lipschitz bound is recovered as a special case.
