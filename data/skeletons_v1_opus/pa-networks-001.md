MECHANISM: A growing relational structure is generated by a stochastic rule in which the number of entities is held fixed while links are added one at a time, each new link attaching to an entity with probability proportional to a power of that entity's current connection count. The quantity of interest is the limiting distribution of per-entity connection counts. A master recurrence is written for an auxiliary quantity indexed by connection count and time, expressing how the count distribution updates as one more link is added. For the linear case the recurrence is simplified using a large-population approximation of a harmonic sum via the digamma function, then solved exactly by introducing a generating function in the time variable, whose closed form is expanded to read off the coefficient and hence a closed-form count distribution. Taking the large-time, large-population limit yields an explicit distribution whose large-count behavior is extracted asymptotically and shown to decay exponentially rather than as a power law. For the nonlinear case the two extreme exponents are solved in closed form by the same generating-function method, the general exponent is bounded using a product inequality, and the leading large-count behavior is again shown to be exponential. The conclusion is a characterization separating exponential-tailed from power-law-tailed regimes.
