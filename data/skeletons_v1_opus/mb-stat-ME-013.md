MECHANISM: Given a sample of angular observations on a periodic domain drawn from an unknown periodic density, the goal is to estimate that density and characterize the rate at which the estimator's error shrinks with sample size. The estimator averages a periodic smoothing weight function, indexed by a concentration parameter, centered at each observation. Both the density and the smoothing weight are represented through their Fourier coefficients, so that the estimator's expected squared error decomposes exactly into a bias term, a weighted sum over integer frequencies of squared density coefficients times the squared deviation of the weight's transfer coefficient from one, and a variance term scaling inversely with sample size. The key construction chooses the weight's transfer coefficients to equal one on a low band of frequencies, taper through a transition band, and vanish beyond a cutoff, which annihilates the bias contribution of all retained frequencies. Closed-form expressions for the weight functions follow by summing the truncated coefficient series. Under three nested smoothness assumptions, namely polynomial coefficient decay, exponential coefficient decay, and finite-support coefficients, upper bounds on the bias are derived and balanced against the variance bound by optimizing the cutoff, yielding explicit error-rate orders including a parametric root-n rate in the finite-support case. Data-driven cutoff selectors are defined by leave-one-out squared-error and by an empirical-coefficient threshold rule. Finite-sample behavior is checked by repeated simulation across canonical periodic distributions.
