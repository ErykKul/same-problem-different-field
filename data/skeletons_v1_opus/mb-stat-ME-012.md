MECHANISM: A large collection of entities each yields a summary statistic obtained by comparing two groups of measurements, and these statistics are transformed through a probability-integral map into standardized scores so that uninteresting entities follow a known reference law. The marginal density of the scores is modeled as a two-component mixture: a fixed known null component weighted by an unknown proportion, plus an alternative component for the interesting entities. The alternative component is represented semiparametrically as a continuous mixture over a scale parameter of a skewed unimodal kernel, where the kernel has finite-dimensional location and shape parameters but the scale-mixing distribution is left fully unspecified and infinite-dimensional. Identifiability of the mixing proportion, the finite parameters, and the mixing distribution is established under linear-independence and support conditions, and consistency of the maximum-likelihood estimator is proven. Estimation maximizes the log-likelihood by an iterative expectation/conditional-maximization scheme: an expectation step computes each entity's posterior probability of belonging to the null component; one conditional step updates the infinite-dimensional mixing distribution by a directional-derivative search over candidate support points subject to optimality conditions; another conditional step updates the proportion in closed form and the finite parameters by gradient-based optimization. Iteration continues until the likelihood converges. Posterior null probabilities are then used to rank entities and to estimate the proportions of false positives and false negatives at any threshold.
