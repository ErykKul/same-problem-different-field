MECHANISM: Each of N entities is assigned a positive latent strength, and the probability that one entity beats another in a pairwise contest is its strength divided by the sum of the two strengths. The observed data are counts of wins in repeated pairwise contests, giving a product-of-binomials likelihood over all pairs as a function of the strength vector. Reparameterizing strengths to their logarithms makes the win probability a logistic function of the difference of two latent values. A zero-mean Gaussian prior with a common variance hyperparameter is placed on the log-strengths, chosen by appeal to invariance desiderata and a maximum-entropy argument. A hierarchical layer places a prior on the variance hyperparameter; its hyperparameters are set from an approximate maximum-a-posteriori expansion of the log-posterior, using the curvature (second-derivative/Hessian) of the log-likelihood to obtain a point estimate and its variance, then matched to a gamma distribution. The full posterior over log-strengths is sampled by gradient-based Markov-chain Monte-Carlo. Point estimates for ranking are posterior means of the log-strengths, which shrink toward zero relative to the maximum-likelihood estimates. Prediction uses the posterior predictive distribution, integrating the contest likelihood over the posterior, with mean predicted counts compared to held-out counts via absolute-error metrics. The Bayesian estimates are benchmarked against maximum-likelihood estimates, which are obtained by iteratively solving fixed-point equations.
