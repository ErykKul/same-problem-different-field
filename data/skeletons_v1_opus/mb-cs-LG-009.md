MECHANISM: A learner faces a sequence of related decision problems, each defined over the same state and action spaces but with differing transition and reward structure, and must produce a single mapping from states to actions that performs well across all of them. Two coupled function approximators are maintained: a fast one specialized to the current problem and a consolidated one accumulating knowledge. A distance between problems is defined via a divergence between their optimal value functions or optimal action distributions. A forgetting quantity is defined as a state-occupancy-weighted divergence between the action distributions (or value functions) before and after training on a new problem, with weights taken from the earlier occupancy. Consolidation is posed as minimizing the cumulative weighted divergence between the consolidated mapping and each past mapping; this is shown to reduce to a weighted maximum-likelihood fit (equivalently a weighted average) over a stored sample of state-action pairs, expressible as an incremental update. When action distributions are assumed parametric, a closed-form incremental update arises under a transport-based divergence. Before each new problem, candidate initializations are evaluated by estimating their expected return over a short interaction window, and a composite one-versus-rest hypothesis test (with a follow-up two-sample test) selects which initialization to adopt. The fast learner is then trained, optionally with a divergence-regularization term toward the consolidated mapping. Performance is summarized by averaged return, an area-under-curve transfer measure, and a drop-in-performance forgetting measure.
DOMAIN: continual reinforcement learning, sequential decision tasks
STRUCTURE: neural network
DATA_OBJECT: sequence or time-series
INFERENCE: maximum likelihood
PROBLEM_FORM: control
DISTRIBUTION: continuous; nonparametric
COMPLEXITY: not stated
