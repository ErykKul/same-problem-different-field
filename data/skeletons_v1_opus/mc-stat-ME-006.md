MECHANISM: A scalar sequence indexed by equally spaced points is passed through an undecimated multiscale linear-filtering transform that splits it into detail and smooth components at successive scales, yielding, at a fixed scale, a derived coefficient sequence. At that scale the coefficient sequence is written as a two-parameter harmonic regression on a cosine and a sine evaluated at a chosen frequency, so the two unknowns are the in-phase and quadrature amplitudes. Rather than least squares, the amplitudes are estimated by minimizing the sum of absolute residuals, an absolute-deviation criterion that is resistant to heavy-tailed and outlier-contaminated noise. A spectral ordinate is defined as a fixed constant times the squared norm of the estimated amplitude pair. To obtain its sampling behavior, the estimator is re-centered and rescaled by the square root of the sample size, and the rescaled absolute-deviation objective is treated as a convex random process whose unique minimizer is studied; an exact decomposition of the absolute-value increment separates a leading term, a normalized sum of regressor-times-sign-of-residual contributions, from a remainder whose expectation gives a deterministic quadratic and whose fluctuation vanishes. Under stationarity, zero median, a residual density positive at the origin, and summable autocovariances of the residual signs, a central limit theorem for weakly dependent sequences makes the leading term Gaussian, so the rescaled amplitude estimator is asymptotically Gaussian with a sandwich covariance built from the residual-density value and the long-run covariance of the residual signs. Consequently the spectral ordinate converges in law to a quadratic form in a bivariate Gaussian, equivalently a weighted sum of two independent one-degree-of-freedom chi-squared variables given by the covariance eigenvalues.
