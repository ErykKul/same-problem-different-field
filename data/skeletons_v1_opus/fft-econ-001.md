MECHANISM: One observes a single ordered sequence of real-valued quantities indexed by equally spaced steps. The sequence is mapped from its original index domain into a conjugate frequency domain by a linear transform that decomposes it into oscillatory components at discrete frequencies. The squared magnitudes of these components yield a profile describing how the total variability of the sequence is distributed across frequencies. A baseline reference model assumes the components are mutually uncorrelated with constant variance, and under this baseline the distribution of each transformed coefficient is derived in closed form, showing it to be approximately of one parametric family with a frequency-dependent variance. A cumulative quantity is then formed by partially summing the contributions across frequencies and rescaling, producing a statistic whose deviation from zero measures departure from the baseline. The sampling distribution of this cumulative statistic is characterized, and pointwise confidence bands are constructed around the baseline expectation. For each observed sequence the cumulative statistic is computed and compared against the bands to classify the qualitative shape of departure: whether low-frequency or high-frequency contributions dominate, or whether the pattern is mixed. Monotonicity and sign properties of the statistic and its derivatives are used to define and distinguish these regimes. The procedure is applied independently to several sequences and the resulting classifications are compared.
