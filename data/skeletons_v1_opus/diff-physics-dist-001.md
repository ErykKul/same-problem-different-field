MECHANISM: A many-body system is mapped onto an auxiliary set of independent single-particle states sharing the same density, reducing the problem to a self-consistent eigenvalue problem. For each particle one solves a linear differential eigenvalue equation whose operator is a sum of a kinetic term and an effective potential. The effective potential depends on the density, which itself depends on the occupied eigenfunctions, so the equations are coupled and solved iteratively until self-consistency. One unknown component of the potential is unavailable in closed form and is replaced by several approximate functionals of the density, its gradient, and related local quantities; one such approximation mixes in a fraction of a nonlocal exchange term split by interaction range. The discretized operator is represented in a finite basis and integrated over a periodic cell on a sampled reciprocal-space grid. Geometric parameters of the periodic cell are relaxed by minimizing total energy and driving computed forces and a stress tensor below a tolerance. Resulting eigenvalues are assembled into dispersion curves and an aggregated density-of-states, and a characteristic spectral gap is extracted. A more expensive correction that updates the single-particle propagator self-consistently against a screened interaction provides a higher-accuracy reference. The competing approximations are benchmarked against this reference and against measured structural quantities. A small perturbative relativistic correction is added non-self-consistently to assess level splittings.
