MECHANISM: The goal is to estimate the mean of an outcome in a target population using individual records from a source population together with only aggregate summaries (moments of selected covariate functions) from the target. Assuming the conditional outcome-given-covariate law is shared while only the covariate marginals differ, the target mean equals the source expectation of the outcome reweighted by an unknown density ratio. First approach: solve a convex program that finds per-record weights closest to uniform (minimizing a sum of weight-times-log-weight entropy discrepancy) subject to the constraint that reweighted source covariate moments equal the target summaries; by Lagrangian duality the weights take a normalized exponential-tilt form indexed by multipliers found from a smooth concave dual. The reweighted outcome average is the estimator. Second approach: posit a flexible parametric form for the density ratio and estimate its parameters by minimizing the same entropy discrepancy augmented by a quadratic penalty that treats the noisy target summaries as a random vector with an estimated covariance, subject to moment-matching constraints; this becomes a saddle-point problem in the tilt multipliers and the ratio parameters, solved with a plug-in covariance from an initial fit. Asymptotic normality of both estimators is derived with explicit sandwich-type variances that propagate the uncertainty of the aggregate summaries, and consistent variance estimators are given. A quadratic-form statistic in the residual moment conditions is shown to be asymptotically chi-squared and used to test correct specification of the ratio model. The method reduces to the entropy-balancing special case when the log ratio is linear in the chosen covariate functions.
