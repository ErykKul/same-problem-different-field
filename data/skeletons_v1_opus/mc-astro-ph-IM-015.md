MECHANISM: A library of high-dimensional output vectors is precomputed by an expensive forward simulator over a multidimensional input-parameter space sampled with a space-filling design. Each output vector is centered by subtracting the mean, and the sample covariance matrix of the centered library is eigen-decomposed; retaining the leading eigenvectors by largest eigenvalue gives a low-dimensional linear projection that captures most of the variance, and each output is encoded as a short coefficient vector. For each retained coordinate a smooth probabilistic regressor maps the input-parameter vector to that coefficient, placing a distribution over candidate functions with a stationary similarity kernel whose per-dimension length scales and amplitude are fit by maximizing a marginal likelihood; each coordinate is modeled independently because the projection basis is orthogonal. Predicting at a new input yields coefficients with predictive variances, which are linearly recombined with the basis vectors to reconstruct an approximate output and propagate a per-component variance. Given an observed vector, a posterior over input parameters is formed from a Gaussian-error likelihood, summing squared residuals weighted by a total variance that combines observational noise, surrogate predictive variance, and truncation error, times a flat prior. Because the posterior is intractable, a Markov chain with a proposal-and-accept rule generates samples whose stationary distribution is the posterior, discarding an initial burn-in. Calibration is checked with recovery experiments on held-out simulations: the cumulative-distribution value of each true input under its marginal posterior is computed, transformed to normal scores, and tested for uniformity, and credible-interval coverage fractions are tabulated. Bias, error magnitude, and coverage jointly assess accuracy and uncertainty reliability.
