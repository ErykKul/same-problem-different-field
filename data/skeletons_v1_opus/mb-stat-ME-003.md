MECHANISM: Given a linear model relating a response vector to a coefficient vector through a design matrix under additive noise, the goal is to select the nonzero coordinates (individually or in predefined groups) while bounding the rate of false selections in finite samples. The estimator minimizes a convex objective: a quadratic data-fidelity term plus a sorted weighted penalty that applies a non-increasing sequence of weights to the magnitude-ordered coefficients (a generalization of an absolute-value penalty). The key contribution is the construction of the penalty weight sequence so that two stricter error notions are controlled: the probability of at least k false selections, and the probability that the false-selection proportion exceeds a threshold. Under an orthogonal design the problem decouples coordinatewise, reducing selection to an ordered multiple-testing problem; closed-form weight sequences are derived from inverse cumulative distribution functions of the noise so that a stepwise ordered-threshold rule provably bounds each error notion. The control theorems are proved by decomposing the error event over the realized number of selections, bounding each term using order-statistic and convexity (top-k sum as a minimization) arguments, and summing. The construction is extended to the grouped setting using a chi-type quantile and group-norm penalty, controlling the analogous group-level errors. For general non-orthogonal designs an approximate weight sequence is calibrated from a Gaussian approximation to the estimator's residual distribution with a sampling-based correction, preserving convexity. The optimization is solved by a proximal first-order iterative scheme. Empirical recovery and error-control behavior are evaluated across sparsity, correlation, and grouping regimes.
