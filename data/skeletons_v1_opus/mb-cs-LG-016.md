MECHANISM: The object of study is approximating an unknown mapping from inputs to outputs, where outputs live in an infinite-dimensional inner-product space, by minimizing expected squared error over a draw of paired samples. The exact predictor is built in a function space generated by a symmetric positive kernel that returns an operator rather than a scalar; this kernel is assumed to have an integral representation as an expectation of an outer product of random feature maps over a probability measure. The exact kernel is replaced by a finite average of such outer products evaluated at a finite number of independent draws, turning the infinite representation into an explicit finite-dimensional feature embedding and reducing storage and arithmetic cost from cubic to roughly linear in sample count. Estimators are obtained by applying a spectral filter function to the empirical second-moment operator of the features and applying the result to the empirical cross-moment with the targets, a family that unifies penalized inversion and iterative descent under one parameterization. The analysis decomposes the excess error into an approximation gap from the finite feature count and a generalization gap of the filtered estimator. Two structural assumptions control the rates: a source condition writing the target as a fractional power of the kernel integral operator applied to a bounded element, and an effective-dimension bound on the trace of the regularized operator that encodes eigenvalue decay. Under these, choosing the regularization level and the feature count as explicit powers of the sample size yields high-probability bounds matching the optimal achievable rate. The number of features needed scales with sample size in a regime-dependent power, and the rates are shown independent of input dimension but scaling with the squared feature width.
