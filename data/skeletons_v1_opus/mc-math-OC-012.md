MECHANISM: A scalar ratio objective, the logarithm of a determinant of an identity-plus-quadratic-form expression divided by an affine power term, is maximized over a positive-semidefinite matrix variable and two diagonal complex matrices, subject to a trace budget and quadratic global-reflection inequalities. The composite linear map relating input to output is a product of fixed matrices interleaved with the two diagonal variables. The diagonal-matrix subproblem is recast by substituting intermediate vector variables, and its optimum is shown in closed form via a Cauchy-Schwarz alignment argument and the dominant eigenpair of a Gram matrix, after which the diagonal entries are recovered by elementwise division using a pseudo-inverse. The remaining scalar power variable is optimized as a ratio of a concave function to an affine function, which is pseudo-concave with a unique stationary point clipped to its feasible bound. For the general higher-rank case the matrix variable cannot be decoupled, so an alternating scheme iterates between the closed-form diagonal updates and a matrix update. The non-concave matrix update is handled by minorizing each squared-norm term with its first-order expansion, turning the step into a concave fractional program solved within a sequential surrogate framework that monotonically increases the objective and converges to a first-order stationary point. Special low-dimensional cases admit fully closed-form solutions. Numerical experiments report the objective and the achievable rate. The dominant computation is constrained fractional optimization with eigen-based closed-form inner solutions and surrogate iterations.
