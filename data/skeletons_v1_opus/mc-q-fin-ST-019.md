MECHANISM: The method trains a parametric generator that maps a vector of standard-normal latent noise to a synthetic sequence, jointly with a critic network that scores real versus synthetic sequences, using an adversarial objective in which the critic maximizes the difference of its expected scores on real and generated samples subject to a gradient-magnitude penalty, while the generator minimizes the critic gap. The novelty is augmenting the generator objective with several differentiable penalty terms that align summary statistics of the generated sequences with those of the real sequences. One term matches a fitted exceedance-tail shape parameter estimated separately on the upper and lower tails beyond a high quantile. A second term matches, by mean squared error over lags, the autocorrelation of the squared sequence values. A third term matches the cross-correlation between a value and the following local dispersion, enforcing the observed negative sign. A fourth term matches, in matrix-norm distance, the correlation matrices of dispersion vectors computed over several rolling window lengths, capturing across-scale dependence. The total generator loss is the adversarial loss plus weighted sums of these alignment penalties, treating the structural statistics as first-class targets rather than diagnostics. Optimization uses a stochastic gradient method with several critic updates per generator update and gradual annealing of the structural penalties early in training. The trained generator then samples many sequences, which are evaluated by recomputing the structural statistics as gap metrics and by running a simple threshold-based decision rule over the generated paths.
