MECHANISM: A multichannel ordered signal is first re-referenced by subtracting paired channels to emphasize local differences, then a fixed-length window is extracted and each channel is mapped into a joint time-frequency representation by a windowed sliding transform, yielding a three-axis array of magnitudes. This array is reshaped into two complementary orderings, one emphasizing the time axis and one the frequency axis, and each ordering is linearly projected into a latent space and processed by stacked self-attention layers with positional encodings, which compute weighted combinations of all positions using scaled dot-product similarity. Each stream is pooled into a fixed vector, and the two vectors are fused with sample-specific weights produced by a small learned softmax gate. In parallel, a paired textual token sequence is encoded by a pretrained sequence encoder into another fixed vector. A symmetric similarity-based contrastive objective pulls matched signal and text vectors together and pushes mismatched ones apart within a batch. An autoregressive decoder reconstructs the text token by token conditioned on the fused signal vector, and a separate head maps the fused vector through a softmax to predict one of several discrete categories. All objectives, a categorical loss, the contrastive loss, and the reconstruction loss, are combined with learnable positive weights and minimized jointly by stochastic gradient updates. Components are removed one at a time to measure their effect on accuracy and on cross-modal retrieval.
