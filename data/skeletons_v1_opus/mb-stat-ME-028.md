MECHANISM: Each observation is a bounded integer count out of a known total, exhibiting an excess of zeros and variance beyond the binomial. A two-part model factorizes the likelihood: a binary participation stage governs whether the count is zero versus positive, and a zero-truncated overdispersed bounded-count kernel governs the positive magnitude, parameterized by a mean and a concentration controlling overdispersion. Both stage parameters are linked to covariates through logit linear predictors with population-level fixed coefficients plus group-specific deviations. The stacked group deviations across both stages are given a joint multivariate normal prior with a full block covariance, whose off-diagonal block encodes cross-stage dependence; this off-diagonal block receives no information from the conditional likelihood (the per-group information is block-diagonal across stages) and is identified only through the hierarchical prior acting on the finite set of groups, with a correlation prior regularizing the high-dimensional covariance. Group deviations may be further decomposed into a group-covariate-explained part and a residual. Inference proceeds by drawing from the posterior with a gradient-based sampler under a non-centered reparameterization using a Cholesky factor. To account for unequal inclusion weights, each observation's log-density is raised to a normalized weight, yielding a weighted pseudo-posterior; the resulting variance is then corrected by a sandwich estimator combining the observed weighted Hessian with a cluster-robust outer product of scores, and posterior draws are affinely rescaled by Cholesky factors so their covariance matches the design-consistent target. Closed-form marginal-effect decompositions translate coefficients into additive log-scale access and intensity components.
