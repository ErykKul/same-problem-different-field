MECHANISM: A latent state vector evolving under a known nonlinear transition map with additive noise is estimated recursively from a stream of noisy nonlinear measurements. The state is augmented with unknown time-varying parameters modeled as random-walk components so they are estimated jointly with the dynamic state. At each step a Gaussian approximation of the conditional state distribution is propagated via two stages. In the prediction stage a small deterministic set of weighted sample points is generated from the current mean and a factored covariance, pushed through the transition map, and recombined to form the predicted mean and predicted covariance factor. In the update stage sample points are regenerated, passed through the measurement map, and used to form the predicted measurement, the innovation covariance factor, and the cross-covariance, from which a gain matrix is computed. The corrected mean is the predicted mean plus the gain times the measurement residual, and the corrected covariance factor is updated by triangularization to preserve positive definiteness. The required moment integrals against the Gaussian are approximated by a fixed symmetric cubature rule whose nodes lie on a sphere. To handle a non-random-walk discrete latency parameter, a bank of parallel estimators each assuming a different latency is run, and a recursively updated posterior weight over hypotheses (from the residual likelihood) blends their latency estimates. The procedure repeats over time as new measurements arrive.
