MECHANISM: A small additional propagation time along a path through a weak position-dependent potential is derived in closed form by treating the medium as having an effective index that deviates from unity in proportion to the local potential, then integrating that index along the geometric path to get total transit time. Taking the difference of this integral between two paths at slightly different potential levels yields a differential delay that scales linearly with the path length and with the potential gradient, expressed analytically through a single dimensionless coupling parameter to be measured. The tiny delay is mapped to a measurable oscillatory observable by converting it into a phase and reading out an intensity through a sinusoidal transfer function biased at its maximum-slope point, so the observable is linear in the delay. A signal-to-noise budget is then computed analytically: each dominant fluctuation source is written as a closed-form expression in terms of bandwidth, received power, and instrument constants, and converted into an equivalent delay-referred noise. The achievable precision on the coupling parameter follows by dividing the noise-equivalent delay by the predicted signal delay. A post-processing gain is estimated by an oversampling argument, where increasing the sampling rate relative to the signal bandwidth spreads quantization noise and improves the ratio by a logarithmic factor capped by the converter's bit-limited dynamic range. Common-mode rejection through a balanced reciprocal geometry and a sign-reversal differencing scheme is invoked to cancel systematic offsets and double the effective signal. Scaling laws (linear in path length, square-root in number of independent stages) are given to project further sensitivity. The computation is entirely deterministic analytic estimation with a noise-propagation calculation; no data are fit.
