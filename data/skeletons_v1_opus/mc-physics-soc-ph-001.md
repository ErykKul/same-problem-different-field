MECHANISM: A symmetric positive-semidefinite operator is formed from a network by subtracting its pairwise-connection matrix from its diagonal degree matrix, giving an operator with a zero eigenvalue along the uniform mode and otherwise nonnegative eigenvalues. A diffusion of a conserved scalar over the nodes evolves by the negative of this operator, so its solution is the action of the matrix exponential of the operator on the initial condition, relaxing to the uniform steady state. The remaining amount at a node at a given time is the corresponding diagonal entry of the matrix exponential, and integrating this entry over all time, after subtracting the steady-state level, is proven via the spectral decomposition to equal a weighted sum of inverse nonzero eigenvalues that coincides with the node's average pairwise resistance to the rest of the network minus a global constant. Summing over all nodes shows that the time-integrated trace of the exponential, minus the stationary mode, equals an aggregate resistance quantity scaled by network size, giving an exact dynamical identity for a structural connectivity metric. Differentiating the trace exposes a time-resolved spectral filter: at short times the initial slope equals the total connection count, at intermediate times only eigenvalues below the spectral mean contribute, and at long times the smallest nonzero eigenvalue dominates. A sensitivity analysis identifies a synergy regime in which raising the smallest nonzero eigenvalue accelerates decay across a whole band of low modes. These regimes motivate continuous, spectrum-guided modifications of the connection structure as an alternative to combinatorial search.
