MECHANISM: A set of automated raters and a set of reference raters each assign ordinal scores to the same collection of items along several distinct rating dimensions, and a separate continuous outcome is associated with each item. To assess agreement that is invariant to scale and to nonlinear monotone transformations, the method works with relative orderings rather than raw scores. For every pair of items, each rater's directional preference is encoded as a sign, and these signs are collected into an antisymmetric matrix per rater or outcome. The concordance between two such matrices is computed as their normalized inner product, a cosine-like ratio of an inner product to the product of norms, yielding a rank-concordance coefficient. The method also computes a bias-corrected squared distance-based dependence measure to detect any form of statistical dependence, including nonmonotone, between raters and outcomes. To attribute variability in disagreement, each item's signed residual from a simple linear fit of the continuous outcome on a rater's score is treated as an error term. This error is decomposed with a fully-crossed random-effects model whose factors are items, raters, prompts, and contexts, including their interactions. The proportion of total variance assigned to each factor and interaction quantifies how much of the misalignment is controllable versus systemic. Aggregations such as consensus and weighted voting are formed and re-evaluated under the same concordance and decomposition machinery.
