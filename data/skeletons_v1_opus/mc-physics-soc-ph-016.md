MECHANISM: A continuous-time Markov jump process is defined on a network whose connections come in two types: pairwise links and higher-order groups that each join three or more entities. Each entity carries a binary state and each group also carries its own binary state, and transitions occur at rates that depend on neighbors through both link types, with one channel driven by neighboring entities and the other channel driven by the contaminated state of shared groups. Taking expectations of the indicator states and applying a moment-closure that replaces expectations of products and of a saturating function by functions of expectations yields a deterministic system of ordinary differential equations for the per-entity and per-group activation probabilities. For symmetric (complete and regular) network families these reduce to a low-dimensional system in the overall fraction of active entities and active groups. The disease-free fixed point is analyzed by linearizing and forming the Jacobian, whose characteristic polynomial is factored using a block-determinant identity and a rank-one matrix-determinant lemma into quadratic factors. Sign conditions on the coefficients of these quadratics give a single scalar threshold, expressible as a sum of two additive contributions, that separates decay to the inactive state from persistence; local asymptotic stability of both the trivial and a unique interior fixed point is proven via trace and determinant signs. A complementary individual-level stochastic simulation, advancing each entity and group by exponential-rate Bernoulli updates over small time steps, is used to check the approximations and explore heterogeneous group-size and recovery-rate effects.
