MECHANISM: The object of study is an iterative estimator for models with hidden components, where each step maximizes an expected complete-data objective formed by averaging, under the current conditional distribution of the hidden component, the joint log-density, then re-maximizing over the parameter. The analysis allows the data-generating law to lie outside the model family, so the target is the set of parameters minimizing a divergence to the truth rather than a true parameter. It further accounts for non-identifiability arising from a group acting on the parameter, so the estimand is an equivalence class (orbit) and all error is measured by a supremum-over-a-function-class distance between induced distributions, transferred canonically to the quotient. The estimator is shown to descend to a well-defined map on the quotient via a local section that intersects each orbit once, yielding identifiable local coordinates. Linearizing the map at a fixed point, the spectral radius of the derivative is proved to be the sharp local linear contraction factor, giving geometric decay of the iterate error and, through a local regularity modulus of the parameter-to-distribution map, of the distributional error. For the finite-sample version, the empirical operator is treated as a uniform additive perturbation of the population operator on a contraction basin, yielding an exact bound that is the geometric term plus the perturbation amplified by one over one-minus-the-contraction-factor. The perturbation magnitude is controlled by high-probability suprema of an induced empirical process bounded via chaining and entropy functionals. Lower bounds and an impossibility result without complexity control are also established.
