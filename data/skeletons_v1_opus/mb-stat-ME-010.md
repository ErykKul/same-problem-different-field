MECHANISM: The problem is to estimate parameters of a conditional model for a response given covariates by combining a detailed sample of individual records with externally reported summary quantities. The summaries are encoded as values of parameters that satisfy population moment equations, which by iterated expectation translate into moment constraints on the conditional model evaluated at each individual record. Estimation maximizes the individual-level log-likelihood, in which the covariate distribution is left fully nonparametric and represented by point masses on observed values, subject to the constraint that a probability-weighted average of the moment functions vanishes. Introducing multipliers for these constraints and profiling out the point masses yields a concentrated objective: the log-likelihood minus a sum of logarithms of one plus the multiplier-weighted moment terms, with the multipliers determined by a stationarity equation. Uncertainty in the external summaries is incorporated by adding a quadratic penalty based on a normal approximation of the summary estimates with a working covariance. To handle differences between the two populations, a multiplicative tilting weight links the two joint densities, modeled in exponential-family form so that the shifted conditional expectations retain closed forms. Asymptotic analysis gives a sandwich-form limiting normal distribution from a partitioned information matrix. Because nested inner solving of the multiplier equation by repeated Newton steps and outer maximization are costly and unstable in high dimension, a one-step estimator is derived by matching the influence-function expansions of the constrained and unconstrained estimators, requiring a single evaluation of the augmented matrix and a correction term added to the unconstrained estimate.
