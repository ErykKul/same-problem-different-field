MECHANISM: A collection of interacting units on a graph each occupy one of a few discrete states and transition between them as a continuous-time Markov jump process, where one transition type fires at a rate proportional to the number of neighboring units in a particular state and another fires at a constant per-unit rate. The full joint distribution over all unit-state configurations obeys a linear master equation whose dimension grows exponentially with the number of units, making direct solution infeasible. Indicator functions selecting each unit's state are introduced, and products of indicators are averaged to define marginal probabilities of fixed order; differentiating the indicator products in time and taking expectations yields exact evolution equations for the marginals. These equations form an open hierarchy: the time derivative of an order-n marginal depends on order-(n+1) marginals because the coupling transition links pairs of adjacent units, paralleling a classical statistical-mechanics hierarchy. To close the system, higher-order marginals are approximated as products of lower-order ones under an independence (chaos) assumption, yielding first-order closures, an averaged closure over weak long-range links, and a higher-order pair closure that retains correlations between connected units. The reduced closed systems are integrated as ordinary differential equations. Independent stochastic realizations of the jump process are also simulated on a fine time grid with per-step transition probabilities, and ensemble plus node averages of state fractions are accumulated. The closures are benchmarked against exact solutions on small graphs and against the simulations on larger random networks, examining how network structure and intervention timing reshape the aggregate trajectories.
