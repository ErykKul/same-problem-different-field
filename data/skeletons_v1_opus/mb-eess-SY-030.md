MECHANISM: A feedback control system couples a model-based compliant mapping for one subsystem with a learned policy for another, plus a recursive state estimator. An external force-and-moment input is mapped to desired linear and angular velocities through an inverse stiffness-damping relation, then resolved into joint commands using a damped pseudo-inverse of a configuration-dependent Jacobian. A second subsystem's actuation is produced by a stochastic policy trained by reward maximization in simulation to track the desired velocities, with realistic state-estimate noise injected from the estimator's covariance. Latent velocity states are estimated by a recursive Gaussian filter: a prediction step advances a discretized rigid-body dynamics map and inflates a covariance, where a learned function predicts the model-versus-truth residual and its outer product supplies the process-noise covariance; an update step forms an innovation, a gain, and corrected mean and covariance from a linear measurement model. A smoothing low-pass recursion is applied to the estimate. Safety is enforced by precomputing a maximal admissible set of states and references that never violate constraints over a long horizon by gridding and forward-simulating trajectories; admissible points are stored in a spatial tree, and at runtime the current query is checked or projected to the nearest admissible reference by a minimum-distance search. The residual-prediction network is fit by minimizing mean squared error against measured residuals.
