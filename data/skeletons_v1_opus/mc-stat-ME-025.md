MECHANISM: A new parametric family of probability laws for a positive continuous quantity is constructed as a probabilistic mixture of the first three extreme order statistics of a base law, yielding a cumulative function that is a polynomial-in-logarithm transform of the base cumulative function with two mixing weights. Differentiating gives the density, and a specific base is substituted to obtain a concrete three-parameter law. Closed-form distributional properties are derived: the hazard function as a density-over-survival ratio, and the raw moments by a change of variable and term-by-term integration of a power-series expansion, proven to converge, from which dispersion, skewness, and kurtosis follow. The three parameters are then estimated by nine alternative criteria, including the likelihood and several discrepancy or spacing objectives comparing the model cumulative function to empirical order-statistic positions, each solved by a numerical optimizer. A simulation study repeatedly draws samples of varying size via acceptance-rejection from a proposal law and recomputes every estimator, summarizing bias, mean squared error, and mean relative error to rank the estimators. Finally the law is fit to observed time-to-event samples and compared against competing laws by several goodness-of-fit statistics and an information criterion.
