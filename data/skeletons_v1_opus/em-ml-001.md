MECHANISM: A finite collection of vector-valued observations is summarized by a small set of prototype vectors and a shared scalar spread parameter, under a generative model that treats each observation as arising from one of several equally weighted components. A surrogate objective lower-bounds the model log-likelihood and is made cheap in two ways: each observation is assigned only to a short list of its few nearest prototypes (truncating the per-observation responsibility distribution to hard zeros elsewhere), and the full collection is replaced by a small reweighted representative subset. The algorithm alternates two steps until convergence. In the first step, holding prototypes fixed, each observation's short candidate list is refreshed by searching only a restricted neighborhood and keeping the candidates with largest joint score, which provably increases the bound and reduces to pairwise distance comparisons. In the second step, holding the lists fixed, each prototype is updated as a responsibility-weighted, subset-weighted average of observations, and the shared spread is updated from weighted squared distances. The representative subset is built in a constant number of passes, prototypes are initialized by a randomized seeding chain, and an initial spread is estimated cheaply from nearest-candidate distances. Per-iteration cost scales with the subset size times the squared neighborhood size times dimension rather than the full product of counts. Accuracy versus speed is traded off by the candidate-list size and the subset size, recovering the exact objective in their limits.
DOMAIN: machine learning, large-scale clustering
STRUCTURE: N-body or all-pairs
DATA_OBJECT: point set
INFERENCE: maximum likelihood
PROBLEM_FORM: optimization
DISTRIBUTION: continuous; gaussian mixture
COMPLEXITY: polynomial iterative
