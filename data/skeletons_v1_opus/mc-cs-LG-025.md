MECHANISM: An entity is described by a symmetric positive semi-definite similarity matrix formed as the inner-product of a feature array, and a coupled linear map produces outputs from those features under a quadratic penalty. Eliminating the fast linear map by its argmin via an envelope/closed-form ridge solution, one derives an autonomous matrix differential equation for the similarity matrix whose right-hand side is a low-rank drive term (an outer product of residual and prediction) opposed by uniform decay. The drive is shown to have rank bounded by the small output dimension, while decay shrinks all directions. A scalar energy functional is identified as a monotone Lyapunov quantity, and analyticity plus coercivity give bounded trajectories converging to a unique stationary matrix. Setting the time derivative to zero yields an algebraic fixed-point equation, and a trace inequality argument forces the stationary matrix to share eigenvectors with the drive's target matrix (simultaneous diagonalization). Projecting onto the shared eigenbasis decouples the matrix equation into independent scalar balance equations per eigenmode. Solving each scalar equation with a non-negativity constraint gives a closed-form spectral law: each eigenvalue is a rectified function of the corresponding target eigenvalue minus a threshold, producing hard truncation of weak modes and square-root compression of strong ones. A parallel construction replaces the drive by a graph-incidence quadratic plus a log-determinant barrier, giving an inverse-spectrum rectified law over the graph operator's eigenvalues. The instantaneous stochastic-gradient covariance is also shown to be confined to a subspace whose rank scales with the output dimension, independent of size.
