MECHANISM: Given a collection of problem instances and a fixed finite set of candidate solver procedures, the goal is to learn a mapping that, for each instance, picks the procedure expected to perform best under a scalar performance metric. Each instance is first encoded as a relational graph capturing its internal control and data dependencies, and a learned message-passing network over this graph produces a fixed-length embedding vector. A small fully connected stack with a normalizing output layer maps the embedding to a categorical distribution over candidate procedures, trained by minimizing cross-entropy against the empirically best procedure per instance. Because labeled instances are scarce, the categorical output and the embedding are concatenated into a state that initializes a sequential decision agent. The agent maintains two networks, one producing an action distribution over procedures and one estimating a scalar value of the state, and receives a reward equal to the normalized gap between the chosen procedure's metric and the best attainable metric. Trajectories of state, action, reward, and stored log-probabilities are accumulated, advantages are computed by an exponentially discounted sum of temporal-difference residuals, and the policy is updated by a clipped surrogate objective combined with a value-fit term and an entropy regularizer. At inference the supervised distribution seeds the agent, which outputs the recommended procedure to run on the instance. Performance is reported as recommendation accuracy and as the downstream metric of the selected procedure.
