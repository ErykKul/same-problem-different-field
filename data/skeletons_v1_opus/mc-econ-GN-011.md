MECHANISM: Two interacting non-negative quantities are modeled by a coupled pair of first-order nonlinear ordinary differential equations, each combining a linear self-growth term, a quadratic self-saturation term, and a bilinear cross-interaction term. The continuous system is mapped to an equivalent discrete-time recurrence, which is algebraically rearranged so that the ratio of successive values is linear in the current state, allowing the interaction coefficients to be fitted by linear regression on a short observed series. Fitted coefficients are back-transformed to recover the growth, saturation, and interaction parameters, and fit quality is reported via mean absolute percentage deviation. The non-trivial interior fixed point is obtained in closed form by simultaneously zeroing both growth expressions. Local stability is assessed by linearizing about the fixed point through the Jacobian and inspecting the signs of its eigenvalues, classifying the equilibrium as an attracting node. Finally a variance-based global sensitivity analysis is performed: each parameter is perturbed uniformly within a band around its estimate, the closed-form equilibrium is re-evaluated over many quasi-random samples, and the total output variance is decomposed into first-order and total contributions per parameter to rank their influence. The procedure is repeated for two distinct pairings of the quantities, and the sensitivity rankings are compared across pairings to identify which couplings dominate each equilibrium coordinate.
