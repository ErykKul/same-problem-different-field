MECHANISM: A given matrix is assumed to factor as a product of an unknown basis-like matrix with unit-norm columns and an unknown coefficient matrix that is mostly zeros. Recovery is posed as minimizing the sum of absolute values of all coefficient entries subject to the exact factorization constraint, while the column-normalization constraint confines the basis-like factor to a manifold; this objective is nonconvex and nonsmooth wherever a coefficient is exactly zero. Because the ordinary gradient is undefined at such points, the analysis replaces it by one-sided directional derivatives and characterizes when the ground-truth pair is a strict local minimizer through an inequality that must hold along every admissible direction. This condition is reduced to requiring, for each coordinate, that a particular signed combination of coefficient columns lies inside the convex polytope formed by projecting a high-dimensional unit hypercube through a submatrix of the coefficients, with a correction term that shrinks as the basis columns become more mutually orthogonal. The geometric condition is decoupled into three scalar quantities measuring the inscribed-ball radius of the polytope, the norm of the signed column sum, and a row-norm, plus a coherence measure of the basis. Treating the coefficients as drawn from a spike-and-slab law (a zero spike of fixed mass mixed with a continuous component), concentration-of-measure inequalities bound these quantities, yielding a high-probability guarantee that a sufficiently incoherent basis is a strict local minimizer once the number of observed columns exceeds a constant times the dimension up to a logarithmic factor, with failure probability exponentially small in the sample count. Low-dimensional enumeration of the objective surface corroborates that no spurious local minima appear.
