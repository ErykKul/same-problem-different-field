MECHANISM: A pretrained layered message-passing network with frozen weights is adapted to a downstream labeled task by learning a small set of additive correction vectors injected into the per-layer node representations, while leaving the backbone fixed. Robustness is sought by casting adaptation as a saddle-point objective: an inner maximizer searches for worst-case additive perturbations to both the attribute matrix and the adjacency structure that maximize the task loss, and an outer minimizer tunes the injected corrections to minimize the loss under those perturbations. The inner problem is solved by iterated projected gradient ascent that simultaneously updates a continuous attribute-perturbation matrix and a relaxed edge-flip matrix using the sign or raw gradient, then projects back onto a norm ball for attributes and onto a sparse binary feasible set for edges via thresholding and probabilistic rounding. The outer corrections are produced by a low-rank bottleneck transformation of the current representations and trained by gradient descent on a combined objective summing adversarial loss, clean-data loss, and a consistency penalty aligning clean and perturbed predictions. The two stages alternate until convergence. A closed-form characterization shows that, under the linear-propagation layer form, additive corrections exist that exactly cancel both the attribute and structure perturbations, with explicit expressions involving the inverse of the normalized adjacency. Performance is evaluated by discrimination scores on clean and attacked inputs and by parameter and latency efficiency.
