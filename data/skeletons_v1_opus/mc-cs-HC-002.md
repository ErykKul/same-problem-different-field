MECHANISM: A controlled within-subject experiment measures one or more continuous and ordinal response quantities from each participant under several repeated conditions, with condition order balanced across participants. For each participant and condition, repeated trials are averaged to a per-condition value. To test whether the condition has an effect, the total variability of the response across conditions is partitioned into a between-condition component and a within-subject residual component, accounting for the repeated-measures correlation. The ratio of the between-condition mean variability to the residual mean variability forms a test statistic compared against its null reference distribution to obtain a significance level, and a normalized effect-size proportion is reported. When the omnibus test is significant, pairwise contrasts between conditions are examined. The same procedure is applied separately to each response quantity. Qualitative free-text responses are coded and summarized thematically to complement the numerical results. The procedure yields, per response, a decision about whether condition means differ and an estimate of the magnitude of that difference.
