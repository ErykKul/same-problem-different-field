MECHANISM: The aim is to construct a state feedback that reshapes the dynamics of an affine input-driven nonlinear system so that the closed loop has a prescribed structured form with an energy-like scalar function that is minimized at a target equilibrium. The central obstruction is a first-order partial differential equation, the matching condition, that the gradient of the unknown scalar function must satisfy after projection by a left annihilator of the input map. The work characterizes a regularity case in which the homogeneous part of this equation admits as many independent solutions as inputs, and proves this lets one introduce a coordinate change splitting the state into these solution coordinates and complementary ones. Under the change of variables the equation collapses to a lower-order one that fixes the gradient with respect to the complementary coordinates in closed form. To recover the gradient with respect to the remaining coordinates, the symmetry of the Hessian of the scalar function (equality of mixed partial derivatives) is imposed, yielding integrability conditions whose solution is obtained by line integration along a segment, an application of the mean-value identity for vector-valued maps. Free symmetric-matrix degrees of freedom are then tuned, via a Schur-complement positivity argument on the Hessian at the equilibrium, to guarantee the minimum condition and hence stability. Sufficient conditions for the regularity case are given in terms of involutivity of certain distributions and gradient-field (exactness) conditions. The construction is verified analytically on a worked physical example, reducing the original equation to a trivially solvable scalar one. The output is an explicit feedback law plus a stability certificate.
