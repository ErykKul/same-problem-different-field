MECHANISM: Each entity carries a vector-valued state on a bounded box plus a normalized weight vector. A pairwise interaction rule updates each entity's state by a step toward the other entity, scaled coordinatewise by a non-increasing function of a weighted cross-coordinate distance, making the interaction generally non-reciprocal. From this microscopic rule a population-level balance equation for the density over states and weights is written as gain minus loss of binary collisions. Taking a small-step (grazing) limit and rescaling time reduces the collision operator to a transport equation of advection type, where the velocity field is the density-averaged pull from all other entities. Boundary characteristics are shown to point inward, so the support stays in the box without imposed boundary conditions. Existence and uniqueness of a measure-valued solution are obtained by establishing Lipschitz continuity of the velocity field and applying a fixed-point argument along characteristic curves; mass conservation and nonnegativity follow from the weak form and an exponential representation along characteristics. The evolution of the mean and of the variance is computed by testing against linear and quadratic functions; with equal weights the mean is conserved and the variance is monotonically non-increasing (exponentially decaying in a constant-interaction limit), while unequal weights can make the variance grow, shown via the sign of the eigenvalues of an interaction matrix and a concrete example. Stationary states and their structure are then analyzed and illustrated by direct numerical integration of the dynamics.
