MECHANISM: Given a discrete-time dynamical system whose update map switches between a nominal closed-loop form and a degraded open-loop form according to a binary availability signal, the goal is to certify that trajectories from an initial region never enter a forbidden region under every admissible availability pattern, and optionally to design the feedback map that guarantees this. Admissible patterns, constrained to allow at most a bounded count of failures per sliding window, are encoded as the accepted language of a finite directed labeled graph whose edges carry the number of consecutive degraded steps. A separate scalar certificate function is assigned to each graph node; the method imposes algebraic inequalities along each edge requiring the certificate to be nonpositive on the initial region, positive on the forbidden region, and, after a nominal step followed by the edge's degraded steps, to stay below margins that scale with the remaining allowed failures. An inductive argument over paths shows these node-wise conditions imply the trajectory never crosses the zero level set, hence safety. To ease computation, the implication-and-recursion conditions are weakened into per-step decrease conditions and conditions avoiding repeated map composition, trading exactness for tractability. For affine dynamics with quadratic regions and quadratic certificates, the conditions become matrix inequalities via a quadratic-form relaxation, linear when the map is fixed and bilinear when the feedback is also unknown, solved by alternating convex subproblems. For polynomial dynamics they become sum-of-squares feasibility programs with multiplier polynomials. Per-iteration cost is polynomial in variables and constraints, while the graph size can grow exponentially in the window length.
