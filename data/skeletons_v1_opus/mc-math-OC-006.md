MECHANISM: A nonlinear vector differential equation governs the rigid-body state of a system whose inertia operator itself depends on a controllable internal offset, giving coupled dynamics. At each step the nonlinear model is linearized by a first-order expansion about the current operating point and discretized with a zero-order hold to yield a time-varying linear state-space recursion with additive process and measurement terms. An unknown, slowly varying external input is appended to the state vector, and a recursive two-stage estimator is run: a prediction step propagates the augmented mean and its error covariance through the linear transition with added process covariance, and a correction step forms a gain from the predicted covariance and measurement covariance, updates the mean by the innovation, and downdates the covariance. All additive errors are assumed zero-mean normal. The estimated state and disturbance then seed a finite-horizon optimal-control problem: future states are rolled out through the linear prediction model, and a quadratic objective penalizing tracking error, input effort, input increments, and slack on softened state bounds is minimized subject to affine equality and inequality constraints on states, inputs, and input rates. An integer on-off actuator constraint is relaxed to a continuous box and later quantized to a pulse. The convex quadratic program is solved each step, only the first control move is applied, and the whole estimate-then-optimize cycle repeats with new measurements. A redundant set of actuators is mapped from the three-axis command by a minimum-norm pseudo-inverse, with a sequential saturation-and-redistribute scheme enforcing per-actuator limits.
DOMAIN: spacecraft attitude control, solar-sail momentum management
STRUCTURE: dense linear algebra

INFERENCE: bayesian posterior
