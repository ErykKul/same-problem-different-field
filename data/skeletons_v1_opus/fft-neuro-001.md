MECHANISM: For each recorded one-dimensional signal, the signal is segmented into overlapping windowed pieces and each piece is mapped to the frequency domain via a fixed-length orthogonal transform to estimate its power as a function of frequency. The per-piece power estimates are averaged and integrated over contiguous frequency intervals to obtain interval energies. Each interval energy is divided by the total energy over a reference band to yield normalized proportions per interval. The normalized power across frequency is treated as a probability distribution and its dispersion is summarized by an entropy functional, optionally divided by the maximum-entropy value. A second transform extracts the instantaneous phase of each signal, and for each pair of signals the magnitude of the time-averaged complex exponential of their phase difference is computed as a synchrony index between zero and one. These per-entity scalar summaries are computed across many channels and frequency intervals for two groups of entities. Group differences in each summary are assessed with two-sample location tests, with multiplicity correction applied across the many simultaneous comparisons. Within one group, the monotonic association between each summary and an external scalar score is quantified by a rank-correlation coefficient, again with multiplicity correction and adjustment for a confounding covariate. The pipeline thus combines a deterministic transform-based feature extraction with classical group-comparison and association testing.
