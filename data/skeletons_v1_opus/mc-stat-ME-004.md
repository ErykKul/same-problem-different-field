MECHANISM: For a stationary sequence whose observations are square-integrable functions on a domain sampled on a dense grid, the second-order structure is summarized by the lag-indexed autocovariance operators, whose frequency transform defines the spectral density operator, a nonnegative self-adjoint kernel operator at each frequency admitting an eigen-decomposition into frequency-varying eigenvalues and eigenfunctions. The target spectral density operator is approximated by a constructed class of objects built from multilayer feed-forward maps on the domain: shallow and deep parameterized functions are combined as outer products weighted by nonnegative frequency-dependent coefficients, and finite frequency-transformed sequences of such networks reproduce the eigenstructure. It is proven that this class is dense in the integrated operator norm, a universal-approximation guarantee, and that its elements are exactly the spectral densities of latent sequences formed by random scores driving network basis fields. To fit the networks without ever forming the prohibitive grid-by-grid kernels, the training loss is the integrated operator-norm difference between lag-window kernel estimators of the data and of the network fields; this squared norm is algebraically expanded into sums of plain inner products of observed and generated fields weighted by window and frequency factors, so each term costs work linear rather than quadratic in the grid size, and the resulting objective is minimized by gradient-based optimization and parallelized across frequencies.
