MECHANISM: Two disjoint sets of units are linked by a weighted bipartite map, and each receiving unit's exposure is a map-weighted average of binary treatments assigned to the acting units. Under an additive outcome model in which the response depends linearly on exposure with entity-specific main and effect terms plus mean-zero noise, a per-subgroup welfare functional is shown to be linear in the assignment policy, equal to a map-weighted sum of an estimated effect function over the receiving units belonging to that subgroup. The effect function is estimated by a doubly robust estimating equation that stays root-count consistent when either a baseline or a propensity component is correct. The set of policies for which no subgroup's welfare can improve without another's worsening is characterized as the minimizers of weighted welfare sums over a probability-weight simplex, then approximated on an equally spaced grid with a slack tolerance. A constrained program with binary indicators selects a policy minimizing the absolute welfare gap between two subgroups subject to lying within slack of at least one grid optimum and to a linear budget limit on total assignment cost. Concentration arguments under spatial weak-dependence and bounded-complexity function classes establish that the welfare estimates converge at an inverse-square-root rate, that the recovered frontier contains the true frontier with high probability, and that the resulting disparity gap attains an inverse-square-root excess bound. The method is validated by repeated synthetic resampling that regenerates treatments and outcomes and averages welfare and disparity metrics.
