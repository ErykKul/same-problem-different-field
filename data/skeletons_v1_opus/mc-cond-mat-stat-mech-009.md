MECHANISM: An object is described by a length-two-to-the-N amplitude vector over a system of N binary subunits, and the goal is to estimate scalar functionals of the distribution of a basis of structured sign-pattern operators. For a fixed operator, its expectation reduces to a sum over the amplitude vector with alternating signs determined by a bit pattern, and computing all such expectations jointly is recast as a fast recursive orthogonal transform of the amplitude vector. By partitioning the operator basis exactly and applying this fast transform, the average cost of evaluating one sampled operator's contribution is reduced from exponential to linear in N. The target functionals are then approximated by drawing operators at random and averaging their transformed contributions, with a variance-reducing preconditioning step applied first to flatten the sampling distribution. The number of random draws needed for a target accuracy is found empirically to be roughly independent of system size. The method is applied to states built by interleaving structure-preserving operations with sparse non-structure-injecting operations, and a control ratio governs how the functional grows. A gap between the sampled estimate and a fully randomized reference is observed to shrink exponentially in the number of injected operations. Closed-form limiting values of the functional per injected operation are derived and matched against the numerics. Overall the computation pairs a fast deterministic transform with a randomized averaging estimator.
