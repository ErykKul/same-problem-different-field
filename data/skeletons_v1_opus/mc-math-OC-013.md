MECHANISM: The goal is to minimize a smooth strongly convex objective defined as the expectation of a noisy function, accessible only through queries that return perturbed scalar values, with no access to derivatives and with the query noise generated by a correlated, geometrically mixing stochastic sequence rather than independent draws. A directional derivative is approximated by a symmetric finite difference between two nearby query points along a probe direction, and scaling this by the dimension and the direction vector gives an unbiased-up-to-smoothing estimate of the gradient. To suppress the amplified noise, queries are aggregated; the key observation is that subsampling every k-th element of the correlated sequence reduces its effective correlation length, so probe directions are assigned across interleaved subsequences to reconstruct all gradient components while shrinking variance from a product of dimension and mixing length to their sum. A randomized-direction estimator further decomposes the error into a direction-variance term and a bias term, each removing one factor. A multilevel telescoping construction with geometrically growing batch sizes and randomized truncation removes residual bias while keeping the expected query count controlled. The resulting estimator is fed into an accelerated first-order iteration, yielding query-complexity bounds to reach a target accuracy under both single-query and paired-query feedback, for smooth and nonsmooth cases via an additional randomized-smoothing argument. Matching information-theoretic lower bounds are proved via multidimensional hypothesis-testing arguments adapted to the correlated-noise setting, and a numerical experiment on a quadratic confirms the predicted additive scaling.
