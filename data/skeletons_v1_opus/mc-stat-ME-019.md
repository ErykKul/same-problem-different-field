MECHANISM: The method discovers groups of entities whose response to a binary intervention differs, using a similarity built from a large ensemble of randomized recursive partitioners. Each partitioner is grown on a bootstrap resample with randomized candidate features, and each candidate split is scored by fitting a proportional-hazards model with the group indicator, the intervention indicator, and their interaction to the right-censored time-to-event outcome; the score blends a concordance measure with the standardized interaction effect, so the split rule explicitly targets differential intervention effect rather than raw outcome prediction. For every ordered pair of entities, a proximity is defined as the fraction of trees in which they land in the same terminal cell, and these co-membership matrices are aggregated across many forests trained under varied hyperparameters into one dense similarity matrix. This matrix is treated as a weighted graph adjacency; the leading eigenvectors of its graph Laplacian give a low-dimensional embedding that is partitioned into clusters. A shallow interpretable partitioner is then fit to label the clusters by feature thresholds, and nested proportional-hazards models with and without cluster-by-intervention terms are compared by a likelihood-ratio statistic. The number of clusters and the declaration of genuine heterogeneity are chosen by a calibrated significance threshold whose null distribution is obtained empirically from homogeneous reference data or permutation, fixing the false-positive rate. Group-level effect sizes are summarized as hazard ratios with associated tests.
