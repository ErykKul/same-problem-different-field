MECHANISM: A domain is formalized as a closed collection of entities together with a directed relation whose ordered pairs denote admissible flows from a source to a destination entity. A distinguished subset of entities is designated internal by a partition, inducing a classification of every relation pair as internal, inbound-crossing, outbound-crossing, or external relative to that partition; this classification is shown to be mutually exclusive and exhaustive. Each entity carries a state set and a transition map from state and admissible input to state, and admissibility is gated by membership of a flow value in a receiving map's domain. From a small set of axioms, definitions of the constructs, and propositional inference, a sequence of theorems is derived: an exogenous admissible flow may trigger a state transition and activate a specific relation pair; a relation pair existing does not entail it being active, activation being relative to a context that selects an active subset under a given input; outcome propositions are truth-valued only relative to such a context and are determined by an associated active subset. Further results prove invariance of outcome truth under context variations that preserve a sufficient subset, existence of an inclusion-minimal sufficient subset for each determinable outcome, non-uniqueness of such minimal subsets, safe removal of pairs absent from all minimal sets, and invariance of outcome truth under re-partitioning even though the internal/external classification changes. A culminating characterization gives necessary-and-sufficient conditions, as an equivalence, for a representation to determine all targeted outcomes across contexts, with a corollary that enlarging the targeted set can break a prior sufficiency claim. The partition operation is shown to apply recursively to any internal sub-entity.
