MECHANISM: A rectangular cross-association matrix between two groups of variables is estimated from a finite window of paired observations, then decomposed by singular value factorization into orthonormal left/right direction pairs and nonnegative strengths. The estimator is denoised within the rotationally invariant class, keeping the empirical directions fixed and replacing each empirical strength with a corrected value; an analytical baseline computes the correction in closed form from resolvent-type scalar functionals of the full spectrum and of projections of the marginal second-moment matrices onto the directions, valid under stationarity and spectral-boundedness assumptions. To operate beyond those assumptions, the correction map is parameterized by a trainable model: per-index feature tokens (empirical strength, marginal projection, aspect ratio) for each side are embedded by a shared small multilayer map, fused symmetrically, passed through a bidirectional recurrent sequence aggregator to inject global spectral context, and decoded by a pointwise map into an additive residual on each strength. The architecture is constructed so the analytical closed-form correction is exactly representable as one parameter setting. Parameters are fit by gradient descent to minimize squared reconstruction error against a target matrix computed on a subsequent out-of-sample window, with the cleaned matrix rebuilt from the original directions and corrected strengths and rescaled by marginal scales. A feasibility analysis via a congruence/complement argument shows admissible corrected strengths lie in the unit interval after whitening. A complementary cross-validation variant estimates corrected strengths by projecting held-out second-moment matrices onto directions learned on training folds and averaging, optionally enforcing monotonicity by isotonic regression. The computation is spectral shrinkage of a matrix's singular values, parameterized either analytically or by a learned map.
