MECHANISM: A latent sequence evolves through an unknown transition map perturbed by additive noise, and each latent value emits a noisy observation through a second map. A prior is placed over the unknown transition map as a jointly Gaussian collection of function values whose covariance is given by a pairwise similarity function, making the latent values and function values jointly distributed. To make computation independent of the sequence length, the function is summarized by a small set of auxiliary representative inputs and their values, which are jointly Gaussian with the function and on which all far-away function behavior is conditioned. An approximate factored distribution over the latent path, the auxiliary values, and the function is posited, and a lower bound on the log marginal evidence is constructed by the standard inequality, with the difficult conditional function terms cancelling. The expectations over function values are solved in closed form, yielding a bound expressed through similarity-matrix products and traces. The optimal distribution over the auxiliary values is shown to be Gaussian with natural parameters given by sums of expected statistics over consecutive latent pairs. The optimal latent-path distribution reduces to the smoothing distribution of a simpler auxiliary Markov chain with Gaussian transitions and an augmented emission term. The bound is then maximized by alternating between drawing approximate samples of the latent path with a sequential particle smoother and taking stochastic gradient steps in the bound's parameters and the natural parameters. Because the required statistics are sums over the sequence, unbiased estimates from random subsequences enable mini-batch and incremental online updating, and predictions at new inputs depend only on the auxiliary values at fixed cost.
