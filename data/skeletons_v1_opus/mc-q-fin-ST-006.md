MECHANISM: A scalar target observed over time is modeled as a constant plus a weighted sum of several lagged values of a single unobserved scalar driver, where the driver is itself a linear combination of a vector of observed explanatory series via cross-sectional weights. This couples two coefficient vectors multiplicatively: a time-lag coefficient vector and a cross-sectional weight vector, which combine through a Kronecker product against a matrix of all lagged regressors. The estimator is obtained by alternating two conditional least-squares solves: holding the weight vector fixed, the lag coefficients are the closed-form normal-equation solution using the regressor covariance matrix and the regressor-target covariance; holding the lag coefficients fixed, the weight vector is the analogous closed-form solution. Each solve requires inverting a structured Gram matrix, and the two updates are iterated to a fixed point from an arbitrary scale-invariant initialization, with the intercept recovered from sample means. For multi-step prediction, unobserved future values of the latent driver are filled in by a first-order autoregressive shrinkage toward the sample mean. An optional moving-average error term is added and its parameter fit by Gaussian maximum likelihood via a derivative-free simplex search. The procedure is run across thousands of combinatorially generated regressor subsets, producing rolling out-of-sample forecasts at multiple horizons; accuracy is scored by mean squared prediction error, methods are ranked per specification and horizon, and improvements over univariate baselines are assessed with one-sided variance-ratio tests. Per-variable effects are summarized by comparing median errors of specifications including versus excluding each regressor.
