MECHANISM: A multivariate response is modeled as a linear function of predictors with structured noise, restricted so that only a low-dimensional subspace of the response carries predictor-related signal while the complementary directions are immaterial. The target of inference is this subspace, represented through an unconstrained matrix coordinate that removes a manifold/orthogonality constraint, with the remaining blocks given conjugate priors. The intractable posterior is approximated by a factorized (mean-field) family, fit by maximizing an evidence lower bound through coordinate ascent: each block's optimal factor is the exponential of the expected log conditional under the other factors. Conjugate blocks have closed-form factor updates whose moments are available analytically; the subspace-coordinate block is nonconjugate, so its update is approximated by a local second-order (Laplace) expansion around the maximizer of the expected log conditional, producing a Gaussian factor with mean at the mode and covariance from the inverse curvature. A reparameterization is introduced that algebraically cancels matrix inverse-square-root factors in the likelihood, eliminating a dominant differentiation bottleneck (large structured tensor-sum inversions) and yielding closed-form gradient and curvature for the nonconjugate block. Iterations cycle the Laplace update and the conjugate closed-form updates until the bound stabilizes. A theoretical analysis bounds the discrepancy between the Laplace coordinate update and the exact update in total-variation distance, showing it vanishes asymptotically under curvature and localization conditions. The method is evaluated on simulated and real data for estimation accuracy and model selection.
