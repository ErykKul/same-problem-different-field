MECHANISM: A set of many observed quantities is summarized by their pairwise association matrix, a dense symmetric matrix of correlations or covariances among the quantities. The model posits that each observed quantity is a linear combination of a small number of unobserved latent quantities plus an individual residual, so that the population association matrix admits a structured decomposition into a low-rank term, a loadings matrix times a latent-association matrix times its transpose, plus a diagonal matrix of residual variances. Estimation finds the loadings and latent associations that make the model-implied matrix best match the observed matrix, by iteratively minimizing a discrepancy function, either an unweighted sum of squared elementwise differences or a likelihood-based criterion. Identification constraints fix the latent scale, and an orthogonal or oblique transformation of the low-rank factor is applied afterward to sharpen the loading pattern toward interpretability. Diagnostic quantities are then computed from the fitted model: per-observation residual vectors are reconstructed from estimated latent scores, a squared standardized distance of each observation from the multivariate center is computed using the inverse association matrix, and a deletion-based influence measure quantifies how much each observation shifts the parameter estimates. Near-degeneracy of the input matrix is checked through its eigenvalues and the ratio of largest to smallest. When the observed quantities are ordinal, the pairwise associations are instead estimated under an assumed continuous latent bivariate structure before the same decomposition is applied.
DOMAIN: psychometrics and latent-variable measurement
STRUCTURE: dense linear algebra
DATA_OBJECT: dense matrix or tensor
INFERENCE: maximum likelihood
PROBLEM_FORM: estimation
DISTRIBUTION: continuous; gaussian
COMPLEXITY: consistency
