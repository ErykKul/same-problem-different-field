MECHANISM: The work empirically profiles a parallel computation that has been offloaded from general processors to accelerators, and quantifies the joint cost in runtime and energy rather than introducing a new algorithm. For a fixed-work and a proportionally-growing-work regime, the same kernel is run on increasing numbers of compute units while wall-clock time and integrated power draw are recorded from hardware counters. From the measured time and energy two scalar comparison figures are formed: a product of energy times runtime raised to an integer weight that tunes the relative emphasis on speed versus energy, and a ratio of runtime speedup over energy ratio relative to a reference configuration. Lower values of the first and higher values of the second indicate the more efficient configuration. The measured speedups and energy reductions are reported as ratios against a baseline and compared across two accelerator architectures. No estimator, model, or mathematical derivation is fit; the analysis is the tabulation and ratio-comparison of measured quantities.
