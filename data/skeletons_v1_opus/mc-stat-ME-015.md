MECHANISM: For two groups of units followed over time, each unit accrues a counting process of repeated events that is truncated by an absorbing terminal event, and the goal is to test whether group assignment affects the repeated-event process through a pathway distinct from the one acting on the terminal process. The follow-up window is partitioned into many small intervals, and a hypothetical assignment is split into two components, one acting only on the repeated process and one only on the terminal process, formalized through counterfactual mean functions and a directed acyclic ordering. Under consistency, exchangeability, positivity, and dismissible-component conditions, a g-formula expresses the counterfactual mean as a sum of increments reweighted by a product of conditional terminal-event probability ratios, with a separate term for the at-risk set; the survival factor is supplied by a discrete product-limit estimator. A multiplicative proportional-rate structural model relates the two counterfactual mean functions by a single constant log-ratio parameter, which is estimated by solving an integral estimating equation summing at-risk-weighted increment differences. Setting the parameter to its null value yields a score functional whose value is standardized by a consistent variance estimator to form a test statistic. Under regularity and martingale conditions the statistic converges to a standard normal limit, giving a one-number, time-integrated test. Finite-sample behavior is checked by repeated synthetic-data replication that varies the baseline event rate over time, comparing error rates and power against an alternative normalization-based test. The method is then applied to a matched observational cohort.
