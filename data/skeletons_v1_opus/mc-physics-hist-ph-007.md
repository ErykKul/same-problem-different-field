MECHANISM: Starting from a canonical commutation relation, the most general momentum-like operator consistent with it is written as a gradient plus an arbitrary vector function, and the coupling parameter to an external gauge field is allowed to take complex values. Substituting the complexified coupling into the governing wave equation and inserting an amplitude-phase decomposition splits the equation into a real transport equation and a real continuity equation, both carrying extra terms proportional to the imaginary coupling. The imaginary part is shown to realize a scale-covariant derivative, so the continuity equation conserves an amplitude-squared quantity only with respect to that covariant derivative; the ordinarily conserved object becomes the ratio of amplitude-squared to a scale factor. That scale factor is solved in closed form as the exponential of a path-dependent line integral of the gauge field along the trajectory fixed by the guidance velocity, with an initial condition chosen so the standard theory is recovered when the imaginary coupling vanishes. Invariance of all results under gauge transformations is verified, and uniqueness of the trajectories is established by showing that any divergence-free addition to the current changes the equilibrium density. The construction is repeated for single- and multi-particle, spin-carrying non-relativistic, relativistic, and field-quantized cases, each yielding an analogous covariant continuity equation and trajectory-dependent density. A relative-entropy functional is introduced to argue relaxation to the modified equilibrium density. A small worked example integrates the trajectory equations under an imaginary potential and evaluates the line-integral scale factor numerically. The work is predominantly a closed-form symbolic derivation establishing conserved quantities and invariance properties.
