MECHANISM: A two-stage procedure integrates many correlated auxiliary measurements recorded at several time points to improve prediction of a single focal quantity across related entities. In the first stage, for each time point the auxiliary measurements are modeled as a sum of a structured between-entity component and an independent residual, both multivariate normal, and the between-entity covariance is estimated and rescaled to a correlation matrix. A low-rank latent-factor decomposition of that correlation matrix is fit by minimizing a normal-theory discrepancy between the sample matrix and a model-implied loadings-plus-diagonal form, with the number of factors chosen from the eigenvalue curve. The loadings are first rotated toward an approximately sparse form, then aligned across time points by solving an orthogonal alignment that minimizes a squared-distance to a chosen reference, recovering a signed permutation so the factors keep a consistent meaning over time. Per-entity factor scores are obtained by a regression-style projection of the measurements onto the latent space. In the second stage, the selected factor scores and the focal quantity are stacked and modeled jointly as multivariate normal with a separable covariance combining a between-entity similarity matrix and a residual structure, and the focal quantity is predicted by the conditional best linear unbiased predictor. A subset of factors is chosen by an information criterion, and accuracy is assessed by repeated split-sample correlation.
