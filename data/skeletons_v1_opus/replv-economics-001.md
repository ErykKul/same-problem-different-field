MECHANISM: The analysis studies a system of coupled nonlinear ordinary differential equations on a probability simplex, where the rate of change of each component equals the component times the gap between its own return and the mean return, with returns given by a fixed payoff matrix acting on the state vector. The unique interior fixed point is found in closed form as a normalized vector parameterized by a single matrix entry. The dynamics are linearized about the fixed point by computing the Jacobian matrix there, whose entries are obtained explicitly as rational functions of the parameter. The eigenvalues and eigenvectors of this dense matrix are then computed in closed form; a conjugate pair of purely imaginary eigenvalues signals a center manifold and persistent oscillation. From each complex eigenvector, a set of plane-projected rotation invariants is built, one per coordinate pair, each defined as the product of the two component magnitudes times the sine of their phase difference, giving signed areas that encode the rotational structure; invariants tied to real eigenvalues vanish and conjugate pairs give opposite signs. A separate rotation-axis vector is defined by analogy to an angular-momentum vector from the trajectory in the reduced coordinates. The same closed-form pipeline is applied to several alternative update rules and noise levels. These theoretical invariants are then compared against the same invariants measured from agent-based simulation runs and from recorded interaction sequences, using correlation coefficients, a difference-of-means test, and linear regression to assess agreement.
