MECHANISM: Given a one-dimensional sequence of regularly sampled observations modeled as a stationary random process, the goal is to estimate its second-order frequency-domain density. First the squared modulus of the discrete frequency transform of the windowed sequence is formed, giving a noisy per-frequency intensity estimate whose values fluctuate around the true spectrum with a known multiplicative chi-square law. A logarithmic transform converts this multiplicative fluctuation into additive zero-mean noise with a known fixed variance. The transformed sequence is then decomposed into a hierarchy of coarse-trend and detail components by repeated convolution with paired low-pass and high-pass filters followed by downsampling by two. The detail components, whose values follow a generalized symmetric exponential-power law, are shrunk toward zero by soft thresholds set from tail-probability quantiles at a chosen significance level, removing the additive noise while retaining the underlying trend. The trend and thresholded detail components are recombined by the inverse upsample-and-filter recursion to reconstruct a smoothed estimate of the log-density, which is exponentiated. For nonstationary input an alternative path decomposes both trend and detail branches into a uniform time-frequency tiling, takes the squared modulus, and reports a robust per-band central order statistic across the time axis as the density estimate. The methods are evaluated by resolution, a mean-squared stability ratio, and downstream matched-template detection scores.
