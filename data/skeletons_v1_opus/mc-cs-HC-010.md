MECHANISM: The study collects repeated-measure responses from a set of participants assigned to two groups, plus behavioral observations coded into discrete categories from recorded sessions. Each participant yields an ordinal score on several items before and after an intervention, and a per-item change is formed as the difference of post and pre values. To test whether group assignment affects these changes, the distribution of each change variable is first screened for departure from normality with a goodness-of-fit test, and because departures are found, two-sample comparisons between the groups are carried out with a rank-based nonparametric test on each item. A multiplicity correction tightens the rejection threshold for the family of items. A second analysis pools both groups and tests, per item, whether the paired pre-versus-post change differs from zero using a signed-rank paired nonparametric test, again with the multiplicity-corrected threshold. The coded behavioral observations are then summarized as category frequencies and tabulated across successive identical stimulus events, and per-event central tendency and dispersion of response latencies are reported. Internal consistency of the multi-item instrument is summarized by a reliability coefficient. No predictive model is fit; the inferential core is a battery of distribution-free hypothesis tests on ordinal differences, supplemented by descriptive frequency and timing tabulations and qualitative comparison against a prior reference study.
