MECHANISM: A bounded quantity on the unit interval is modeled as the ratio of one nonnegative quantity to the sum of two nonnegative quantities, where the pair has a dependent joint distribution built by coupling two marginal distributions through a one-parameter dependence function (a copula) that admits both positive and negative association. With each margin taken as a two-parameter nonnegative distribution sharing a scale, a change-of-variables (Jacobian) integration yields a closed-form density for the ratio, expressed as a signed combination of base terms involving special functions; the classical two-parameter bounded density is recovered when the dependence parameter is zero. The cumulative distribution, moments via a moment-generating series, and symmetry conditions are derived analytically using integral identities for these special functions, and the density is characterized equivalently as a signed mixture of ratio variables. Because closed-form root conditions for shape are intractable, regions of multimodality are mapped by numerical grid search over the parameter space. Parameters are estimated by maximizing the log-likelihood numerically, since no analytic maximizer exists; a cheaper two-stage approach first fits the margins, then estimates the dependence parameter by maximizing a copula pseudolikelihood or via a moment-matching closed form. A simulation study draws repeated samples by inverse-transform sampling from the coupled joint and reports bias and root-mean-square error of the estimators across sample sizes, and identifiability is probed through the information matrix's smallest eigenvalue, determinant, and conditioning. Model fit to data is assessed by information criteria, quantile residuals, and a likelihood-ratio test of the dependence parameter.
