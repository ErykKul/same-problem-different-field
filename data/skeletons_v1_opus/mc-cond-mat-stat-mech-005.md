MECHANISM: The aim is to derive a closed expression for the limiting log-normalization-constant density of a disordered system of many binary variables coupled by random pairwise weights drawn from a Gaussian ensemble. The starting point is an incremental functional from an add-one-variable argument, expressing the change in the log-normalizer as the difference of two logarithms of measure-averages, one involving a hyperbolic-cosine of a random local field and one an exponential of a quadratic correction term, both driven by fresh independent Gaussian noise. The configuration measure is written as a mixture of atomic product measures indexed by configurations, with mixture weights set by Boltzmann factors. The key ansatz partitions the variables into a nested increasing sequence of blocks and assumes the equilibrium measure factorizes across blocks as a product of independent sub-measures, each modeled as an extreme-value-type random measure whose atoms are normalized points of a Poisson point process. The random field and the correction term are decomposed additively over blocks, with each block contribution scaled by the square-root of differences of block-size fractions, and shown equal in distribution to independent standard Gaussians. A special averaging identity for the Poisson point process converts an average of a function against the normalized atoms into a power-mean of the function with an exponent equal to the process rate. Applying this identity recursively from the innermost block outward produces a chain of nested power-mean recursions, and the proportionality constants from each level cancel between the two terms. The result reproduces the known variational functional exactly, and an alternative overlap-hierarchical factorization is shown to give the identical functional.
