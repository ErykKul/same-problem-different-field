MECHANISM: Two interdependent quantities are defined over the two sides of a bipartite weighted incidence structure, where each quantity at a node on one side is updated from the weighted values at its neighbors on the other side, one update being a weighted sum and the reciprocal one being an inverse of a weighted sum of reciprocals, followed by normalization by the current mean. The question is when this nonlinear iteration converges to a stable fixed point rather than driving a subset of values to zero. The structure is coarse-grained into a two-by-two condensed incidence under a mean-field assumption that all members of a group share a common value, collapsing the update into four scalar recurrences whose normalization constants cancel when ratios are formed. Taking ratios of the two paired values yields a pair of affine recurrences in two ratio variables, x' = a1 y + b1 and y' = a2 x + b2, which converge if and only if the product of the two linear coefficients is below one. Expressed in the structural multiplicities this gives a clean criterion: the product of group sizes along the connected diagonal must exceed the product along the unconnected diagonal, independent of edge weights. A harmonic-versus-arithmetic mean inequality shows the mean-field rate never overestimates the true rate, so the criterion is a necessary condition. Limiting regimes reduce the criterion to a minimum-degree-versus-aspect-ratio condition. Remedies are analyzed: removing or merging low-degree nodes, or adding a constant regularizer to each update, which enlarges the convergence region by shrinking the effective coefficient product.
