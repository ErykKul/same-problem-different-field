MECHANISM: A large collection of entities is compressed into a much smaller synthetic collection such that a parametric model fitted to the synthetic collection reaches loss comparable to one fitted to the original; the synthetic collection is produced by iteratively descending a matching objective between gradient-descent weight trajectories. The adversarial procedure first fits many parametric models to synthetic collections produced by every pairing of generating procedure and model family, recording the per-step loss sequence of each fit. These loss sequences are used as inputs to train an auxiliary classifier that maps a loss sequence to the identity of the generating procedure and model family. A perturbation argument shows that two parametric models of identical structure fitted to two collections that differ by a bounded per-element displacement remain within an arbitrarily small distance in parameter space for a fixed number of steps, hence their loss sequences stay close, justifying separability. With the inferred structure, a surrogate model is fitted on the synthetic collection, granting full internal access. A second auxiliary binary classifier is then trained on the concatenated intermediate-layer and final-layer outputs of the surrogate to decide whether a given observation belonged to the original collection. Finally a paired generator is trained by iterative noise-removal: one subnetwork predicts an added noise term and a second predicts the clean signal, combined through a learned per-location mixing weight. The generator is optimized with squared-error terms plus a label-consistency term and a trajectory-matching term that forces regenerated entities to reproduce the surrogate's loss sequence. Sampling proceeds through the deterministic reverse recursion to reconstruct representative entities.
