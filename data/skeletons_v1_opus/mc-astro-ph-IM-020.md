MECHANISM: Two-dimensional fields of a measured quantity are recorded over a spatial domain and repeatedly over time, forming a stack of images with an associated per-location temporal sequence. The fields are systematically degraded to emulate coarser observation: spatial resolution is reduced by convolving each image with a synthetic blurring kernel of controllable width, and detector resolution is reduced by aggregating neighboring locations into larger cells via an intensity-weighted average. Sweeping the kernel width and aggregation size produces a two-parameter family of degraded datasets. For each degradation setting, locations are selected by thresholding a secondary field at a high quantile, and for each retained location the temporal sequence is transformed to its frequency-domain power distribution. The per-location spectra are averaged into a single mean spectrum per setting, then normalized, and the integrated power within fixed frequency bands is extracted as a scalar summary. These summaries are mapped against the degradation parameters to reveal how spectral energy redistributes between bands as resolution coarsens. A separate comparison convolves the data with a calibrated instrument-specific kernel and resamples to a target cell size, then differences the resulting spectrum against the original. The distribution of an auxiliary angular quantity is also tracked and shown to flatten toward uniformity as degradation increases. The core computation is spectral transformation of time sequences combined with spatial convolution and aggregation, summarized by band-integrated power.
