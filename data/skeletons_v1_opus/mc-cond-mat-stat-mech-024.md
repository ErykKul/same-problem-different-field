MECHANISM: A parametrized operator acting on the configuration space of N coupled binary variables on a regular lattice is constructed as a sparse symmetric matrix. For finite system sizes, its lowest-lying eigenvector is computed exactly by iterative sparse eigensolution. A scalar diagnostic is defined as the negative second derivative, with respect to an external control parameter, of the overlap magnitude between two ground eigenvectors at infinitesimally separated parameter values, evaluated at zero perturbation; the perturbing term is coupled to the absolute value of a collective sum rather than the raw sum so the diagnostic peaks near a transition. This diagnostic is evaluated over a grid of a tuning parameter for several system sizes. The peak location of the diagnostic is extracted for each size by a derivative root condition, then extrapolated against a power of inverse linear size by least-squares fit to estimate a limiting critical value. The diagnostic is then collapsed onto a single curve using a finite-size scaling ansatz with fixed exponents, confirming a universality class. A second tuning axis measuring distance from a multi-critical point is introduced and the ansatz is extended to a two-argument crossover-scaling form; one scaling dimension is tuned to optimize collapse of the largest sizes, yielding an exponent estimate with uncertainty. As an independent cross-check, a flow-function is formed from finite-difference ratios of the diagnostic across consecutive sizes and compared to its expected linear asymptote, whose zero locates the critical value and whose slope gives the inverse correlation-length exponent.
