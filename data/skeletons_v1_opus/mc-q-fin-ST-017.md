MECHANISM: For each time unit, the allocation of a quantity across an ordered set of horizon bins is treated as a normalized vector of proportions summing to one, and two such vectors (one for a volume measure, one for a value measure) are compared and modeled. Their dissimilarity is computed as the integrated absolute difference of cumulative distribution functions, producing one scalar per time unit and a long series of such scalars. That series is searched for a small number of level shifts by a change-point procedure that minimizes segmented residual sums subject to a minimum segment length and selects the number of breaks by an information criterion, with serial-correlation-robust standard errors and a block-resampling scheme for confidence intervals. Tail behavior is summarized by ratios of mass beyond several thresholds and by fitting an extreme-value exceedance distribution above thresholds via maximum likelihood with method-of-moments fallbacks, then checking whether the fitted shape parameter stabilizes as the threshold rises to detect truncation artifacts. Each time unit's proportion vector is also fit by several two-parameter positive right-skewed families, treating each bin as interval-censored under a truncated continuous law and minimizing a cross-entropy objective by quasi-Newton optimization, with families ranked per unit by that objective. A flexible penalized-spline alternative is fit by restricted likelihood as an upper benchmark, and all fits are scored by a strictly proper cumulative-discrepancy rule and a divergence. The central finding is a systematic shape divergence between the two measures concentrated at mid-range horizons, with bounded tails over the reliable threshold range.
