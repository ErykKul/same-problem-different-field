MECHANISM: Observations are points on a bounded one-dimensional periodic manifold, so a wrap-around distance equal to the shorter of the two arcs between points replaces ordinary difference. Classical spread is defined through the resultant length of unit vectors, but it is shown to be highly sensitive to contamination. Three robust spread functionals are constructed by transporting linear robust-scale ideas to the manifold: the median of distances from a central point, the smallest median distance to any point, and the smallest trimmed root-mean-square over arcs that capture at least half the mass. For each functional, attainable ranges and limiting values under reference parametric families are derived in closed form. Robustness is quantified analytically by computing the influence function, the limiting effect of an infinitesimal point contamination, obtained by differentiating each functional along a contaminated mixture; the dominant functionals yield piecewise-constant or shifted-cosine influence shapes that are bounded. Larger contamination is studied through relative-bias curves evaluated by simulation over a grid of contamination fractions and concentrations. Each robust spread functional is then converted into a consistent estimator of a model dispersion or concentration parameter by composing it with an inverse of the known monotone relation between population spread and the parameter, which itself requires numerical inversion of a special-function ratio. Breakdown values, the largest contamination fraction tolerated before the estimate implodes to zero or explodes to its supremum, are derived for each estimator, and asymptotic relative efficiencies against the maximum-likelihood estimator are computed numerically. Finally, the best-behaved functional yields a fenced anomaly-flagging rule and an accompanying density-shaped visualization, applied to real samples.
