MECHANISM: Given paired scalar observations of an input and a response, an unknown smooth conditional-mean function is estimated as a locally weighted average of responses, with weights given by a symmetric kernel of the scaled input distance under a global bandwidth. A subset of inputs is designated as deterministic anchor points whose responses lie exactly on the target function, and their kernel weights are multiplied by a tuning factor at least one, so increasing the factor pulls the fitted curve through the anchors while leaving the smoothing scale unchanged; the estimator is the closed-form solution of a weighted local constant least-squares problem. A bias-reduction iteration repeatedly forms residuals between current fitted values and the original responses and adds each residual back to the original response, then re-applies the same weighting operator, reducing successive bias orders at modest variance cost. The bandwidth and tuning factor are selected by minimizing a cross-validation error plus a squared anchor-deviation penalty on standardized responses. Asymptotic analysis under shrinking bandwidth with growing local count derives the leading squared-bandwidth bias and the inverse-local-count variance, establishes asymptotic normality at interior points equivalent to the unweighted estimator, and proves convergence in probability to the anchor values under a scaling condition on the tuning factor. The method is evaluated against alternative smoothers using global error, anchor error, and a curvature-based smoothness penalty, and extended to a curve parameterized by a path coordinate.
