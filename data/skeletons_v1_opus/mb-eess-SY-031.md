MECHANISM: A latent state evolving on a curved matrix group is to be reconstructed online from a stream of scalar projection-type measurements together with a noisy rate input that is corrupted by an unknown slowly-varying offset. A coupled dynamical estimator is posed directly on the group: it propagates a state estimate using the rate input minus an offset estimate, and corrects both via an innovation term built from the difference between predicted and observed scalar outputs. The estimation error is linearized about the identity using a local minimal parametrization, producing a linear time-varying error system with known system and output matrices that depend on the trajectory. The correction gain is generated by integrating a matrix differential equation of quadratic-in-state form with chosen weighting matrices, and the innovation is the gain times the output-matrix-transposed times the weighted output error. Local exponential stability of the zero-error equilibrium is established by tying it to a uniform observability condition expressed as a positive-definite lower bound on an integral observability operator over a sliding window. Explicit sufficient conditions are derived in terms of persistence-of-excitation of the rotated reference directions, including counting how many independent scalar channels are needed in static versus moving regimes. The biased case is handled by an augmented error system and a Schur-complement argument linking two integral conditions. The method is then run on recorded sensor data and its error measured against ground truth. The dominant computation is forward integration of the coupled estimator and quadratic gain equation, with stability shown by proof.
