MECHANISM: A densely sampled one-dimensional sequence is compressed to a small set of landmark points and later reconstructed. Local maxima and minima are detected as sign changes of the discrete first difference, each retained only if its prominence, the vertical drop separating it from the nearest higher neighbor, exceeds a threshold that suppresses minor fluctuations; minima are found by repeating the procedure on the negated sequence. To capture shape between these extrema, additional support points are chosen by a greedy iteration: at each step every remaining candidate position is tentatively added, the sequence is reinterpolated through the current landmark set, and the candidate that most reduces the squared reconstruction error is selected, repeating until a target count consistent with a desired compression ratio is reached. Each landmark stores its position and value. Reconstruction fits a shape-preserving piecewise cubic interpolant through the stored landmarks to restore the original resolution while avoiding overshoot. Fidelity is assessed by the ratio of original to compressed size, by mean absolute error between original and reconstructed sequences, and by agreement of summary statistics derived from each, with a uniform decimation baseline that drops every fixed number of samples. The landmark-based scheme preserves excursion-sensitive summaries far better than uniform decimation at equal compression, though rare brief excursions falling between selected points remain underrepresented.
