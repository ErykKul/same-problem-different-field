MECHANISM: A hidden state vector evolves in discrete time according to a known nonlinear transition function with additive process noise, and is observed only through a known nonlinear measurement function with additive observation noise; the goal is to recursively estimate the state and its error covariance. The estimator carries a mean estimate and a covariance matrix and alternates a time-update (prediction) step with a measurement-update (correction) step. In the first variant, prediction propagates the mean through the transition function and propagates the covariance using the local Jacobian matrices of the transition function; correction computes the Jacobian of the measurement function, forms a gain matrix from the predicted covariance and the measurement-noise covariance, and adjusts the mean by the gain times the residual between the actual and predicted observation, then deflates the covariance. In the second variant, rather than linearizing, a deterministic set of sample points is placed symmetrically around the current mean using a matrix square root of the scaled covariance; each point is pushed through the nonlinear transition function and recombined by weighted averaging to give the predicted mean and covariance. The same sample points are mapped through the measurement function to obtain a predicted-observation mean, an observation covariance, and a state-observation cross-covariance, from which the gain is the cross-covariance times the inverse observation covariance. Both variants assume zero-mean noises with specified second moments and produce a point estimate with an associated uncertainty at each step. The procedure is run sequentially over a stream of observations to track the evolving state.
DOMAIN: dynamical state estimation in power systems
STRUCTURE: dense linear algebra
DATA_OBJECT: sequence or time-series
INFERENCE: bayesian posterior
PROBLEM_FORM: estimation
DISTRIBUTION: continuous; gaussian
COMPLEXITY: closed-form
