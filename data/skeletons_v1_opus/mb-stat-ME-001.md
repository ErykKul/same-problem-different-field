MECHANISM: Several groups of dependent observations are sampled at fixed locations on a bounded planar lattice, and the goal is to test whether their underlying multivariate cumulative distribution functions are identical. Raw values are replaced by their ranks within the pooled sample, which yields a distribution-free representation under the null and removes sensitivity to the shape of the marginals. At a reference location, a locally weighted empirical cumulative distribution is formed by summing kernel weights, decaying with spatial distance, over indicator functions of the observations, then normalizing by the total weight. An effective local sample size is defined as the reciprocal of the sum of squared normalized weights, and the smoothed estimate is centered and scaled by its square root to form a normalized empirical process. A pooled estimate is built as a weighted average across groups, and per-group contrast processes are defined as scaled deviations from the pool. The test statistic is a quadratic form, a weighted discretized squared norm of the contrast processes integrated against the empirical measure. Under mixing conditions that bound the decay of dependence, the contrast process converges weakly to a mean-zero Gaussian limit, and the quadratic statistic converges to a weighted sum of chi-squared variables on a reduced contrast subspace. The mixture is approximated by a single scaled chi-squared by matching its first two moments using eigenvalues of the limiting covariance operator. The covariance is evaluated exactly on the finite grid under an assumed dependence model, enabling p-values without resampling.
