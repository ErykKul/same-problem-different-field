MECHANISM: Many binary units are observed simultaneously over repeated trials, each trial accompanied by a time-varying external covariate signal, and the goal is a probability law over joint binary configurations conditioned on that covariate. Among all conditional laws consistent with a chosen set of measured averages, the one of maximal entropy is selected; this least-structured law provably takes an exponential-family form whose log-probability is a sum of per-unit bias terms plus pairwise interaction terms between units, normalized by a configuration-sum partition function. The per-unit biases are not constant but are driven by the external covariate through a per-unit linear filter passed through a saturating nonlinearity, while the pairwise interaction terms are held constant across covariate values, so the model extends a single-unit covariate-to-response mapping to a coupled collection. The free parameters, the filters and the pairwise couplings, are fit by matching model-expected averages to the empirically measured covariate-conditioned activation rates and pairwise co-activation rates. Because the partition function over all joint configurations is intractable, expectations are estimated by stochastic sampling of configurations, and parameters are adjusted iteratively to reduce the discrepancy between model and data statistics. The fitted law is evaluated by how well it reproduces held-out per-unit response profiles and the empirical frequencies of entire joint configurations. Performance is contrasted against a reduced model that omits the pairwise interaction terms.
