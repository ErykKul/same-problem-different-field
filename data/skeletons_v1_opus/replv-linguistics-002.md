MECHANISM: The state is a vector of bounded fractions, one per interacting subpopulation, each giving the prevalence of one of two competing variants. A continuous-time system of coupled nonlinear ordinary differential equations governs the state: each variant's growth rate in a subpopulation is its frequency-dependent payoff relative to its competitor, modulated by transition terms that let each variant convert into the other at fixed per-direction rates. The payoff is deliberately nonlinear, combining a within-group reinforcement term proportional to the variant's own prevalence and a between-group contrast term proportional to its prevalence times the complementary prevalence in other groups, weighted by interaction-strength and alignment coefficients. To analyze long-run behavior the fixed points are obtained by setting all derivatives to zero, and their local stability is classified from the signs of the eigenvalues of the system's linearization (the Jacobian) evaluated at each fixed point. Imposing strong symmetry on the coefficient matrices collapses the free parameters to an alignment ratio and a single transition rate, allowing the fixed points, their count, and their stability to be solved in closed form and revealing critical transition-rate thresholds at which the number and type of fixed points change. For an asymmetric, higher-dimensional extension that resists closed-form treatment, the equations are integrated numerically from many random initial states across a grid of parameter values, and the fraction of trajectories settling into each qualitative fixed point is tabulated to determine which outcome is selected.
