MECHANISM: An outcome is modeled as a linear combination of many standardized predictors that are partitioned into groups by source. Coefficients are estimated by minimizing a goodness-of-fit term plus a penalty equal to a weighted sum of the absolute values of coefficients, where each group receives its own penalty weight; the absolute-value penalty drives many coefficients exactly to zero, yielding a sparse selection of predictors. The fit term is squared error for a continuous outcome, the negative log-likelihood for a binary outcome, or the negative partial likelihood for a censored time-to-event outcome, so the same scheme covers several response types. A key observation is that assigning a group its own penalty weight is equivalent to rescaling that group's predictors by the weight, running a single global-penalty solver, and rescaling the resulting coefficients back, so no special optimizer is needed. The relative group weights and the overall penalty strength are chosen jointly by repeated cross-validation: for each candidate weight vector the best global penalty is tuned by held-out predictive performance, and the weight vector with the best tuned score is kept, optionally subject to a cap on the number of selected predictors. The selected model is then evaluated on held-out data by predictive-error and discrimination measures. Behavior is characterized over many synthetic regimes that vary group sizes, fractions of truly relevant predictors per group, effect magnitudes, and correlation structure, and is compared against single-weight and group-structured alternatives.
DOMAIN: genomics, high-dimensional biomarker prediction
STRUCTURE: numerical optimization
