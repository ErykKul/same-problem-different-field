MECHANISM: Two coupled agents act in discrete time; one agent transforms an exogenous independent identically distributed driving sequence into an output stream via a one-sided linear filter with real coefficients summing to one, and the second agent forms a one-step-ahead forecast of that stream from its observed past. The total objective is a weighted sum of two scalar costs: the stationary standard deviation of one agent's accumulated state and the root mean squared one-step forecast error of the other, expressed in the transform domain by mapping the coefficient sequence to an analytic transfer function on the unit disk. The forecast-error cost is the geometric-mean (log-integral) functional of the output spectral density, and the state-variance cost is the energy of a related transfer function, so the problem becomes minimizing a functional over analytic functions in a Hardy space subject to a normalization constraint. The transfer function is factored into an outer (invertible) part and an inner all-pass part via canonical inner-outer factorization; the all-pass part preserves the spectral density and hence is invisible to the forecaster but inflates the state variance by exactly its phase-derivative-at-origin (a group-delay functional). Optimality is characterized in two regimes by the cost weight: a finite first-order filter when the weight is large, and a sequence approaching a transfer function with a nonrational singular inner factor when small. Bounds are proven relating the group delay to exponential decay of the forecast error and linear growth of the state variance. The nonrational optimum is then approximated by a family of invertible rational filters built from a power-limit representation that matches the group delay, and the relative cost gap is evaluated numerically as the approximation order grows.
