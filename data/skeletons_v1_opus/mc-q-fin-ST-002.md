MECHANISM: Given a multivariate stationary sequence, directional dependence between a designated source and target is characterized through the second-order geometry of feature vectors built from time-shifted source coordinates and contemporaneous target coordinates. For each admissible order-preserving shift in a fixed set, a joint second-moment matrix of the stacked features is estimated by averaging outer products over the sample, and a whitened cross-block operator is formed by pre- and post-multiplying the cross-covariance by inverse square roots of the marginal covariances. The eigenvalues of this symmetric positive-semidefinite operator are computed, and a scalar summary is obtained by averaging a chosen function of the eigenvalues, or alternatively the full empirical eigenvalue measure is retained. A directionality statistic is defined as the spread (supremum minus infimum, or a measure metric between the extreme spectra) of these spectral summaries across the shift set, so a nonzero spread signals sensitivity of dependence geometry to temporal re-alignment. Inference targets the null that the summary is constant over shifts; its sampling distribution is approximated by repeatedly recomputing the statistic after circular shifts of the source coordinate and by block resampling, exploiting an order-induced invariance. The estimators are shown to be uniformly consistent and pointwise asymptotically normal, with the resampling giving finite-sample-exact or asymptotically valid tests. The construction is applied in rolling windows to monitor evolving structure. The dominant computation is repeated eigendecomposition of whitened covariance operators with resampling-based testing.
