MECHANISM: The lowest eigenvalue of a large Hermitian operator describing interacting discrete modes is sought by variational minimization rather than direct diagonalization. The operator is first built in second-quantized form from one-body and two-body integrals over a chosen finite basis restricted to an active set of modes. A structure-preserving transformation rewrites each fermionic creation/annihilation term as a weighted sum of tensor-product strings of elementary unitaries, expanding a handful of physical terms into hundreds of such strings. A trial state is parameterized by a layered sequence of single-mode rotations and entangling operations applied to a reference state, with an optional projection onto the correct particle-number subspace. The objective is the expectation of the operator in the trial state, evaluated by summing contributions of the tensor-product terms. Its gradient with respect to each parameter is obtained exactly by evaluating the objective at two shifted parameter values and differencing. Parameters are updated by momentum-accelerated gradient descent with a decaying step size until the gradient norm and energy change fall below thresholds. The converged state yields the energy and, via its one-mode reduced density matrix, natural occupations and charge-transfer quantities through diagonalization. Convergence is post-hoc decomposed into exponential, power-law, and slow-asymptotic phases by nonlinear least-squares fits, and resource scaling exponents are estimated empirically against system size. Downstream scalar predictions are obtained from energy differences plus auxiliary correction terms.
