MECHANISM: The goal is to select, from a large redundant collection of candidate explanatory variables, the small subset that carries genuine incremental association with a cross-sectional outcome, and to estimate their effect sizes with valid inference despite high dimensionality and confounding. A first stage regresses the mean outcome across units on the full block of established control variables using an L1-penalized least-squares fit, retaining controls with nonzero coefficients as drivers of the outcome. A second stage regresses, in turn, each candidate test variable on the same block of controls with another L1-penalized fit, retaining controls predictive of the candidate to guard against omitted-variable bias. The union of variables selected in the two stages is then used as the conditioning set in a final ordinary least-squares regression of the outcome on the controls plus the candidates, where heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors yield test statistics for each candidate's incremental coefficient. Inputs are formed as standardized sample covariances between unit-level outcomes and variable series, so coefficients read as effect per standardized exposure. Auxiliary validation aggregates higher-frequency series to a coarser horizon, computes serial-correlation-robust statistics over overlapping windows, and tests monotonic growth of significance with horizon and equivalence of two aggregation schemes by paired tests. Robustness re-runs the selection under finer partitions and against alternative penalized and dimension-reduction estimators. The double-selection structure is what restores consistency and asymptotically valid post-selection inference for the retained effects. The output is a selected variable set with signed, tested effect estimates.
