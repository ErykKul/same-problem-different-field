MECHANISM: A collection of observed entities, each represented as a vector of continuous measurements in a low-dimensional space, is modeled as a weighted superposition of several multivariate components, each component being an elliptical density specified by a center vector and a covariance matrix. The model parameters (component weights, centers, and covariances) are estimated by an iterative two-step procedure that alternates between computing soft responsibilities of each component for each observation and re-estimating the weighted means, weighted covariances, and weights from those responsibilities. The iteration repeats until the product of per-observation mixture densities (the total data likelihood) stops increasing, and the procedure is restarted from many random initializations to seek the highest-likelihood solution. The number of components is selected by progressively increasing it and testing goodness-of-fit with a multivariate maximum-discrepancy test between the empirical and modeled cumulative distributions, whose significance threshold is obtained by repeated random resampling. Robustness of the fitted parameters is assessed by refitting on resampled-with-replacement copies of the data. Once fitted, the components are partitioned into a target subset and a complementary subset, and for any entity a log-ratio of the summed target-component densities to the summed complementary-component densities is formed. This log-ratio is the most-powerful statistic for deciding membership against a threshold, and entities are also ranked by it. A linear boundary in the transformed coordinates is then derived as a closed-form approximate decision rule, and the ranking is validated against a held-out labeled subset.
