MECHANISM: An observed nonnegative data matrix whose rows lie on the unit simplex is approximated by a product of two nonnegative matrices, one of mixing weights and one of basis profiles, each constrained to have rows summing to one, so every observation is a convex combination of a few basis profiles. Because such a factorization is not unique, an extra criterion is imposed: among admissible factorizations, the one whose basis profiles are as spread out as possible is selected by maximizing the determinant of the basis matrix times its transpose, a proxy for the enclosed volume. A sufficient-scatter geometric condition on the basis is shown, via cone-duality and a determinant inequality, to make the maximizer unique up to relabeling, which is proved as a theorem. Computationally the volume term is added as a regularizer with negative sign to the squared reconstruction error, and the resulting nonconvex objective is minimized by alternating between the two matrices, holding one fixed while updating the other. Each block update decomposes into independent per-row subproblems that are quadratic programs with nonnegativity and sum-to-one constraints; the volume contribution to each basis-row subproblem is reduced to a quadratic form using a null-space projection of the remaining rows. Subproblems are solved by a projected accelerated first-order method, and the regularization weight is auto-scaled by the initial fit-to-volume ratio. Sign of the weight toggles between favoring spread-out and tightly-enclosing bases.
