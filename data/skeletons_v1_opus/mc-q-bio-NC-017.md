MECHANISM: The work formalizes a three-level ordering of how a system maps a time-varying input to an output and instantiates each level as a concrete low-order dynamical system that is integrated forward and then characterized. The first level is a memoryless map: output equals a time-varying gain times the input plus zero-mean noise, where the gain evolves only from an exogenous binary square-wave signal independent of the input. The second level applies a fixed nonlinear transformation operator to the input whose instantaneous value depends on an internal state variable governed by an ordinary differential equation with a driving term proportional to the input over a state-dependent factor and a linear relaxation term. The third level makes the transformation itself a state updated by a recurrence: slow auxiliary variables modulate the input amplitude and bias, with their derivatives driven by the gap between a running exponentially weighted mean and variance of the internal state and fixed target values, on a timescale much slower than the inner state. Each model is simulated under periodic and frequency-swept driving. Memory is assessed by plotting input-output trajectories and detecting hysteresis loops and whether the loop drifts over successive cycles. Adaptivity is probed by fitting a local linear input-output model over short sliding windows to extract a time-varying slope and offset, and by extracting successive zero-crossing instants of input and output and tracking the drift of their lag. Constant slope, no loop, and constant lag indicate the lowest level; drifting slope, drifting loop, and growing lag indicate the self-modulating level.
