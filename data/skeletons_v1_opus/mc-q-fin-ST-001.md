MECHANISM: Multiple parallel sequences are transformed into a dependency network and analyzed. Each sequence is first detrended against a common reference sequence by fitting a linear regression and keeping the residual, isolating sequence-specific variation. For every pair of residual sequences a dependency strength is estimated by a histogram-based information-theoretic measure of shared variation. The significance of each pairwise value is assessed by a permutation procedure that repeatedly shuffles one sequence, recomputes the measure to build a null distribution, and derives an empirical tail probability; pairs failing the threshold are zeroed. Surviving strengths are converted to distances by a decreasing transform, and a minimum-total-weight spanning tree is extracted from the largest connected component by a greedy edge-adding algorithm, yielding a sparse backbone. A battery of graph descriptors is then computed on this tree, including shortest-path lengths, several centrality scores, average path length and efficiency, a degree-mixing correlation coefficient, and the second-smallest eigenvalue of the graph Laplacian, plus core-versus-periphery concentration ratios. Separately, regime changes are detected by a rolling distributional-dissimilarity statistic between a windowed reference histogram and the current cross-section, thresholded at mean-plus-multiple-standard-deviations, and characterized by an empirical-mode time-frequency-energy decomposition. The pipeline is repeated per period to compare structural reorganization.
DOMAIN: quantitative finance, financial market networks
STRUCTURE: graph traversal
