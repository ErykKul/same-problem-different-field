MECHANISM: The task is to select, from a very large ordered set of candidate predictors, the few associated with a response, in a setting where predictors are far more numerous than observations and adjacent predictors are correlated and expected to have similar effect magnitudes. A penalized objective is built from a sum of per-predictor squared-error fits plus two penalty terms: a sparsity-inducing absolute-value penalty on each coefficient, and a smoothing penalty that, over a sliding window of consecutive predictors, penalizes weighted squared differences of coefficient magnitudes, with weights given by the correlation strength between predictor pairs so that only strongly correlated neighbors are coupled. The penalty is invariant to arbitrary sign-flips of individual predictors. The objective is minimized by cyclic coordinate descent: holding all other coefficients fixed, each coefficient's subproblem reduces to a univariate quadratic-plus-absolute-value form with a closed-form soft-threshold-type update, and cycling is iterated to convergence to a stationary point. For a binary response, the same machinery is applied through an iteratively reweighted quadratic approximation of the log-likelihood. The window size is chosen from the average lagged correlation among predictors, and the two penalty strengths are tuned by a bisection search targeting a desired number of selected predictors. Performance is assessed on simulated data by precision and recall against the planted nonzero predictors and demonstrated on real data.
