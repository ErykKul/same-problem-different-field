MECHANISM: A dissimilarity between two nonnegative weight vectors that each sum to one is defined as the minimum total cost of redistributing all the mass of the first onto the second, where moving a unit of mass between two coordinates incurs a fixed given pairwise ground cost. Computed exactly, this is a constrained linear minimization over the set of nonnegative coupling matrices whose row sums and column sums equal the two input vectors, and its cost grows steeply with vector dimension. The work replaces this with a smoothed variant: an entropy term of the coupling matrix is subtracted from the linear transport objective, scaled by a positive regularization weight, biasing the solution toward higher-entropy couplings. The unique minimizer of this strictly convex regularized problem has a closed structural form, namely a fixed kernel matrix obtained by exponentiating the negated ground cost, rescaled on the left and right by two unknown positive diagonal vectors. Those two scaling vectors are found by a fixed-point iteration that alternately rescales rows so their sums match the first input and rescales columns so their sums match the second, repeated to convergence; each iteration is only matrix-vector products and elementwise divisions, giving order-of-magnitude speedups and natural parallel and batched evaluation. The resulting regularized cost is shown to retain metric-like properties and, with a bound on the coupling's entropy, defines a proper distance. The smoothed distances are evaluated as inputs to a nearest-neighbor classification benchmark, where they improve accuracy over the unregularized version.
