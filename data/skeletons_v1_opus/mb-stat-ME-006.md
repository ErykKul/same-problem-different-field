MECHANISM: An instantaneous failure rate for a population of interest is written as an additive sum of several non-negative component rates, separating an intrinsic cause, a baseline differential relative to a reference population, an induced differential, and the reference population's own rate. A standard adjustment procedure subtracts only the reference component, so the quantity it actually recovers is the sum of the remaining three components rather than the intrinsic component alone; the two coincide exactly only when both differential components vanish. A scalar ratio of the total non-intrinsic rate to the reference rate is derived as a one-number diagnostic, equal to one precisely when the differentials are absent and exceeding one otherwise, signalling and quantifying the gap. Survival-type probabilities for each target quantity are obtained in closed form as the exponential of the negative time-integral of the corresponding summed rate. Using a parametric intrinsic rate with increasing hazard and a constant reference rate, the divergence between the adjusted quantity and each intended target is computed exactly across a grid of ratio values and three allocation splits of the differential between its two sources. The resulting curves show the gap is zero at unit ratio, grows with the ratio, and is non-monotone over follow-up time, peaking at intermediate horizons. The construction is purely deterministic and analytic, requiring no individual-level simulation, since the divergence follows directly from the additive decomposition.
