MECHANISM: A coupled set of partial differential equations governs the evolution of one or more interacting scalar fields over a two-dimensional spatial region, where each field changes through a diffusive (second-derivative) transport term plus a nonlinear local interaction term. The continuous problem is solved on a regular grid by a mesoscopic update rule: at each node a set of discrete-direction population variables is stored, and each step alternates a local relaxation of these populations toward an equilibrium value with a propagation of populations to neighboring nodes, while a source term injects the local interaction. The macroscopic field at each node is recovered as the sum of its population variables, and a parameter maps a relaxation constant to the effective diffusion coefficient. A multiscale asymptotic expansion in a small parameter is carried out to high order to derive the leading truncation error, yielding a closed-form polynomial in the relaxation parameter whose minimum locates the optimal coefficient. Separately, spatially uniform steady states are found by solving the algebraic fixed-point equations, and their stability is assessed by linearizing the dynamics, forming the Jacobian matrix, and examining the sign of the real parts of its eigenvalues. Adding a spatial perturbation mode introduces a wavenumber-dependent matrix whose determinant changes sign to define an instability band over a range of wavenumbers. The discrete simulation is validated against an exact analytical solution obtained by transforming a single linear equation, using a relative error norm summed over all nodes. The analysis also shows that uniformly rescaling all diffusion coefficients changes the characteristic length scale while leaving the dimensionless pattern shape invariant.
