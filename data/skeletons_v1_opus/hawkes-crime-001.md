MECHANISM: A sequence of events is observed as points in time and space, and the process is described by an instantaneous occurrence-rate function that depends on the entire history of prior events. This rate is decomposed additively into a constant baseline component that varies smoothly over space and a sum of contributions from every earlier event, where each past event adds a decaying influence determined by a triggering kernel in elapsed time and spatial separation. The model is self-reinforcing: each occurrence transiently raises the rate, making nearby subsequent occurrences more likely, so events arrive in clusters of a parent followed by descendants. Fitting treats the unobserved parent-or-baseline origin of each event as a latent assignment and proceeds by an iterative two-step scheme. In the first step, given current rate estimates, each event is probabilistically attributed either to the baseline or to a specific earlier triggering event, yielding responsibility weights. In the second step these weights are used to re-estimate the baseline component and the triggering kernel without assuming a fixed parametric shape, by weighted smoothing of the inter-event time and space separations. Iterating this attribution-and-reestimation converges to a self-consistent decomposition that thins the clustered sequence into an approximately structureless residual background. The recovered baseline maps the underlying rate while the kernel quantifies how strongly and how long one event elevates the chance of another. The fitted intensity supports short-horizon forecasts of elevated risk.
