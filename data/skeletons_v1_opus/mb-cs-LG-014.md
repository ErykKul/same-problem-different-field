MECHANISM: A scalar quality outcome is produced by a stochastic fitting procedure whose only varied input is a random seed, and the study quantifies how the choice of a constant offset term inside the objective affects the run-to-run dispersion of that outcome. First, two variants of the objective are written in closed form: one adds the offset to a bounded similarity and saturates it at a ceiling, the other subtracts the offset after a log-normalization. A short differentiation argument proves the second variant only shifts each per-anchor term by a parameter-independent constant, so its gradient with respect to all parameters is identical to the no-offset case, establishing it as a neutral reference. The first variant, by contrast, has a piecewise derivative that is zeroed wherever the similarity exceeds the ceiling, producing data-dependent gradient truncation. The procedure is then run many independent times per condition in a two-factor crossed design, and for each condition the sample variance of the outcome across seeds is computed. The ratio of variances between the two offset variants is the primary statistic, with equality of variances tested by an F-test and rank-based dispersion tests, equality of means by a Welch test, and a resampling scheme producing a confidence interval for the variance ratio. Diagnostic quantities (per-layer saturation-activation rate and per-layer gradient norms) are averaged to link the dispersion difference to gradient truncation. The whole audit is repeated across several datasets and an offset-magnitude sweep to map when the dispersion inflation appears.
