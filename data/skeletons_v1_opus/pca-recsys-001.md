MECHANISM: Sparse pairwise interaction values between two index sets are modeled by assigning each index a low-dimensional latent vector, with each observed value approximated by the inner product of the corresponding two vectors, optionally plus per-index offset terms and a global offset. A point-estimation baseline minimizes squared reconstruction error over only the observed entries with a quadratic norm penalty on the vectors, solved by gradient descent and interpretable as a maximum-a-posteriori estimate. To mitigate overfitting under extreme sparsity, the latent vectors and offsets are treated as random with Gaussian priors, and a likelihood is placed over the observed entries as Gaussian around the predicted value, defining a joint density whose normalizing evidence integral is intractable. Approximate posterior inference is performed by variational optimization: a tractable factorized family is fit to the posterior by minimizing the divergence to it, equivalently maximizing an evidence lower bound that contrasts an expected-log-joint term with an entropy term. Predictions for unobserved pairs are formed as posterior-expected inner products by integrating the predictive density against the approximate posterior, drawing samples to evaluate the expectation. An extended variant folds additive bias terms into the same prior-likelihood-inference pipeline. The procedure is iterated until the fitted approximating density and the drawn parameter samples stabilize, and reconstruction accuracy on held-out entries is used for evaluation. The core computation is low-rank factorization of a sparse interaction array under a probabilistic model solved by variational posterior approximation.
