MECHANISM: A time-stamped graph of entities and links is split at a cutoff time chosen so a fixed fraction of links predates it. For every node the connection count up to the cutoff is computed, and separately the number of new links the node acquires after the cutoff. The relationship between accrual rate and prior connectivity is posited to follow a shifted power law: the expected new-link count is proportional to one plus the prior count raised to an exponent, with an additive offset for regularization and a multiplicative scale that is left unidentified. Because prior counts span many orders of magnitude, the exponent is estimated by least squares on log-transformed quantities: minimize over the scale and exponent the sum of squared differences between the log of the offset prior-count term and the log of the offset new-link count, summed over all nodes. This yields a closed-form linear fit in log space whose slope is the reported exponent, distinguishing sub-proportional from super-proportional accrual against the proportional baseline. A logarithmic root-mean-square residual is defined as the geometric average factor by which predictions miss, bounded below by one. The exponent is computed across many such temporal graphs spanning several categories, and an independent robust estimate of the tail exponent of the connectivity distribution is also obtained. To probe the super-proportional regime, the ratio of the log of the maximum connectivity to the log of the node count is plotted against the fitted exponent to test for emergence of a single dominating node.
