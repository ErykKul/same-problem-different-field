MECHANISM: A pre-trained generative model defines, for each input, a conditional and an unconditional density over a high-dimensional continuous output, both expressible through the expected reconstruction error of a corrupting-noise channel at varying noise scales. The quantity of interest is the difference of the two log-densities, an information measure between a discrete label and a generated continuous object, written as an integral over noise scales of the squared difference between the conditional and unconditional reconstruction predictions. The goal is to adjust the parameters of a second copy of the model so that this information measure is driven toward zero for objects it generates, while staying as close as possible to the original model on everything else. The author derives the gradient of the per-scale information term with respect to the adjustable parameters and shows it factors into three pieces: a difference of fixed-model predictions, a fixed-model sensitivity matrix, and the gradient of the adjustable model's prediction. The expensive sensitivity matrix is dropped, leaving an approximate gradient that is shown to equal the gradient of a divergence between the conditional and unconditional output distributions. To preserve unrelated behavior, the target is characterized as the family of label-independent distributions, and the closest such distribution is identified as the original unconditional one. The final training objective therefore aligns the adjustable model's conditional reconstruction prediction with the fixed model's unconditional prediction, minimized by stochastic gradient steps over corrupted samples. This is iterated as a fine-tuning loop with a fixed model acting as a discriminator-like reference.
