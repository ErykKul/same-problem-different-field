MECHANISM: A vector-valued continuous-time random field is constructed so that each coordinate is a stationary Gaussian process with a memory-controlling exponent and an amplitude (intermittency) parameter, and the cross-dependence between coordinates is governed by matrices of pairwise exponents and pairwise amplitudes. Each coordinate is built as an integral of an inhomogeneous Gaussian white noise over a time-scale domain, with a prescribed scale-dependent covariance measure; closed-form expressions for the within- and cross-coordinate autocovariance functions are derived, and the construction is shown to be well-defined for exponent-matrix entries in a bounded range. A nonnegative random measure is then formed coordinate-wise by exponentiating the Gaussian field, and a limit theorem establishes weak convergence to a multiplicative-cascade measure as the exponents vanish, unifying two regimes. For estimation, a small-amplitude expansion shows that the cross-covariance of log-measures over intervals matches, to leading order, a known closed-form expression in the pairwise exponent and amplitude. Empirical autocovariances of the observed sampled series are computed across a set of lags, and the unknown pairwise parameters are estimated by minimizing a quadratic-form discrepancy (weighted by a positive-definite matrix) between the empirical lag curve and the theoretical closed-form curve, a moment-matching minimum-distance procedure; diagonal parameters are obtained per coordinate by the one-dimensional analogue. The estimator's empirical covariances are proven to converge in probability to their theoretical limits and the parameter estimates are proven weakly consistent. The procedure is validated on simulated paths and applied to real multi-asset data.
