MECHANISM: An agent that selects actions from observed states to maximize accumulated reward is represented by a sequence model that conditions on past states, actions, and a target cumulative-reward token and autoregressively predicts the next action. Starting from a model pretrained by imitation on logged sequences, it is improved by interacting with the environment and adjusting its parameters using reward signals rather than imitation. A previously standard step that overwrites the target-reward tokens with realized returns is identified as incompatible with ratio-based policy updates, because it breaks the correspondence between the distribution actions were sampled from and the distribution they are scored against, so it is removed. For improvement, the current policy collects full rollouts; from each, several restart points are chosen and, from each restart, a group of short continuations is sampled. Each short continuation is scored by its discounted reward, optionally extended by deterministic-action lookahead or evaluated by an auxiliary value function, and a relative advantage is formed by standardizing scores within the group against their own mean and spread. The policy is updated by maximizing a clipped objective using the ratio of new to old probabilities computed over whole sub-sequences, with a divergence penalty to a reference and an entropy floor to maintain exploration. Restart points are sampled preferentially toward states where the action distribution is most uncertain. The procedure is benchmarked against alternatives and ablated over sub-sequence length, restart consistency, ratio granularity, and uncertainty-based selection.
