MECHANISM: A high-dimensional latent multi-way array is compressed into a low-complexity representation that is sent to an external noisy operator, whose response is then used to refine the array, repeating in a cycle. Compression is posed as minimizing expected reconstruction distortion subject to a budget on representation cost, written equivalently as a penalized objective, which connects to the classical cost-versus-fidelity trade-off. The encoder factorizes each unfolding of the array by singular decomposition and retains only directions whose singular values exceed a fixed fraction of the leading one, equivalent to an orthogonal projection onto the dominant subspace per mode. Under a quadratic distortion criterion, keeping the leading singular directions is proved optimal, both in the matrix case and mode-wise, and a tail bound expresses the residual error as the sum of discarded squared singular values across modes. The budget proxy is the product of retained per-mode ranks. The refinement objective is the expected task loss after integrating the operator's response, with the response modeled as a true value plus zero-mean bounded-variance noise, optimized over a fixed-rank manifold and optionally penalized by the budget. Updates take a step along the negative stochastic gradient and map back onto the manifold by a retraction. Under smoothness and bounded-variance assumptions with diminishing nonsummable-square step sizes, the gradient norm is shown to vanish in the limit and the iterates approach stationary points.
