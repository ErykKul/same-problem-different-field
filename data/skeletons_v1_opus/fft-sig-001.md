MECHANISM: A nonnegative continuous function defined on a bounded interval is estimated from one observed sequence. The unknown function is represented as a finite weighted mixture of locally supported basis functions of fixed degree, with both the mixture weights and the basis-knot locations treated as unknown and given by two independent random distributions drawn from a nonparametric prior. A precision-times-base construction (stick-breaking) turns each random distribution into an infinite weighted sum of point masses, truncated to a finite number of components for computation. The number of mixture components carries a discrete prior, so model complexity is learned from the data. An approximate likelihood is formed by transforming the sequence into the frequency domain, taking squared moduli to get a periodogram, and combining a log term and a ratio term across frequencies; this likelihood is multiplied by the prior to give an unnormalized posterior. Posterior draws are obtained by iterating component-wise stochastic accept-reject updates with symmetric proposals on the unit interval, direct conjugate updates for a scale parameter, and a mixed local/heavy-tailed proposal for the discrete component count. To traverse a multimodal target, several replicas at different temperature exponents are run in parallel and occasionally swap states by an accept-reject criterion. Posterior summaries (pointwise medians and simultaneous deviation bands) are computed from retained draws and compared to a known target via an integrated absolute-error norm and coverage frequencies.
DOMAIN: signal processing, time-series spectral estimation
STRUCTURE: MCMC sampling
DATA_OBJECT: continuous function or field
INFERENCE: bayesian posterior
PROBLEM_FORM: estimation
DISTRIBUTION: continuous; nonparametric
