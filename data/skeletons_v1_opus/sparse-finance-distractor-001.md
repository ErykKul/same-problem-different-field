MECHANISM: A scalar future quantity is modelled as an unknown nonlinear function of a vector of contemporaneous predictor variables, approximated by layered compositions of affine maps and elementwise nonlinearities. Both purely feedforward stacks and a gated recurrent variant, which carries a hidden state and a memory cell updated through input, forget, and output gates to encode temporal dependence, are fitted by minimizing a robust regression loss with a first-order adaptive gradient method, using dropout and weight decay for regularization and averaging an ensemble of randomly initialized fits. Hyperparameters are tuned on a held-out validation split that preserves temporal order, refit on a rolling window each decision period. Each predictor is also modelled by a first-order autoregressive recursion, and the innovation covariance is estimated from residuals. A decision-maker then chooses a one-dimensional allocation weight to maximize the expectation of a constant-relative-risk-aversion utility of compounded multi-step return; this expectation is evaluated by drawing a large number of joint Gaussian innovation paths, propagating predictors and the predicted quantity forward, computing realized utility, and averaging, with the optimum found by search over a weight grid. Out-of-sample predictive accuracy is summarized by squared-error skill relative to a historical mean with a significance test, and decision quality by a certainty-equivalent and a risk-adjusted ratio compared across regimes via paired tests. Predictor relevance is gauged by the performance loss from omitting each input and by nonparametric serial-dependence measures.
