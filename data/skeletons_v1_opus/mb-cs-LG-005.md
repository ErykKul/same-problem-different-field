MECHANISM: The goal is to learn a generator over high-dimensional discrete objects that concentrates mass on a rare region where an unknown noisy scalar response exceeds a threshold, given only a small budget of expensive queries. A generative process is defined that transforms a simple source distribution into a target by letting probability mass flow continuously along a time-indexed path while states stay discrete, governed by a continuous-time jump process whose rate is set by a learned conditional endpoint predictor. The endpoint predictor is a parameterized function trained by minimizing expected cross-entropy to reconstruct a clean object from a partially corrupted intermediate state sampled along an interpolating path. Because the marginal likelihood of the generator requires summing over exponentially many trajectories and is intractable, variational objectives are reformulated to act on the tractable conditional endpoint distributions instead of the marginal. Both a forward and a reverse divergence between the model and a tilted reference (reference generator times a classifier of threshold-exceedance) are derived, plus their sum. Each objective is estimated by self-normalized importance sampling, drawing candidates from a proposal and weighting them by the ratio of the classifier-tilted target to the proposal density. A three-component mixture proposal blends the source prior, the previous-round generator, and a fitness-weighted replay buffer of confirmed high-response objects, with per-component weight simplifications. The whole loop alternates proposing batches, querying the response, updating the classifier, and re-fitting the generator over rounds. A consistency argument shows the forward variant reduces to standard fitting against the tilted target, recovering the correct terminal distribution. Performance is the rarity-region objective reached under a fixed query budget.
