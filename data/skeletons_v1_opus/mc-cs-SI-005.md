MECHANISM: A preference structure is represented as a complete bipartite graph in which one node class holds agents and the other holds options, with edge attributes giving each agent's graded score for each option. A parametric message-passing network operates on this graph: at each layer, edge latents are updated from their endpoint node latents and current edge latent, and node latents are updated from a permutation-invariant aggregation of neighbor messages, with all sub-operations realized as small parametric maps. After several layers, option nodes are masked and passed through a normalized exponential to yield a probability distribution over options, which by construction is invariant to relabeling agents and equivariant to relabeling options. The model is trained to maximize an expected aggregate-value objective, defined as the inner product of the option distribution with a chosen aggregation of agents' true latent values, by minimizing its negative; an auxiliary penalty enforces a monotonicity constraint via a second backpropagation through the gradient. A second parametric network maps true latent values to manipulated per-agent scores and is trained to maximize each agent's own expected value, with gradients restricted to that agent's actions and the first network frozen. The two networks are trained jointly in an alternating adversarial scheme so the decision network adapts to manipulated inputs. Synthetic inputs are drawn from a simplex distribution and a spatial distance model, and real datasets are also used. Evaluation reports decision accuracy against reference rules and achieved aggregate value under varying manipulation. The dominant computation is gradient-based adversarial training of message-passing networks on a graph.
