MECHANISM: A system of coupled second-order partial differential equations on an unbounded spatial domain governs the time evolution of several interacting nonnegative quantity-fields, each combining a diffusion term with a local nonlinear interaction term of logistic-competitive type plus cross-coupling. The fields start from spatially localized (compactly supported) initial data on top of a uniform background and the goal is to characterize how the support of one field invades the region occupied by another as time grows. The analysis first reduces the parameter count by rescaling, then classifies the spatially homogeneous steady states of the associated ordinary differential system and determines their stability as a function of a key parameter. Asymptotic propagation is quantified through a spreading speed defined as the supremum of velocities along which the front persists, shown to equal the larger of two parenthesized rates derived from linearization at the leading edge. Upper bounds on propagation are obtained by constructing exponentially decaying super-solutions and invoking a comparison argument; lower bounds in the interaction zone use a limiting argument and a strict Lyapunov function for the reduced two-field system. A logarithmic-in-time correction to the front position is established by a refined super- and sub-solution construction adapted to the coupled system, since the standard scalar argument fails. The work proves rigorous limit statements (liminf/limsup over moving spatial windows) confirming four qualitatively distinct propagation regimes previously seen only numerically. The central difficulty is the absence of a comparison principle for the full coupled system, handled by isolating monotone sub-blocks.
