MECHANISM: A finite population of entities is arranged on two overlapping weighted networks, and each entity simultaneously carries a binary trait on each network that evolves by an imitation-style stochastic update. On the first network each entity's payoff is a linear function of its own trait and the trait of its neighbors weighted by normalized edge weights; on the second network each trait carries a fixed type-dependent fitness independent of neighbors. Total payoff sums the two contributions, and a small selection strength turns payoff into a reproductive weight. At each step a focal node is chosen and one of its neighbors replaces its trait with probability proportional to that neighbor's weight, defining an absorbing Markov chain that ends when each network is uniform. The central quantity computed is the probability that a single introduced variant eventually takes over, evaluated to first order in the selection strength. This fixation probability is expressed in closed form through coefficients that are expectations over random walks on the networks: products of multi-step transition matrices weighted by the stationary distribution, including walks that traverse one network and then the other. Comparing the variant's fixation probability to the neutral baseline yields an explicit threshold condition on the payoff ratio that determines whether the prosocial trait is favored. The condition is enumerated exhaustively over all small networks and all single-variant initial states, then evaluated on random-graph ensembles and on empirical networks, with direct stochastic simulation used to confirm the analytic thresholds.
