MECHANISM: A continuous-time dynamical system is described by a set of coupled nonlinear ordinary differential equations whose right-hand side is bilinear in inputs and states and contains saturating, non-monotonic rate terms with uncertain parameters. At each discrete control step, a finite-horizon constrained nonlinear program is solved to choose an input sequence that minimizes a weighted quadratic deviation of predicted outputs from a reference trajectory, subject to the state-evolution equations and box constraints on states, inputs, and input increments. To handle bounded disturbances, the problem is split into a nominal undisturbed subproblem and an ancillary subproblem: the nominal subproblem optimizes an idealized trajectory under tightened constraints, and the ancillary subproblem penalizes the squared distance of the true disturbed trajectory from the nominal one while enforcing the real dynamics from the current measured state. An extra decision variable lets the nominal initial state be re-optimized and fed back across steps, injecting a degree of closed-loop correction. The receding-horizon loop applies only the first input, advances one step, and re-solves. Stability and the size of the invariant tube around the nominal trajectory are assessed empirically by repeated forward simulations under many random parameter draws. Trajectory-corridor width and tracking error are summarized as means and maxima over the replicates. Solver-level relaxation via penalized slack variables is compared against the tube tightening.
