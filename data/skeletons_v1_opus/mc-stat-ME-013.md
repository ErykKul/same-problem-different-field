MECHANISM: An entity is observed repeatedly across several ordered occasions, and each observation is modeled as a sum of a time-invariant latent component, a first-order autoregressive latent component with a single persistence coefficient, and an occasion-specific noise term, all mutually uncorrelated. Expanding the recursion expresses the autoregressive part as a weighted combination of an initial term and per-occasion residuals, yielding a structured covariance matrix that is a closed-form function of a handful of variance and coefficient parameters. To avoid the boundary and improper estimates that plague likelihood fitting of this nearly-singular structure, the model is recast in a data-space factor form where the observation matrix equals a common-factor-score matrix times a loading matrix plus a unique-factor matrix times a diagonal scale, with factor scores treated as additional parameters constrained to be orthonormal. A two-stage iterative scheme alternates: factor-data cross-covariances are recomputed from current parameters via an eigenvalue decomposition of a small symmetric matrix, the variance and noise parameters are read off directly by averaging the relevant cross-covariance blocks, and the remaining autoregressive parameters are obtained by minimizing an unweighted least-squares discrepancy between the structured within-entity covariance and a covariance assembled from a triangular submatrix of the cross-covariances. Because the joint objective is non-convex and non-monotone, early stopping with patience and multiple random restarts select the lowest-loss solution. Because parameters enter through the eigen-decomposition, negative variances cannot arise, suppressing improper solutions; performance is assessed by simulation on improper-solution frequency, bias, and root-mean-squared error.
