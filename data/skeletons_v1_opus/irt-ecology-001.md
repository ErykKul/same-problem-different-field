MECHANISM: A partially observed binary latent state at each spatial location is modeled as a Bernoulli draw whose probability is a logit-linear function of covariates, where selected regression coefficients are allowed to vary smoothly across space. Each spatially-varying coefficient is treated as a realization of a zero-mean Gaussian random field with a parametric distance-decay covariance. To avoid cubic cost from inverting dense covariance matrices, the joint field density is rewritten as a product of univariate conditionals, each conditioned only on a small fixed set of nearest predecessors, yielding a sparse-precision approximation governed by local covariance solves. Observations are conditioned on the latent state through a second logit-linear sub-model with no false positives. For many simultaneous entities, the per-entity spatial surfaces are reduced via a low-rank factor decomposition into a few shared latent fields times entity-specific loadings, with identifiability constraints on the loading matrix. Estimation is fully Bayesian: priors are placed on coefficients, variances, decay, and loadings. An auxiliary-variable augmentation transforms the logit likelihood into a conditionally Gaussian form, enabling closed-form conditional updates for all parameters except the decay, which is sampled by a random-walk step. Posterior draws are produced by iterating these conditional updates, and predictions at new locations are generated by conditional simulation followed by inverse-logit mapping. Model fit and out-of-sample discrimination are assessed by information criteria and cross-validated area-under-curve.
