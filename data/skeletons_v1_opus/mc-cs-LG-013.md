MECHANISM: Given paired observations of inputs and a scalar response, the goal is to decide whether the relationship is distorted by an unobserved common influence, without assuming a parametric form. Inputs are mapped through a fixed nonlinear feature map into an inner-product function space so that a flexible response function becomes linear in the features. Two regularized least-squares fits of the response on the mapped inputs are computed: a standard one minimizing expected squared error, and a second one that reweights each term by the squared magnitude of the input, emphasizing higher-order moment structure. A representation argument confines each solution to a fixed finite-dimensional subspace spanned by selected basis functions, so each fit reduces to solving a finite linear system, giving closed-form coefficient vectors involving a Gram-type matrix, its product or a diagonally reweighted version, plus ridge regularization. The core claim is that, in the infinite-sample limit, the two coefficient vectors coincide coordinate-by-coordinate exactly when no hidden common influence is present, and differ otherwise; this is established by characterization. For finite samples the empirical difference of the two coefficient vectors is shown to be asymptotically zero-mean Gaussian with a consistently estimable covariance. A standardized per-coordinate statistic is then formed and compared to a normal reference, with a multiplicity correction across coordinates, rejecting the no-hidden-influence null if any coordinate deviates significantly. Overall cost scales polynomially in sample size and subspace dimension.
