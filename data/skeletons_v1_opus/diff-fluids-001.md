MECHANISM: A scalar quantity evolving on a one-dimensional periodic domain is governed by a partial differential equation combining a quadratic nonlinear transport term and a second-derivative dissipative term. The continuous operator is reduced to a system of coupled ordinary differential equations by approximating spatial derivatives on a uniform grid with high-order compact implicit stencils, each requiring the solution of a tridiagonal system. The semi-discrete system is advanced in pseudo-time with a multi-stage explicit total-variation-diminishing integrator. After each time step an auxiliary parabolic smoothing operator is applied for a single iteration: it adds gradient-dependent dissipation through a discretized diffusion update in which the local conductivity is a decreasing function of the local first derivative magnitude. Several alternative conductivity kernels (rational and exponential forms) are compared, each parameterized by a scaling constant and a step-ratio constant that jointly control the amount of injected dissipation. Random initial fields are synthesized in transform space from a prescribed energy distribution with uniformly random phases satisfying a conjugate-symmetry constraint, then mapped to physical space by an inverse fast transform. An ensemble of such realizations is advanced and the second-moment energy distribution across transform modes is averaged. Coarse-resolution runs are compared against a highly resolved reference solution, and a parameter sweep quantifies sensitivity of the modal energy distribution to the free constants.
