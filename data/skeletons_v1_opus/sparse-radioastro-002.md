MECHANISM: A two-dimensional field discretized on a uniform grid is to be recovered from a number of noisy linear measurements that is far smaller than the number of grid unknowns, where each measurement samples one coefficient of the field's transform under a known incomplete, masked sampling operator, giving an underdetermined ill-posed linear system. The recovery exploits that the field has a sparse or compressible representation in some chosen basis, meaning only a few representation coefficients are significant. The forward operator is the composition of the sampling mask, the transform, and an optional diagonal weighting, and a data-fidelity term measures the squared, noise-variance-normalized residual between predicted and observed measurements, which under an assumed additive noise model follows a known tail used to set a residual tolerance. Reconstruction solves a global convex program: minimize the sum of absolute values of the representation coefficients subject to the residual norm staying within the tolerance, equivalently an unconstrained sum of data-fidelity plus a weighted sparsity penalty. This penalty is the negative log of a heavy-tailed prior, so the solution is a maximum-a-posteriori point estimate that concentrates energy in few coefficients. Additional convex side constraints such as nonnegativity, or replacing the penalty by the total variation of the field, encode further prior knowledge and are incorporated through iterative splitting using proximal steps. The solver is iterative, with each iteration dominated by a fast forward and adjoint transform. Recovery accuracy is guaranteed when the composed operator approximately preserves norms of sparse vectors, yielding an explicit reconstruction-error bound that grows with the noise tolerance and the tail of the discarded small coefficients. Reconstructions are compared against an iterative greedy beam-subtraction baseline across sampling densities.
