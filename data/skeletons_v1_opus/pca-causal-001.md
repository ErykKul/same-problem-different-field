MECHANISM: Observations are arranged into a two-way array indexed by entities along one axis and by ordered periods along the other, where some cells are observed and a structured subset of cells is missing. The goal is to impute the missing cells by recovering an underlying array that closely reproduces the observed cells while having low complexity, measured by the sum of its singular values as a convex surrogate for rank. The estimator minimizes a squared-error fit over observed cells plus a penalty equal to the scaled sum of singular values, optionally augmented with additive per-row and per-column offset terms. The minimization is solved by an iterative procedure that alternates between filling missing cells with the current low-complexity reconstruction and applying soft thresholding to the singular values of the completed array, shrinking each toward zero by the penalty weight. This thresholding step is itself computed from a singular-value decomposition and yields, at each iteration, the proximal solution of the penalized objective. The penalty weight is selected by held-out validation on a randomly masked portion of the observed cells. The analysis extends recovery guarantees to the case where the pattern of missing cells exhibits dependence along the ordered axis rather than being uniformly random. The recovered low-complexity array represents the values the treated cells would have taken absent intervention, and contrasts between observed and imputed cells estimate the intervention effect. The method is related to factor-structured models in which each cell is an inner product of latent row and column vectors plus additive offsets. Performance is assessed on synthetic arrays calibrated to real data.
