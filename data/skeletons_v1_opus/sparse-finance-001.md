MECHANISM: A weight vector over many items is chosen to minimize a quadratic risk term (a positive-definite quadratic form) minus a linear reward term, subject to the weights summing to one, with two added ingredients: a worst-case inner maximization of the linear term over an ellipsoidal uncertainty region around the estimated reward vector, and a fixed per-item charge applied through a counting (cardinality) term that penalizes the number of nonzero weights. Solving the inner robust maximization in closed form converts the ellipsoidal-uncertainty problem into the base quadratic plus a square-root-of-the-quadratic-form regularizer, which is shown analytically to equal the unregularized solution at a larger effective risk-aversion level, yielding a one-to-one map between uncertainty level and that coefficient and expressing the robust solution as a convex blend of two benchmark allocations. The counting term makes the full problem combinatorial and NP-hard, so it is replaced by a continuous difference-of-two-convex surrogate (a capped absolute-value penalty), and the resulting program is shown to share local and global minimizers with the original under a parameter bound. The surrogate is minimized by a proximal scheme that, at each step, linearizes the concave part and solves a strongly convex subproblem using second-order (semismooth-Newton) information; a sharp piecewise-linear structure gives a property guaranteeing a locally linear convergence rate to a stationary point that is at least a local optimum. Closed-form sensitivity conditions characterize when the count of selected items rises, falls, or stays fixed as uncertainty and per-item cost vary. Solution quality and timing are checked against an exact solver and a convex-relaxation baseline.
