MECHANISM: A collection of candidate items is grouped into nested clusters by hierarchical agglomeration based on pairwise correlation, producing a tree of subsets including singletons. For each subset a composite null hypothesis states that the subset contains no truly relevant item, and a conservative test statistic with a superuniform null distribution is computed, here by refitting a model with the subset removed and comparing to the full fit via a likelihood-ratio or analogous test. The number of discoveries is redefined through a weighting that assigns each rejected subset a size inversely proportional to its cardinality and counts only the minimal rejected subsets, so that resolving a region finely and selecting a single item are scored coherently. A step-up thresholding rule rejects all hypotheses whose p-values fall below the largest cutoff at which a linear function of this generalized discovery count exceeds a slope times the cutoff, with the rejected set constrained to lie in a family closed under intersection so that implications among hypotheses are respected. A variant first replaces each p-value by the supremum over all hypotheses that imply it, forcing monotonicity along the tree. The required slope is derived under no dependence assumptions and under successively stronger positive-dependence conditions, including pairwise and setwise generalizations, with each weaker case recovered as a special instance. It is proven that the resulting procedures keep the generalized false discovery proportion in expectation at or below a nominal level. The selected subsets are the minimal rejected clusters.
