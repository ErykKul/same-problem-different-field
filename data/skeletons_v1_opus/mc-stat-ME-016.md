MECHANISM: A scalar sequence is generated by a model in which each observation, conditional on the past, follows a specified parametric distribution whose location-type quantity is driven recursively by a link-transformed function of earlier observations, earlier driving quantities, and covariates, with a finite parameter vector to be estimated. Some entries of the sequence are missing, and the goal is to estimate the parameter despite the gaps. Given a current parameter guess, missing entries are filled in one at a time by reconstructing the recursive driving quantity from previously observed and previously imputed values and drawing a random value from the assumed conditional distribution, producing a completed sequence; the chosen estimator is applied to this completed sequence to obtain a parameter estimate. This imputation-and-estimate step is repeated several times and the resulting estimates are averaged to form an updated guess, which seeds the next round; the outer loop iterates until a variability-based stopping rule is met. The procedure is cast as repeated application of a measurable map on parameter space, i.e. a discrete-time dynamical system whose iterates form a Markov chain conditional on the observed indices; using consistency of the base estimator and ergodic arguments, the true parameter is shown to be the unique attracting fixed point of the infinite-sample map, giving almost-sure convergence regardless of the missingness rate. Averaging across the inner replicates reduces conditional variance at rate one over the replicate count. The method is evaluated by repeated simulation across several bounded-support conditional models and missingness proportions, comparing two stopping criteria against full-data estimates, and is applied to bounded real series.
