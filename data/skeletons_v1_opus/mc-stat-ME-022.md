MECHANISM: An observed sequence is modelled as emissions from an unobserved finite-state chain with first-order transition structure, parameterized by an initial vector, a transition matrix, and an emission matrix, with conjugate priors over the parameters. Some entries of each sequence are missing, and the aim is posterior inference over parameters and latent states. The key move is analytic marginalization: missing emissions are integrated out, and crucially the latent states attached to missing positions are also integrated out, because summing the chain over an unobserved intermediate state collapses to a single transition raised to the power equal to the time gap between successive observed positions. This reduces the chain over observed positions to an inhomogeneous chain whose transition between consecutive observed times is a matrix power. A forward recursion accumulates joint probabilities of observed emissions and the current state over only the observed positions, and a backward pass samples states sequentially from conditionals built from the forward quantities and the gap-power transitions. Parameters are then drawn from their conditionals, conjugate-closed for the emission rows (a Dirichlet update by transition/emission counts) and via a Metropolis-within-step for the others, alternating in a coordinate-wise sampler. Convergence is analyzed through the spectral gap of the induced transition kernel, and a marginalization ordering argument proves the collapsed kernel has a gap at least as large as partially-collapsed and full augmentation schemes, giving a geometric mixing bound. Complexity per sweep scales with the observed fraction times sequence length, with matrix powers precomputed and cached.
