MECHANISM: A system of ordinary differential equations depends on a vector of uncertain parameters modeled as independent bounded random variables with a known distribution, making each state trajectory a random process. To compute the mean trajectory efficiently, the state is expanded in a basis of multivariate polynomials orthogonal with respect to the parameter distribution, so that the random-parameter dependence is confined to the basis and time dependence to spectral coefficients; the mean is then the leading coefficient and the variance the sum of squared remaining coefficients. The expansion is truncated at a total-degree order and its coefficients are obtained non-intrusively by integrating the dynamics at a set of sampled parameter points and fitting by least squares, with the order increased until coefficients stabilize. The resulting mean signal is sampled into a time series, from which a full pairwise absolute-difference distance matrix is formed and normalized to unit range, producing an unthresholded recurrence image. The image is binarized at a sweep of thresholds, and at each threshold the number of connected components above a minimum size is counted; the count that persists over the widest contiguous threshold interval is selected. By enlarging the parameter uncertainty support stepwise and tracking when this persistent count changes by more than a relative tolerance, the largest tolerable uncertainty preserving the nominal qualitative regime is identified and tiled across the parameter space.
