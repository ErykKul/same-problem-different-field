MECHANISM: The goal is to quantify the sampling error and an equivalent independent-sample count for a correlated sequence of discrete combinatorial objects produced by a dependent-sample simulator targeting a fixed distribution. For real-valued summaries, the classical equivalent-sample count is the run length scaled by the ratio of the marginal variance to the limiting variance, the latter being the autocovariance series; this is generalized to the combinatorial objects by replacing means and variances with their metric-space analogues defined through a chosen pairwise distance, and by expressing lagged correlations via averaged squared distances between time-separated blocks, then summing the smoothed positive correlation series. Alternative counts project each object to a scalar by its distance to reference objects and apply the standard scalar estimator, summarized by a median or minimum over references. To evaluate these counts, a known target over the objects is built empirically and a dependent-sample simulator with symmetric local moves and accept/reject steps is run on it, so that truly independent draws from the same target are also available. For each summary of interest (component-inclusion frequencies, object probabilities, and a representative aggregate object obtained as a metric centroid), the true sampling standard error is estimated brute-force across many replicate dependent runs and compared to the standard error obtained from independent draws numbering the estimated equivalent count. Agreement between the two standard errors, measured by relative and ratio discrepancies, indicates whether a given count faithfully captures the sampling error. The methods are validated on simulated targets and then applied to real datasets to build error bars on inclusion frequencies and to separate between-run disagreement attributable to limited sampling.
