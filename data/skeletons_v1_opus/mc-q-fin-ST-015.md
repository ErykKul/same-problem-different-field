MECHANISM: A multivariate sequence is decomposed into linear combinations of its components ordered by how slowly a chosen statistic of the combination drifts over time, subject to a unit-variance normalization. For the second-moment variant, minimizing the instantaneous drift of the variance of a projected combination under the normalization constraint yields, via a Lagrange multiplier, a generalized eigenvalue problem pairing the symmetrized lag-shifted cross-product matrix against the zero-lag cross-product (covariance) matrix. Because the right-hand matrix is symmetric positive definite, the problem is solved by a standard symmetric-definite generalized eigensolver; the eigenvectors give the projection weights and the eigenvalues are the corresponding lagged autocorrelations, which map monotonically to relaxation timescales. For the heavy-tail variant, the same drift-minimization is applied to a higher even-order moment of the projected combination under a normalization constraint; the stationarity condition no longer reduces to a linear eigenproblem, so the projection weights are obtained instead by a normalize-after-update fixed-point iteration, run either one component at a time with deflation or for all components jointly with a symmetric orthogonalization step. The two variants are interpreted as separating components governed by volatility versus by tails. Fitted weights are estimated on an initial window and the resulting projections are carried forward onto later data to assess out-of-sample persistence of the recovered timescales and of the orthogonality of components.
