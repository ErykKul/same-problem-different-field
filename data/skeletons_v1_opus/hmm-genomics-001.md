MECHANISM: An observed sequence of discrete symbols over a finite alphabet is modeled as generated by an underlying unobserved sequence of discrete states evolving as a first-order Markov chain. The model is parameterized by a state-to-state transition probability matrix and a state-to-symbol emission probability matrix, assumed homogeneous along the sequence. The joint probability of an observation sequence and a state path factorizes as a product over positions of transition times emission terms. Parameters are fit by maximum likelihood using an iterative expectation-maximization procedure: forward and backward recursions compute, in time proportional to sequence length times squared state count, the probabilities of partial sequences ending or beginning at each state. Expected transition and emission counts are accumulated from these quantities and renormalized to update the parameters, and the procedure repeats until the likelihood change falls below a threshold, with the likelihood guaranteed nondecreasing each step. After fitting, the single most probable state path for a sequence is recovered by a dynamic-programming maximization recursion with backtracking. Predicted state labels segment the sequence into regions, and a post-hoc length-range filter derived from the training labels prunes predicted segments outside the observed bounds. Accuracy is summarized by true-positive rate and positive predictive value against annotated labels.
