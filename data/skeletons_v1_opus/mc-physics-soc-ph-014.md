MECHANISM: An object is represented as a collection of tuples grouping entities into interactions of varying sizes, stratified into layers by tuple size. For each layer, the number of bits needed to transmit it is computed from a logarithm of a combinatorial coefficient counting how the observed tuples could be chosen from all possible tuples of that size over the entity set. A layer can instead be transmitted relative to a higher-size representative layer by projecting the representative down to the target size, encoding the shared tuples via their overlap and the remainder via the residual count, again through combinatorial coefficients. The total description length under a chosen subset of representative layers sums the cost of transmitting the representatives directly plus the conditional cost of every other layer given its cheapest representative. This total is minimized over all subsets of representative layers, exactly by exhaustive enumeration when the number of layers is small and otherwise by a greedy procedure that adds one best layer per round in cubic time. A normalized score is formed as the reduction in description length achieved by the optimal subset divided by the maximum possible reduction, yielding a value bounded between zero and one. The framework is extended to coarse-grained representations under an entity partition by replacing entity tuples with group-label multisets and using multiset coefficients. As validation, the reduced representation is compared to the original via summaries of connected-component count, partition agreement, degree correlation, and a stochastic copying dynamics on the structure run to an absorbing consensus.
