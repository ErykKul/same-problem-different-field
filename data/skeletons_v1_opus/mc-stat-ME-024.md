MECHANISM: Observations are matrices indexed by time, with rows indexing entities embedded in a known fixed graph and columns indexing several co-evolving quantities per entity. A one-step transition is modeled as a separable bilinear map: a left operator acts on the entity dimension and a right operator acts on the quantity dimension, plus additive noise. The left operator is constrained to a two-parameter form, a scalar multiple of identity plus a scalar multiple of the row-normalized adjacency, encoding self-persistence and neighbor spillover. The right operator is constrained to be low rank and is factored into two thin matrices, so cross-quantity influence passes through a small latent subspace. Parameters are estimated by minimizing the summed squared one-step prediction error, a smooth but non-convex objective whose two parameter blocks have mismatched scales. The estimator is a preconditioned gradient descent in which each block's gradient is rescaled by norms of the complementary operator and by inverse Gram matrices of the factors, removing the scale ambiguity and balancing curvature. A warm start is obtained by alternating closed-form least squares: a reduced-rank regression via a partial eigen-decomposition for the subspace, then ordinary least squares for the two scalars. The rank is chosen by a stabilized ratio of successive singular values. Local linear convergence to the global optimum is proven under restricted strong convexity and smoothness, and non-asymptotic estimation-error bounds are derived under an equivalence-invariant distance, with the graph structure shown to reduce the error rate for the network parameters as the entity count grows.
