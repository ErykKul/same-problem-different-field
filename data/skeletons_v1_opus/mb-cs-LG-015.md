MECHANISM: A parameterized model maps an input context to a vector of real-valued scores over a finite outcome set, which a normalized exponential converts into a probability distribution over outcomes. Two training objectives are contrasted: a likelihood-matching objective whose gradient with respect to the scores is the predicted distribution minus an indicator of the target, and a clipped surrogate objective whose gradient is the same difference rescaled by an outcome-dependent factor that can be large for intermediate-probability outcomes. The analysis defines a curvature property as positive semidefiniteness of the objective's Hessian in score space and proves, under a first-order linearization linking parameter displacement to score displacement, that this property forces the parameter-space gradient to be positively aligned with the direction toward a near-optimal parameter set. Starting from a divergence-regularized expected-gain objective, a closed-form optimal distribution is derived as the base distribution reweighted exponentially by a per-outcome gain divided by a temperature, and an equivalent additive shift of the scores is obtained. The proposed method replaces the surrogate with fitting the model's scores or distribution to these closed-form optimal targets via a squared-error term, a smooth robust term, or a forward divergence term, each of which is shown to have the curvature property. Gradient-norm upper bounds are proved that are monotone in the loss, so update magnitudes shrink as the loss decreases. Convergence-rate bounds are derived showing linear contraction governed by the spectral radius of a linearized update operator, and the additive shift is chosen to minimize the initial discrepancy and tighten the bound. The per-outcome gain signal is estimated either from a single sampled outcome, from log-probabilities of an auxiliary scorer, or from a log-ratio of two scorers. The construction is justified theoretically and then assessed empirically against alternative update rules.
