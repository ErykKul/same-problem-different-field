MECHANISM: An observed quantity is modeled as a discrete-valued signal of interest scaled by a constant plus additive independent noise, observed component-by-component as a vector per time step. The goal is to characterize the minimum mean-square error of estimating the discrete signal from the observation, where the optimal estimate is the conditional expectation. The method first derives the joint probability distribution of a transformed observation vector as a mixture of shifted noise densities weighted by the joint probabilities of the underlying discrete symbols. From this joint distribution the second-moment (covariance) matrix of the transformed vector is computed in closed form, with diagonal entries depending on per-symbol probabilities and off-diagonal entries on a pairwise dependence parameter. Assuming the transformed vector behaves as an innovation process, its covariance is decomposed as the sum of an estimation-error covariance and a known observation-noise covariance, so the error covariance is obtained by subtraction. Removing the scaling constant yields the error covariance of the discrete signal, whose trace gives the mean-square error. A correlation correction term is introduced because the diagonal contributions are statistically dependent, and bounds on the dependence parameter are derived from positive semidefiniteness of the covariance. The derived error is then validated against a known integral identity linking input-output mutual information to the mean-square error, and because the input is non-Gaussian only inequalities and approximations are available, so concrete cases are evaluated numerically. The overall output is a closed-form expression for the estimation error written in terms of the symbol distribution.
