MECHANISM: A large population of identical units is each modeled by a small system of coupled nonlinear ordinary differential equations forming a delayed negative-feedback loop with saturating (Hill-type and Michaelis-Menten-type) rate terms. The units are coupled all-to-all only through a single mean-field variable, the population average of one component, which feeds back into each unit's first equation. The population is partitioned into two fractions: one fraction receives an external time-periodic square-wave forcing term, the other receives none, and a scalar fraction parameter sets the split. The full system is integrated numerically forward in time past a long transient, and the dominant period and amplitude of each subgroup are extracted from the Fourier spectrum of the averaged variable. Sweeping the fraction parameter reveals that one subgroup's intrinsic-mode amplitude shrinks and then vanishes abruptly at a critical value, where its period jumps discontinuously to the forcing period. Because units within a subgroup synchronize exactly, the population is reduced to a two-unit surrogate that reproduces the same critical value. The mean-field input is decomposed into a constant part plus harmonic components, the saturating coupling is linearized since its argument is small, yielding an effective single forced unit whose effective coupling and forcing amplitudes are explicit functions of the fraction. The squared intrinsic-mode amplitude is shown to vanish as a square-root of the distance to threshold, identifying the transition as a supercritical Hopf-type bifurcation, while the forced subgroup loses its free mode through a subcritical-type transition.
