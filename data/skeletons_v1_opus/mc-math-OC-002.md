MECHANISM: Consider a quantity whose location over a space is uncertain and described by a density, and a nonnegative allocation function that distributes a limited budget over that space. A detection function gives, for each location and local amount of allocated budget, the conditional probability of success, assumed zero at zero budget and concave and increasing in budget. One functional integrates the detection function against the density to give an expected (subjective) success probability; a second functional evaluates the detection function only at the single true location to give the true success probability. The allocation that maximizes the subjective functional at every budget level subject to a total-budget constraint is characterized in semi-closed form by inverting the marginal-gain function and equalizing a multiplier across the space. The work then proves, by constructing explicit discrete and continuous instances, that the two functionals may coincide identically, may differ everywhere, and that the subjective-optimal allocation can be strictly dominated on the true functional by another allocation. A composite density built from inconsistent inputs is shown to yield a subjective-optimal allocation whose true value can be exceeded by a combination of allocations optimized separately. An associated existence question is argued to be unsolvable. Finally it is established that, as the budget grows without bound, both functionals converge to one. All claims are obtained by analysis and explicit computation, not by data fitting.
