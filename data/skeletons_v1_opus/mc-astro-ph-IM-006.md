MECHANISM: A coupled fluid-and-particle system is evolved forward in time on a fixed uniform spatial mesh inside a periodic box from an early to a late epoch. The continuous medium is advanced by solving, at each cell interface, a local conservation-law flux problem with a high-order reconstruction of cell quantities, while a collisionless particle component is integrated under self-gravity, with the two coupled through the gravitational field. A nonequilibrium reaction network for several chemical species, together with tabulated heating and cooling source terms and an externally prescribed time-dependent radiation field, updates internal energy and species fractions each step. The background expansion enters through an externally integrated scaling law obtained by quadrature of an ordinary differential relation; a separate second-order ordinary differential equation for a growth amplitude is integrated numerically. From the evolved fields, many one-dimensional traversals are extracted along principal axes; along each, an integrated attenuation quantity is accumulated cell by cell as a sum whose per-cell contribution reduces, after assuming a single broadened line shape, to a closed-form difference of error functions that accounts for bulk-motion frequency shifts. A transmitted-signal field is formed by exponentiating the negative attenuation, its fractional fluctuation is taken, and its one-dimensional power spectrum is computed via a discrete transform. Relative differences of these spectra and of aggregated thermodynamic quantities between parameter variants are then quantified, and a local logarithmic slope is fit by piecewise polynomial regression.
