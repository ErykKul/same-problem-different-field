MECHANISM: For each unit, several relational graphs over a shared set of entities are restricted to the entities flagged for that unit, yielding induced subgraphs. On a pair of static subgraphs a walker with restart follows a mixture transition operator that picks one graph with a fixed probability and then a uniform neighbor, so the combined row-normalized transition matrix is a convex combination of the two adjacency operators. The restart distribution is seeded by a prior: when an auxiliary co-occurrence graph is large enough, each entity is scored by a weighted blend of two normalized path-based centrality measures, otherwise the prior is uniform, and a length-based down-weighting is applied. A separate dynamic graph is built by thresholding the larger of two correlation matrices computed under two conditions, restricted to flagged plus most-perturbed entities, with the restart distribution set proportional to standardized deviation scores. Each walk is iterated until its probability vector reaches the stationary distribution of the restarted chain. The two stationary vectors are put on a common scale by quantile normalization and blended. A second-level walk is then run on a hypergraph whose hyperedges are units and whose vertices are flagged entities, with vertex weights taken from the first-level scores and hyperedge weights decaying with cross-unit similarity; its two-step transition operator selects a hyperedge then a vertex, and a restarted walk converges to a refined stationary ranking at both unit and population level. Entities are ordered by their stationary scores, and accuracy is assessed by top-ranked overlap with a reference set and area-under-curve summaries.
