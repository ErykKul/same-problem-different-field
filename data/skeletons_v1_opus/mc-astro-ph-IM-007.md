MECHANISM: Expensive stochastic simulations produce, for many discrete settings of an input scalar, large ensembles of one-dimensional nonnegative profiles along an axis. Each profile is summarized by fitting a two-parameter positive-support curve via nonlinear least squares, plus an integrated total amplitude. The two shape parameters are mapped through monotone transforms onto the unit square and aggregated into a two-dimensional histogram conditioned on the input scalar. A joint density over the transformed parameters is then estimated as the exponential of a linear combination of tensor-product basis functions, fit as a generalized linear model with log link and per-bin count error, with a roughness penalty on high-order finite differences of the coefficients, solved efficiently using the separable structure of the design array. The amplitude is modeled separately by fitting a skewed parametric density to the ensemble at each input level by maximum likelihood. Each density parameter is then regressed against the input scalar by fitting low-order polynomials in its logarithm via weighted least squares. New profiles are generated by sampling the factorized joint shape density and the amplitude density, then reconstituting the curve. Agreement between fitted and empirical distributions is quantified by an integrated-absolute-difference distance between cumulative functions and by a maximum-deviation two-sample statistic. The whole pipeline replaces direct simulation with a fast generative surrogate that reproduces ensemble-level fluctuations.
