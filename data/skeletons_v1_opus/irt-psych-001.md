MECHANISM: A vector of latent real-valued scores indexing one group of entities is estimated from sparse binary outcomes of paired interactions between two groups, where the probability of each binary outcome follows a logistic function of the difference of two latent scores. To remove the nuisance scores attached to the second group, observations sharing a common second-group element are randomly matched into disjoint pairs, converting cross-group comparisons into within-group comparisons whose likelihood depends only on the score difference of interest and whose terms are mutually independent by construction. The matched comparisons induce a weighted comparison graph over the target entities. The score vector is then obtained by maximizing the resulting concave log-likelihood, equivalently solving the first-order stationarity condition, under a centering constraint. A bootstrap-style variant repeats the random matching multiple times and averages the resulting estimates to recover information lost by any single disjoint matching. The estimation error is controlled by linearizing the stationarity condition: the deviation of the estimate from the truth is expanded as the pseudo-inverse of the negative log-likelihood curvature (a weighted graph Laplacian) applied to the centered score residuals, plus a higher-order remainder shown to be negligible. From this linear representation an entrywise (sup-norm) finite-sample error bound and a top-set recovery guarantee are derived, matched against an information-theoretic lower bound to establish near-optimality. The leading term is shown to be approximately a centered Gaussian vector with covariance equal to the pseudo-inverse Laplacian, yielding a distributional approximation with an explicit Berry-Esseen-type rate. This supports confidence statements and a sharp characterization of the squared-error magnitude. A fixed-size limit further establishes asymptotic normality and equivalence of the averaged estimator to a reweighted pseudo-likelihood estimator.
