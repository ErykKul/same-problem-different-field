MECHANISM: Each entity is a finite symbolic sequence over a small alphabet, summarized as a high-dimensional count vector whose components tally occurrences of every fixed-length contiguous subsequence pattern, giving a feature-by-entity count matrix. A label is one of several disjoint classes, and the goal is to assign new entities to classes via a linear decision rule defined by a weight matrix plus intercept passed through an activation. Two families of rules are compared. The first models a joint density of class and count vector and inverts it by the product rule to obtain a posterior, choosing the class that maximizes the class-prior times class-conditional likelihood. One generative variant treats the count vector as draws from a categorical-multinomial distribution whose per-symbol parameters are estimated either by normalized frequencies or by adding a small pseudo-count constant for smoothing. Another generative variant factorizes the sequence likelihood as a product of conditional probabilities of each pattern given its truncated predecessor, expressed as ratios of length-k to length-(k minus one) pattern probabilities. The second family models the posterior directly: one estimator squashes a linear score through a sigmoid and fits weights by minimizing a log-loss plus a norm penalty, the other fits weights by minimizing a margin-based hinge loss plus a norm penalty. Penalties are either a sum of absolute weights, yielding sparse weights, or a sum of squared weights, yielding dense weights. Multiclass assignment uses a one-versus-rest decomposition, and parameters are chosen by exhaustive sweeps over pattern length and penalty strength with cross-validated evaluation. Sparse smoothed counts versus dense counts and matching of training and test sparsity govern accuracy, especially when test sequences are truncated fragments.
DOMAIN: alignment-free viral genome sequence classification
STRUCTURE: sparse linear algebra
DATA_OBJECT: sparse matrix
INFERENCE: maximum likelihood
PROBLEM_FORM: prediction or classification
DISTRIBUTION: count; multinomial
