MECHANISM: Two decision-makers interact in a hierarchical leader-follower game. The upper-level agent chooses a small tuple of bounding parameters to maximize an aggregate welfare objective, subject to a participation constraint that the lower-level agent's optimal payoff stay above a baseline. The lower-level agent, taking the upper-level parameters as fixed, solves its own constrained profit-maximization over a large vector of nonnegative capacity, dispatch, storage charge/discharge, purchase, and unmet-quantity variables indexed by category, technology, period, representative day, and hour. The lower-level program enforces supply-demand balance, capacity and ramp limits, a budget cap on discounted investment outlays, and binary switches enabling or disabling certain capacities. A nonlinear cost relationship is approximated by a piecewise-constant function over utilization bands to preserve linearity, yielding a mixed-integer linear program. A lemma establishes that the two price-like coupling variables are always driven to their respective bounds at the lower-level optimum, so they can be fixed to the upper-level parameters; this removes the bilinear terms and decouples the levels. The overall bilevel problem is then solved by exhaustive grid enumeration over the upper-level tuple: at each grid point the lower-level mixed-integer program is solved to optimality and the upper-level objective is evaluated, and the best feasible tuple is selected. Sensitivity sweeps over budget and participant-share parameters trace how the optimal policy and objective respond.
