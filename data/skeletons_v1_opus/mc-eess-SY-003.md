MECHANISM: The computational core is a single illustrative experiment in which several mobile agents must each choose a sequence of continuous action vectors to move through a constrained spatial environment toward distinct targets. The task is cast as a sequential decision process over a finite horizon: each agent observes its local state and a shared context summary and selects an action to maximize an expected discounted cumulative reward subject to penalties for constraint violation, proximity collisions, and excursions outside the allowed region. The reward combines a quality-of-link term, distance-to-target terms, separation margins, and smoothness penalties on the control variables. A perception front end first processes high-resolution images through a deep convolutional network with hierarchical context encoding to produce a pixel-level segmentation of spatial obstacles, which is summarized into a context feature and gated by a confidence score before being passed to the decision module. The decision is solved by a parameterized policy per agent paired with a centralized value estimator; the estimator is trained by minimizing a temporal-difference error against lagged target copies on replayed past transitions, and each policy is updated by following the gradient of the estimated value with respect to its parameters. Training is centralized while execution is decentralized, so each agent acts on local observations at inference time. The learned policies are run forward to produce trajectories that are compared against simpler non-learned and ablated-input baselines on quality-of-link and collision metrics. The remainder of the work is architectural and descriptive, surveying use cases, interfaces, and experimental platforms.
