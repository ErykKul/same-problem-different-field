MECHANISM: An unknown constant or piecewise-constant parameter vector relates a scalar observation to a known regressor vector through a linear inner product. Two parallel estimators are run, each evolving by a continuous gradient flow that drives its prediction error toward zero at distinct positive adaptation rates. The continuous evolution of each estimator's error is governed by a time-varying linear differential equation whose transition operator over a fixed window is recorded. After a fixed timer interval the system performs a discrete reset that replaces both estimates with an affine combination of the two; the combination weights are chosen, using the two recorded transition operators, so that the combined error cancels exactly, yielding exact recovery in finite time after a single reset. The weight matrix is well defined precisely when the difference of the two transition operators is invertible, which is guaranteed when the regressor injects sufficient energy over the window, a relaxed integral excitation condition rather than a perpetual one. For the piecewise-constant case the reset is applied recursively at each interval, requiring the stronger perpetual excitation and an interval shorter than the parameter dwell time. Boundedness and stability of the combined error are established with a quadratic energy function that is nonincreasing during flow and strictly decreased at resets. Robustness to additive measurement perturbation is analyzed by bounding the deviation between perturbed and nominal trajectories and proving input-to-state and integral input-to-state stability bounds. Per-step cost is linear during flow with cubic operations only at sparse resets for transition-operator computation and inversion.
