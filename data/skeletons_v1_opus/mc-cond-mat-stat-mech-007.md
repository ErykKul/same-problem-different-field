MECHANISM: A reversible local update rule on an infinite regular lattice, where each site carries a value in a finite vector space over the two-element field, is encoded algebraically as multiplication by an invertible matrix whose entries are Laurent polynomials in shift variables over that field. Repeated application of the dynamics corresponds to taking powers of this matrix acting on a free module, and a duality identifies the quantum automorphism with this classical linear update. A size functional counts the number of nonzero monomial terms of an element, measuring the spatial extent of the corresponding local quantity. The central question is whether iterating the matrix drives this size to infinity for every nonzero starting element, either for all iteration counts (strong) or for all but a vanishingly sparse subset of counts (weak). These growth conditions are characterized algebraically by the absence of nonzero solutions to an eigenvalue-type equation, equivalently the nonvanishing of a determinant for all powers, meaning no local quantity is preserved up to translation. Theorems then prove that whenever this no-invariant condition holds, expectation values of arbitrary local quantities under a broad class of initial states converge to their reference equilibrium values, by bounding each expectation by a constant raised to the growing size. A counterexample shows weak growth need not imply strong growth, and explicit constructions of provably strongly growing maps are given via a doubling embedding. A direct simulation iterates the matrix and tabulates the size and expectation values to confirm convergence numerically.
