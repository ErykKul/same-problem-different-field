MECHANISM: A high-order dynamical system must follow a reference trajectory despite an unknown, hard-to-model input-to-output actuation relation and external disturbances. The unknown actuation map is first approximated by a stacked weighted-sum-plus-nonlinearity function fitted to logged input-output samples; fitting is posed as nonlinear least squares and solved by repeatedly forming the Jacobian of residuals, building an approximate curvature from its product with itself, adding a tunable diagonal damping, and solving the resulting linear system for the parameter step, which interpolates between gradient descent and a second-order step. The fitted map is augmented at run time by an adaptive correction term and a barrier term that grows without bound as a tracking-error measure approaches a safety threshold; an accompanying scalar adaptation law updates online to absorb bounded unmodeled effects. Above this, a receding-horizon optimal-control layer discretizes a constrained trajectory-tracking objective over a finite time grid into a finite-dimensional nonlinear program with multiple-shooting continuity constraints and box bounds on states, inputs, and input rates, re-solved each cycle from the measured state by a single sequential-quadratic step with warm starting. Required state estimates are produced separately by a feature-based estimator that maximizes a posterior over poses with loop-closure corrections. Closed-loop stability is established by constructing an energy-like function combining squared errors and adaptation states and showing its time derivative is bounded above by a negative multiple of itself plus a constant, giving uniform exponential convergence to a bounded set, and the whole stack is validated experimentally.
