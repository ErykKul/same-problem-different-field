MECHANISM: The aim is to quantify and test the effect of a binary intervention on a hierarchically ordered set of outcomes when units are grouped and observed over successive time periods. Every cross-group pair of units is compared by examining the ordered outcomes in priority order and recording the first one that differs, yielding a favorable, unfavorable, or tied label for the pair. Counts of these labels are aggregated into a ratio-type summary measure of benefit, with tied pairs split evenly. To respect the grouped and temporal structure, pairwise comparisons are restricted or stratified by group and by period, producing group-and-period-specific summaries that are combined on a logarithmic scale with weights reflecting how many comparisons each contributes. These log-scale summaries are then modeled by a hierarchical linear model with fixed terms for time and treatment contrast and nested random intercepts and random slopes at the group and sequence levels. An alternative embeds the same pairwise-superiority probability directly into a regression with a link function, estimated by solving estimating equations, optionally restricting comparisons to within a group. To evaluate these estimators, synthetic datasets are repeatedly generated from a latent linear model with a nonlinear link, group- and period-level random effects calibrated to target within-group correlation and temporal persistence, and a secular trend. For each generated dataset every estimator is applied, and across many replicates the rejection frequency under the null measures false-positive control while the rejection frequency under alternatives measures power.
