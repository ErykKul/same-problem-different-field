MECHANISM: The system state is a weighted graph whose nodes each carry a complex-valued variable evolving under a normal-form nonlinear oscillator equation with an intrinsic frequency, an amplitude-saturating cubic term, and a forcing term that sums phase-difference couplings over incident edges. The coupled equations are integrated forward with a semi-implicit scheme treating the stiff cubic term implicitly. Edge weights are adapted online by a local three-factor rule: a per-edge eligibility trace accumulates a coincidence quantity (the real part of the product of one node with the conjugate of its neighbor) with two decay timescales, and the weight change equals a learning rate times the slow trace times a sparse global scalar times an oscillation-phase gate indicator. Multiple target phase patterns are stored in one step by a complex outer-product superposition normalized by node count. Retrieval suppresses destructive interference by reweighting each incident contribution with a softmax over the cosine of its phase mismatch to the local field, optionally rotating contributions to the field phase. Learning is scheduled into phases: an online phase accumulates fast traces under reward gating, an offline replay phase regenerates and perturbs stored trajectories to update values, and a consolidation phase commits slow traces inside burst-gated windows while a multiplicative decay enforces a norm budget. Diagnostics compute a mean-resultant order parameter, pattern overlap, and phase error; a finite-size sweep of coupling strength locates the maximal-variance critical window, and ablations and timing-shuffle controls falsify each mechanism.
