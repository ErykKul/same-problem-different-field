MECHANISM: A parametric multilayer model with shared feature-extraction layers and several task-specific output branches maps a two-dimensional time-frequency array to a presence probability and to several normalized continuous targets. Inputs are produced by a preprocessing chain that estimates a noise power spectrum robustly via a median statistic, divides each frequency component by the square root of that spectrum to flatten variance, restricts to a band, and forms an overlapping windowed transform into the time-frequency grid. The model is fit by minimizing a composite loss summing a down-weighted cross-entropy term that emphasizes hard examples for the binary branch and squared-error terms over only positive instances for the continuous branches. Parameters are optimized by stochastic mini-batch gradient descent with adaptive step sizes, weight decay, gradient clipping, a decaying schedule, and early stopping, using batches balanced between positive and negative instances. Training instances are synthesized from a parametric damped-sinusoid template with randomized parameters and heavily perturbed by injected artifacts and distortions exceeding the perturbations used for held-out evaluation. Several independently initialized copies are trained and their outputs averaged, with the dispersion across copies used as an uncertainty estimate. Evaluation computes a threshold-sweep detection curve and its area, detection rate at a fixed false-positive level, and absolute error of the continuous outputs, and contrasts these against a template-correlation baseline and cited reference estimates. A consistency check regresses output uncertainty against a signal-strength proxy on log-log axes. The dominant computation is gradient-based training and forward evaluation of the layered model.
