MECHANISM: Paired interacting macromolecular chains from several related sources are compared through an integrative deterministic pipeline that computes geometric and physical descriptors and contrasts them. First, one-dimensional symbol sequences are globally aligned in pairs by a dynamic-programming scheme to yield identity scores. Second, three-dimensional coordinate models of single chains and of binary assemblies are predicted from sequence by a learned predictor, retaining only high-confidence models. Third, extracted sub-structures are superposed by rigid-body alignment and their similarity is quantified by a root-mean-square coordinate deviation and a length-normalized fold-overlap score (an all-pairs geometric comparison over point sets of atoms). Fourth, the contact region of each assembly is characterized by buried surface area, an estimated interaction free energy, and counts of polar and charged contacts identified by distance criteria, which are then normalized by interface area to give densities. Fifth, the electrostatic potential field around each assembly is obtained by discretizing space and numerically solving a linearized continuum partial differential equation for the potential given assigned charges and dielectric and ionic parameters; the resulting gridded field is interpolated trilinearly onto atomic centers. Per-contact potential differences are computed, ranked by magnitude, and weighted by contact multiplicity to locate clustered high-impact sites. Finally, these sites are mapped back onto the aligned sequences to assess conservation of physicochemical role across sources. All quantities are compared descriptively across single representative models without statistical sampling or inference.
