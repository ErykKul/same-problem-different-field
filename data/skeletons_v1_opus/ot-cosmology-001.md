MECHANISM: Two configurations of mass are given, an early near-uniform distribution and a late observed distribution of discrete locations, and the task is to recover the displacement that carried each parcel of mass from its early to its late position. The displacement is assumed to be the one that minimizes the total squared distance that mass is moved, subject to the constraint that all mass from the source is conservatively redistributed onto the target with matching totals. This is posed as the assignment of source elements to target elements minimizing aggregate squared transport cost, a constrained linear minimization over the set of mass-preserving couplings. Because mass is discretized into equal units at both ends, the problem reduces to a one-to-one matching between two point sets that minimizes the sum of squared pairwise distances, a combinatorial optimization with a guaranteed unique geometric structure under squared-distance cost. Solving this matching yields, for each element, a unique straight-line path and hence an estimated initial position and velocity, resolving the ambiguity that plagues approaches lacking velocity information. The recovered matching is the discrete realization of a map that is the gradient of a convex potential, which guarantees uniqueness of the optimal displacement. The method is validated by applying it to synthetic data produced by a forward dynamical simulation with known ground truth and comparing the reconstructed initial configuration to the true one. The reconstructed early state can then seed a forward simulation for comparison against observation.
DOMAIN: cosmology, large-scale structure reconstruction
STRUCTURE: numerical optimization
DATA_OBJECT: point set
INFERENCE: deterministic optimization
PROBLEM_FORM: reconstruction or denoising
DISTRIBUTION: continuous; none
COMPLEXITY: combinatorial or NP-hard
