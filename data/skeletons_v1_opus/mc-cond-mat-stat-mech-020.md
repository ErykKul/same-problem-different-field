MECHANISM: A fixed number of interacting entities, each carrying a position and an orientation angle, are evolved in continuous time by numerically integrating coupled overdamped stochastic differential equations. The positional update sums a self-driving term along the orientation, a short-range repulsive pairwise force derived from a truncated potential evaluated over neighboring entities, and additive Gaussian white noise. The orientation update sums a constant intrinsic rotation rate, a distance-weighted steering torque from entities lying inside a forward angular sector, an averaging torque toward the mean orientation of nearby entities, and angular noise. The steering and averaging contributions are separately normalized by neighbor counts, making the pairwise interaction asymmetric. The system is advanced with a fixed time step after an equilibration interval, and trajectories are recorded over many steps. Control parameters are swept and the emergent organization is classified by a clustering fraction equal to the largest connected group size over the total count. Aggregate descriptors are then computed: a global alignment magnitude, a pair-distance histogram normalized against uniform density, the time-averaged squared displacement, a squared angular displacement, and a temporal orientation autocorrelation fitted to a decaying oscillation. Additional cluster-level quantities such as a moment-of-inertia-based shape asymmetry and a cross-product-based rotation rate are evaluated to separate the regimes. A rescaling of one control parameter collapses the alignment curves onto a single master curve.
