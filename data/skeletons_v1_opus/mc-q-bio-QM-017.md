MECHANISM: The procedure reconstructs the time-evolving deformation of a thin curved structure between two extreme configurations by coupling a continuum-mechanics solve with an image-alignment optimization. A mid-surface is extracted from a segmented reference configuration and discretized into a triangulated shell mesh. A nonlinear, nearly-incompressible hyperelastic constitutive law, whose stored-energy density separates shape-changing and volume-changing parts with an exponential stiffening term, is assigned, and prescribed boundary displacements plus a surface pressure load are applied. The discretized nonlinear equilibrium equations are solved with an implicit time-stepping scheme to generate a sequence of intermediate deformed meshes approximating the target configuration. Selected intermediate shapes are rasterized to binary fields and used as structural priors that guide a multi-resolution deformable registration: an affine map followed by a dense displacement field is optimized to maximize an intensity-similarity criterion between successive fields, and the composed transformation propagates the reference labeling onto the target frame. Local strain is then computed: per-element relative area change, and a finite-strain tensor formed from the deformation gradient, are summed across the simulation step and the registration step, and contracted into scalar total and shape-only (deviatoric) magnitudes. Accuracy is assessed by surface-distance between the propagated and reference labelings and a mesh-refinement convergence check, and strain summaries are compared across groups of structures.
