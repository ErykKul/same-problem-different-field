MECHANISM: Multiple synchronized streams of continuous multichannel signals are recorded from several interacting entities under several conditions, before and after an intervention. For one signal type, each entity's trajectory is reduced to a time-resolved scaling exponent: sub-movements are isolated by velocity zero-crossings, and within sliding time windows a power-law slope between mean speed and displacement is fit on log-log axes, yielding a one-dimensional exponent time series per entity. Pairwise coupling is then quantified by an ordinal-pattern method: each scalar series is converted into a sequence of short order patterns encoding local up-down shapes, and the coupling between two series is the fraction of matching patterns evaluated at small positive lags, repeated over sliding windows to yield a time-varying adjacency between nodes. For the other signal type, this all-pairs pattern matching is applied across all channel pairs within each entity to build a time-varying graph per entity, and inter-entity coupling is summarized by an index that multiplies how often a given edge co-occurs in both graphs by the fidelity of that co-occurrence relative to the edge's own frequency, producing a weighted between-entity network. Random edge reshufflings provide a significance threshold so only edges above chance are kept. Summary scalars such as mean edge weight and weighted node degree are extracted per condition. Distributions of these summaries are compared across conditions and the intervention using analysis-of-variance and rank-based two-sample tests with multiple-comparison correction. The pipeline therefore estimates scaling exponents and pairwise coupling networks and then tests whether their summaries shift after the intervention.
