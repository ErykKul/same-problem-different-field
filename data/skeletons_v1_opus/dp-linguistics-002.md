MECHANISM: Given two finite ordered strings of discrete symbols, a pairwise dissimilarity is computed as the optimal global alignment score under a fixed substitution-cost table and a gap penalty. A two-dimensional table indexed by prefixes of the two sequences is filled, where each cell stores the best cumulative score of aligning the corresponding prefixes. The boundary cells are initialized as cumulative gap penalties, and each interior cell is set to the maximum over three candidate transitions: a diagonal step adding the match or mismatch cost, and two axis-aligned steps each adding the gap penalty. By the principle of optimality, the final corner cell holds the optimal score for the full sequences, which is taken as the score of interest. This quadratic-cost recurrence is evaluated independently for every unordered pair drawn from a large collection of sequences, exploiting the symmetry of the score so only the upper-triangular set of pairs is processed. The computation is mapped to a massively parallel device by assigning each pair to one independent unit of work, with a closed-form index-to-pair inversion (solving a quadratic for the row and deriving the column) so each unit locates its operands from a single linear index. The resulting all-pairs scores form a weighted complete graph, which is then normalized by sequence length and passed to downstream graph operations such as community partitioning, force-directed layout, and shortest-path queries. The dominant computation is the repeated fill of the prefix-alignment table across all pairs.
