MECHANISM: Observed nonnegative integer counts are indexed by two ordered axes (a within-unit position and a time index) and split across several mutually exclusive categories whose per-cell totals must sum coherently to an overall total. The overall count in each cell is modeled as a count-distributed variable whose rate is an exposure times a latent intensity, and the vector of category counts given the total is modeled as an overdispersed compositional-count distribution governed by a concentration parameter and a probability vector on the simplex. The log of the overall intensity is decomposed into an intercept plus additive smooth effects of the two ordered axes, in one variant, or into an intercept plus a smooth position effect plus a position-loading times a shared latent time-factor, in another variant. The category probabilities are obtained by a normalized-exponential transform of per-category latent linear predictors built from the same additive or factor structure. Smoothness across the ordered axes is imposed by second- or first-order Gaussian random-walk priors on the effect sequences, with diffuse priors on the remaining scalars. The joint likelihood factorizes over cells into a count component for totals and a compositional-count component for the category split, and the full posterior, which has no closed form, is explored by gradient-guided Hamiltonian trajectory sampling with automatic step adaptation, run as multiple chains and checked by a between-chain convergence diagnostic. Forecasts are produced by propagating the random-walk dynamics of the time effects forward for each posterior draw, recombining the projected total intensity and category shares into coherent per-category rates, and accuracy is summarized by held-out mean absolute and root-mean-squared errors against alternative methods.
