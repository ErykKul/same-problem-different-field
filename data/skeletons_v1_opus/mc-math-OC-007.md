MECHANISM: A coupled system is posed over abstract function spaces, combining one evolution equation in a continuous spatial domain with two pointwise-in-time inequality conditions, each requiring an unknown to satisfy a one-sided inequality against all test elements of a convex admissible set. The argument decomposes the system and treats sub-problems sequentially. For a fixed value of the other unknowns, a stationary inequality is shown to have a unique solution by invoking strong monotonicity and Lipschitz continuity of the principal operator together with convexity and lower semicontinuity of the accompanying functional. Continuity in time of this solution is established by subtracting two instances of the inequality, exploiting the monotonicity coercivity bound on one side and Lipschitz bounds on the data on the other, yielding a quantitative estimate of the solution difference. A solution operator is then defined and shown to be an almost-history-dependent contraction: its increment is bounded by a contraction term plus a time-integral of the increment, with the contraction constant strictly below one under a smallness condition relating the monotonicity constant to the perturbation constants. A fixed-point theorem for such operators delivers a unique fixed point. The evolution equation with a mixed boundary condition is handled by an existence-and-estimate result giving a unique bounded weak solution depending continuously on its source. Chaining these results, the full coupled map is again shown to be a contraction under a sharper smallness inequality, so the whole system has a unique solution. The abstract result is then instantiated on a concrete continuum problem to certify well-posedness.
