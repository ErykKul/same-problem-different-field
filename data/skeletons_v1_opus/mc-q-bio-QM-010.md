MECHANISM: A collective unit composed of two classes of binding sites switches between two global conformations, and the probability of the active conformation as a function of a scalar input is given in closed form by a two-state allosteric expression: a ratio whose terms are products over each class of one-plus-input-over-affinity raised to the site count, weighted by a baseline conformational bias. From this response curve three scalar performance summaries are computed. The first treats the input-to-binary-output map as a discrete memoryless channel on a logarithmic grid of input values and computes its capacity, the maximum mutual information over input distributions, by an iterative reweighting scheme that repeatedly recomputes the output marginal, forms a per-input divergence score, and multiplicatively updates and renormalizes the input distribution until convergence, also returning the optimizing input distribution. The second summary is the closed-form magnitude of the difference between the zero-input and infinite-input response limits. The third is a closed-form local steepness exponent obtained as a logarithmic derivative of the normalized response at its midpoint. All three summaries are evaluated over a seven-dimensional grid built by varying each model parameter around a reference operating point, and local flatness at each operating point is assessed by the Euclidean norm of a finite-difference gradient in mixed log/linear coordinates. Pairwise correlations among the summaries across the grid are also reported.
