MECHANISM: A finite population of interconnected dynamical units is each described by a set of coupled nonlinear ordinary differential equations for a primary state variable, several auxiliary gating variables with voltage- or concentration-dependent steady states and time constants, and an internal accumulator variable. Units of two types are wired into a fixed directed network, with directed coupling terms entering each unit's equations through saturating activation functions of the sending unit's state. A few global coupling-strength and external-drive parameters are designated as the control parameters of interest, and an optional periodic forcing term is injected into one population. The full system is integrated forward in time with an adaptive higher-order step method after discarding an initial transient, over a grid of the control parameters. From the resulting trajectories, a summary aggregate signal is formed as a weighted sum of coupling inputs, both this signal and individual unit traces are band-pass filtered, and instantaneous phases are extracted by an analytic-signal transform. A stroboscopic return map of one phase relative to another is built, its plane is partitioned, and transition rates between a locked region and the rest quantify intermittent coordination. A linear dimensionality reduction on a slow auxiliary variable across units counts components needed to capture a variance threshold, classifying regimes, and a normalized dispersion of inter-event intervals classifies firing patterns. Parameter regions whose summaries match a reference are flagged.
