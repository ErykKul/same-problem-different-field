MECHANISM: A sequential two-option allocation game is analyzed in which, each round, a decision rule places probability mass on two alternatives, an adversary reveals bounded losses, and the cumulative shortfall against the best fixed alternative is to be controlled over a known horizon. The problem is reduced to tracking a single nonnegative integer-valued gap between the two cumulative losses, and the shortfall is rewritten as a sum of the mass on the trailing alternative times gap increments, a discrete path integral against a reflected random walk. A continuous-time surrogate replaces the random walk by a reflected Brownian path and the shortfall by a stochastic integral of the allocation function against that path. Using the stochastic change-of-variables identity, the surrogate shortfall equals a potential function evaluated along the path provided that potential satisfies a backward diffusion (time-reversed heat) equation; solving this with appropriate boundary conditions yields a closed-form allocation expressed through the complementary error function, whose continuous shortfall is exactly the square root of horizon over two-pi. The continuous solution is then discretized by taking a finite difference of the potential in the gap variable, and a discrete analogue of the change-of-variables identity bounds the resulting shortfall by the continuous value plus accumulated discretization-error terms. Each error term is shown to decay polynomially in the remaining horizon, so the summed error is bounded by a constant, giving a total shortfall of the optimal leading term plus a small additive constant, with constant-time per-round updates. Concavity arguments extend the guarantee from binary to bounded real losses. The work is a closed-form derivation and worst-case bound proof bridging a discrete random walk and a continuous diffusion.
