MECHANISM: Consider a population of entities arranged as nodes of a network, each carrying a discrete state drawn from a small finite set plus a binary protective tag. Two coupled stochastic processes run in discrete time: one governs transitions among the categorical states via contact-driven and spontaneous rates, the other governs adoption of the protective tag through a payoff-comparison rule. For each entity, the payoff of adopting versus not adopting is accumulated over its neighbors using a fixed interaction matrix and augmented by a population-level aggregate (current category densities weighted by perceived costs and benefits); the adoption probability is a logistic function of the payoff difference. The coupled dynamics are closed into deterministic per-node recursions for the marginal probabilities of occupying each state, by treating neighbors as independent and multiplying per-neighbor non-transmission probabilities (a mean-field product over contacts weighted by a contact matrix derived from node degrees). This produces a system of nonlinear difference equations, one block per node, with a normalization constraint across states. The equations are iterated forward numerically from an initial seeding to trace the time evolution of category densities. Parameter sweeps over transmission strengths, mutation/branching rate, adoption cost, efficacy, perceived risk, and a conformity weight are run to locate critical thresholds separating contained from sustained spreading, and to produce frequency distributions and sensitivity heatmaps of final outcomes. No estimation is performed; the method is forward simulation of the coupled recursions.
