MECHANISM: A high-dimensional state vector evolving under a nonlinear dynamical model is estimated sequentially by fusing model forecasts with noisy partial observations. An ensemble of state realizations is propagated forward through the model to form a forecast ensemble, from which an empirical mean and a sample covariance of the deviations are obtained. At each analysis time a linear update shifts each ensemble member toward the observations, weighted by a gain matrix formed from the forecast covariance, an observation operator, and an observation-error covariance. To avoid forming and inverting the full covariance, the update is written in the low-dimensional ensemble subspace: the relevant matrix is factored via singular value decomposition and inverted on a small block, yielding a compact transform applied to the ensemble. Stochastic variants perturb the observations per member with sampled noise, while deterministic variants apply square-root transforms to the deviations so the analysis covariance matches the target without perturbations. Unobserved parameters are estimated jointly by augmenting the state vector, with bounded stochastic evolution and inflation used to sustain spread and prevent collapse. Covariance inflation and spatially tapered localization mitigate sampling error and spurious long-range correlations from limited ensemble size. The forecast step is independent across members and thus parallelized, while the analysis introduces coupling through the shared gain. The output is an updated ensemble approximating the posterior distribution of states and parameters over repeated assimilation cycles.
