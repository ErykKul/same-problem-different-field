MECHANISM: A large collection of interacting binary variables is governed by an energy function defined as a sum, over many stored reference patterns, of a power of the inner product between the configuration and each pattern, plus a coupling to an external driving field. The configuration evolves by a continuous-time stochastic process in which single variables flip with rates set by the energy change and a temperature parameter, satisfying detailed balance at fixed temperature. To make this tractable at large size, low-dimensional order parameters are defined as the normalized overlaps of the configuration with each reference pattern. The equilibrium distribution's normalizing sum is rewritten by inserting integral constraints for the order parameters, decoupling the variables and reducing the sum to an integral dominated, as the size grows, by the stationary point of an effective action, which yields self-consistency equations for the order parameters and a closed expression for the free energy. The same reduction applied to the stochastic dynamics shows the order parameters concentrate and obey a small closed set of deterministic ordinary differential equations, which are integrated numerically. From the trajectories and the equilibrium free energy, the work done by driving and the irreversible entropy production are computed as differences of free energy and accumulated driving contributions. Sweeping the nonlinearity order, temperature, corruption level, and driving protocol reveals attractor structure, a spurious fixed point, recovery accuracy, relaxation times, and tradeoffs between dissipation, accuracy, and speed.
