MECHANISM: A scalar field over a bounded spatial region evolves under an evolution equation combining a diffusion operator acting on the spatial Laplacian with a saturating quadratic source term, where the time evolution is governed by a nonlocal memory operator defined through convolution with a singular power-law kernel. The equation is recast into an equivalent convolved form so that the nonlocal time operator naturally encodes the initial state. Existence of weak solutions is established by projecting the problem onto a finite-dimensional subspace spanned by eigenfunctions of the spatial operator, yielding a finite system of coupled nonlocal-in-time ordinary equations for the expansion coefficients. The projected solution is tested against itself to derive energy identities, and a chain inequality for the nonlocal time operator bounds the time-derivative of the squared norm. Convolution with the conjugate kernel, together with Holder, Young, interpolation, and embedding inequalities, produces a self-referential integral bound on the norm. A nonlinear convolution-type comparison inequality then yields a uniform a priori bound on a short time interval, and a smallness condition on the initial state extends it to all time. Uniform bounds give weak and strong compactness, and a compact embedding lets one pass to the limit and recover the nonlinear term. The limit is shown to satisfy the variational identity and the initial condition, proving local and global well-posedness. A separate discrete scheme approximates the operator by nonuniform convolution quadrature on a grid and compares trajectories of two competing memory formulations.
