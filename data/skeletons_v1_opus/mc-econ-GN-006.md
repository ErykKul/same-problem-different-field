MECHANISM: A scalar observation arrives each period together with a high-dimensional vector of auxiliary measurements derived from finer-grained data. The goal is to forecast two coupled lower-tail summaries of the conditional distribution: a threshold level and the average magnitude beyond it. The threshold evolves through an autoregressive recursion on a transformed scale, driven by its own lag, a standardized innovation, that innovation squared, and an exogenous signal. A separate nonnegative state, the gap between the two summaries, follows its own recursion driven by its lag and the same exogenous signal. To avoid redundancy among the many correlated auxiliary measurements, each is written as a noisy linear function of a few latent common components plus idiosyncratic noise, and these common components are extracted and fed into both recursions. Because one of the target summaries is not individually identifiable, the two are estimated jointly by minimizing an empirical objective built from a strictly consistent joint scoring rule, combined with a Gaussian discrepancy term for the measurement equations. All states are deterministic functions of past observations given the parameters, so the objective is a recursive function evaluated over the sample and minimized by constrained nonlinear optimization. Estimation is repeated on a rolling window to produce one-step-ahead forecasts. Forecasts are then assessed with coverage and independence diagnostics, and competing models are compared by averaging scoring losses and applying a resampling-based elimination procedure.
DOMAIN: financial econometrics and tail risk forecasting
STRUCTURE: numerical optimization
DATA_OBJECT: sequence or time-series
INFERENCE: maximum likelihood
PROBLEM_FORM: prediction or classification
DISTRIBUTION: heavy-tailed; nonparametric
COMPLEXITY: consistency
