MECHANISM: A population of unit-like emitters is placed along a one-dimensional track, each assigned a tuning profile equal to a bell-shaped function of position with a randomly drawn center and, in some variants, a randomly drawn width and a randomly scaled amplitude, plus small multiplicative noise per spatial bin. A moving point representing the system's location is advanced in discrete time by a stochastic update that adds a constant drift increment plus a Gaussian random increment whose scale encodes a diffusion magnitude, producing a biased random-walk trajectory. At each step the emitters' instantaneous activations are evaluated from their profiles at the current location, and an aggregate directional throughput quantity is accumulated across the population over the trajectory. By comparing constant-drift trajectories against trajectories with added diffusion, and by varying heterogeneity in widths, amplitudes, and spatial clustering of centers, the simulation isolates which ingredient causes the aggregate quantity to decay along the track. Separately, the drift and diffusion magnitudes are estimated empirically from an observed trajectory by partitioning the track into equal bins, computing the mean speed per bin, excising the contiguous low-speed bins, and fitting the remaining motion. The simulated aggregate quantity using the estimated parameters is then checked against an independently computed reference. The overall procedure is a forward stochastic simulation of a noisy transport process driving a population of tuned responders, used to confirm analytical predictions and to rule out alternative explanations.
