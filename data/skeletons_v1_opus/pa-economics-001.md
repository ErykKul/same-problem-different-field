MECHANISM: An entity carries a continuous nonnegative quantity that evolves by a deterministic first-order rule combining a constant inflow, a state-dependent boost, and a decay proportional to the current level, while a binary internal mode switches at random between two states at constant transition rates. The two regimes give two different drift functions for the quantity, bounding it between a minimum and a maximum determined by the rate parameters. The stochastic dynamics are described by a pair of coupled evolution equations for the mode-conditioned probability densities of the quantity, each consisting of a transport (continuity) term from the deterministic drift plus gain and loss terms from inter-mode transitions. Setting the time derivatives to zero yields the stationary regime, and the coupled stationary equations are solved analytically. After nondimensionalizing the quantity by its maximum and introducing dimensionless ratios of the transition rates to the decay rate, the closed-form stationary density is obtained as a two-parameter bounded distribution with a normalizing constant expressed through a special-function integral. Closed-form expressions for the mean and variance follow, depending only on the rate ratios. The resulting shape is examined across parameter regimes and an inequality summary statistic is reported, and the distribution is identified as a special case of a broader parametric family. The dominant computation is closed-form solution of the stationary master equation for a switching stochastic process.
