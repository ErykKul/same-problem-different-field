MECHANISM: The goal is to estimate an unknown probability distribution over latent per-entity parameter vectors, given indirect noisy observations produced by a known nonlinear forward map applied to each entity's parameters plus additive zero-mean noise. The latent vectors are assumed drawn independently from a common unknown distribution, and the observation likelihood integrates the per-entity likelihood against that distribution. A first estimator maximizes the marginal likelihood over the space of all distributions; by a support-point argument the optimum is a discrete distribution placing mass on a finite set of atoms, so the problem reduces to choosing atom locations and nonnegative weights summing to one. With atoms fixed, optimizing the weights is a convex program solved by a primal-dual interior-point method that follows a central path and uses Newton steps with quadratic convergence. The atom set is refined by an adaptive cycle that deletes low-mass atoms and expands surviving atoms by adjoining hypercube vertices, iterating until the likelihood stops increasing. A second estimator treats the unknown distribution as random under a nonparametric prior built from a stick-breaking construction, truncated to finitely many components, yielding a finite mixture with random weights and atoms. Its posterior over weights and atoms is sampled by a blocked Gibbs / Markov-chain Monte Carlo scheme with burn-in and convergence diagnostics, producing posterior summaries and credible intervals for any functional of the distribution. Both estimators return a discrete mixing distribution and are compared against a known ground-truth distribution on simulated data.
