MECHANISM: Each observational unit is itself a discrete distribution over a fixed set of categories, and the goal is to explain many such distributions through a small number of shared latent components. A binary hierarchical partition of the categories is imposed, and by a recursive factorization each distribution is represented bijectively by the conditional split probabilities at the internal nodes. Applying a logit transform to these split probabilities maps each distribution to an unconstrained real vector, removing the simplex constraint. In this transformed space each unit's vector is modeled as a mean plus a linear combination of shared latent factor vectors weighted by unit-specific loadings, so the units share a low-rank structure. The loadings receive a structured prior combining a spatial autoregressive dependence across units, local shrinkage, and a multiplicative shrinkage that lets the effective number of factors grow only as needed. Because each node likelihood is a count-versus-total binomial in the logit parameter, an auxiliary latent-scale augmentation renders the conditional likelihood a Gaussian linear model in the parameters. Conditional on the auxiliary scales, all parameters are sampled with standard Gaussian conjugate updates, yielding a full sampler over the posterior. After sampling, the effective rank is selected from the ordered eigenvalues of the loading cross-product, and rotational and sign ambiguity is resolved by orthogonally aligning posterior draws to a reference. The partition itself is built greedily by recursively choosing splits that maximize the variance of a log-ratio coordinate across units. Predictive fit penalized by predictive variance is compared against mixture-based and parametric alternatives.
