MECHANISM: Given an attributed graph and a query node, the goal is to learn per-node vector representations and then retrieve a fixed-size subset of nodes most consistent with the query. The encoder first decomposes the normalized adjacency operator into exact-distance channels by subtracting consecutive powers of the operator and keeping positive residual entries, so each channel carries only neighbors at one specific hop and avoids cross-hop blending. Each hop channel is split into a smoothing (low-pass) branch that averages neighbor features and a contrasting (high-pass) branch obtained as the feature minus its neighbor average, computed without materializing high-order operators. To control memory, the one-hop operator is approximated by a truncated low-rank factorization, so any power is obtained by raising the diagonal singular factors and projecting features once into the reduced subspace, turning per-hop aggregation into cheap diagonal scalings. Edge-wise attention scores from learned linear projections reweight and row-normalize each channel. The two frequency branches are fused per hop by node-wise attention weights summing to one, hop representations are concatenated and mixed by a multilayer transform or a per-class prototype-gated attention, and the unified embedding is trained by minimizing a categorical negative-log-likelihood loss against node labels. At query time, edges are signed by thresholding embedding cosine similarity to build a positive subgraph; a breadth-first expansion from the query, augmented by similarity-based jumps when stuck, or a ranking score blending cosine similarity with a connectivity reward weighted by the estimated label-agreement ratio, selects the top scoring nodes. Theoretical results bound retained-edge triangle support and give margin conditions under which hop selection preserves label-consistent similarity.
