MECHANISM: Each entity is an attributed graph that must be made consumable by a sequence model. A vocabulary of recurring connected subgraphs is learned by an iterative frequency-driven pair-merging procedure over a corpus, producing an ordered list of merge operations whose prefix length defines a tunable granularity; a closure step enumerates connection-aware single-node tokens to guarantee full coverage and avoid unknown-token breakpoints. At a chosen granularity, the merge prefix partitions a graph into non-overlapping fragments, and these fragment-nodes are linearized into a deterministic causal order by a breadth-first traversal rooted at the largest fragment, yielding a per-scale token subsequence. Subsequences at several increasing granularities are concatenated fine-to-coarse with separators to form one sequence, so that coarse tokens are predicted conditioned on uncorrupted fine-scale prefixes. A decoder-only attention stack with a causal mask is pretrained by minimizing the negative log-likelihood of each next token given its prefix, with multiple augmented views per entity. For downstream tasks, a fixed auxiliary descriptor vector is linearly projected and both prepended to the inputs and concatenated with the terminal-token representation, then fed to a small head trained with cross-entropy for categorical targets or squared error for continuous targets. An information inequality argues the uncorrupted causal context carries at least as much mutual information about a target as a stochastically masked context, and the per-update fraction of supervised tokens is higher than under masked training. Attention mass on differential versus shared tokens is measured to interpret sensitivity to small structural edits.
