MECHANISM: To compare two nested parametric models, the marginal probability of the data under each is approximated by a second-order expansion of the log of the likelihood-times-prior about its maximizing parameter; because the gradient vanishes there, the remaining Gaussian integral evaluates in closed form to the peak value times a factor proportional to the inverse square-root determinant of the negative Hessian. Taking logs turns the ratio of marginals into a difference of maximized log-likelihoods, a dimension term, and a half-log-determinant-ratio of the curvature matrices. This motivates augmenting the standard maximized-likelihood-plus-dimension-times-log-sample-size penalty with an added log-determinant of the observed information matrix, so the score charges a model not only for parameter count but for the curvature and information geometry at its optimum. The added term is derived explicitly for a balanced grouped-mean model, where the information matrix is block-diagonal at the optimum and its log-determinant reduces to terms in the group count, per-group size, and the plug-in variance estimate, yielding a data-dependent penalty that grows when fit is unusually tight. The criterion is proven to select the smaller true model with probability approaching one as the sample grows. Selection is then evaluated by Monte-Carlo: many synthetic datasets with added spurious variables are fit across linear, grouped, binary, and count families, and the fraction of correct selections is compared against alternative criteria.
