MECHANISM: The task is to predict an ordered categorical rating for each pair drawn from two large finite index sets, from a sparse set of observed pairs with known ratings, and to attach a confidence to each prediction. Each of the two index identifiers is mapped through a learned lookup table to a dense low-dimensional vector (an embedding), replacing high-dimensional sparse indicator inputs. The two dense vectors are concatenated and passed through several fully connected nonlinear layers with interleaved regularization dropout, allowing the model to capture nonlinear interactions between the two embedded entities. The final layer has one output unit per rating category with a normalized exponential activation, so the model emits a discrete probability vector over categories that sums to one. Parameters (embedding tables and layer weights) are fit by minimizing categorical cross-entropy against the observed one-hot rating labels via stochastic gradient updates. At inference the predicted rating is the category with maximal probability and its associated probability serves as a reliability score. Recommendations are produced by filtering predictions above a category threshold and ordering the survivors by their reliability scores. Quality is assessed by precision and recall of recommended items at varying list lengths and relevance thresholds, by per-category prediction accuracy, and by a precision-versus-coverage trade-off as the acceptance threshold is varied. Note the model is a multilayer nonlinear classifier over learned embeddings, not a linear low-rank factorization of the interaction matrix.
