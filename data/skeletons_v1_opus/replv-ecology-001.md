MECHANISM: A low-dimensional continuous dynamical system is given by coupled nonlinear first-order ordinary differential equations in which each nonnegative coordinate changes at a rate equal to itself times an affine function of all coordinates. The aim is to characterize the global behavior of all trajectories in the whole nonnegative state space by constructing an explicit scalar potential that never increases along the flow. The construction posits that the gradient of the unknown potential equals minus a coordinate-dependent matrix multiplied by the velocity field, and the matrix is chosen so that two conditions hold: the resulting gradient is curl-free, ensuring a consistent potential exists, and its directional derivative along the flow is everywhere nonpositive, vanishing only on limit sets. A diagonal choice of the matrix satisfies both, and integrating the gradient gives the potential in closed form as a low-degree polynomial. The interior fixed point is found by setting all rates to zero, and the definiteness of the potential's Hessian there partitions parameter space into qualitatively distinct regimes such as a single globally attracting state, two competing attractors, or a degenerate manifold of minima. For a system that does not admit this gradient structure, auxiliary invariants formed from the product and the sum of coordinates are introduced; their time derivatives are combined so that a sign-definite quantity emerges, and a closed-form function with nonpositive directional derivative is identified, again yielding the limiting sets. The velocity field is finally decomposed into a symmetric dissipative part and an antisymmetric conservative part to expose whether motion is conserved or contracting on the invariant surface. The whole analysis is exact and deterministic, classifying attractors and ruling out or permitting cyclic limit behavior analytically.
