MECHANISM: A noisy real-valued sequence is mapped to the frequency domain by a discrete orthogonal transform, and the squared magnitude of the coefficients at each frequency index defines a per-frequency energy quantity. A parametric family of smooth functions of frequency is proposed for the expected value of this energy, governed by a few shape parameters that interpolate between flat, power-law-decay, and steeper-decay regimes while keeping total energy finite. The per-frequency energy is modeled as the baseline function multiplied by independent scaled chi-square(2) random variables, giving a multiplicative-noise stochastic model of the spectrum. The baseline parameters are fit to observed spectra by weighted least-squares with frequency-dependent weights. The same baseline is derived as the transform of an integral-of-exponentials autocorrelation arising from a maximum-entropy equilibrium over a continuum of relaxation modes, and is also realized as the limit of a sum of many first-order autoregressive subprocesses as step size and mode spacing tend to zero. For inference, threshold curves parallel to the fitted baseline are computed from the chi-square quantiles, and any coefficient exceeding the threshold is declared a detected periodic component via a per-frequency hypothesis test. An alternative test forms a ratio of normalized energy at a target index to averaged normalized energy over a reference index set, which follows an F-distribution under the null. Detector accuracy is compared against rival statistics using bootstrap-resampled null distributions and receiver-operating-characteristic curves with pooled significance testing.
