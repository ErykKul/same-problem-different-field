MECHANISM: Multichannel grid-structured signals are assigned to one of several discrete categories by a trained multilayer feature-extraction network. Each input is preprocessed by edge trimming, intensity gamma adjustment, local contrast enhancement, and region-dependent enhancement, and a single-channel intensity map is derived as a fixed weighted combination of the channels. The input is passed through two parallel processing streams: one is a deep stack of residual blocks, in which each block adds its input to a learned nonlinear transformation so that gradients propagate through identity shortcuts, producing high-level features; the other applies several parallel convolutional branches with different dilation factors to the intensity map and reweights channels within each branch by a squeeze-and-excitation gate computed from pooled statistics passed through a sigmoid. The two streams' feature maps are aligned and concatenated, then reduced in channel dimension, refined by a depthwise-separable residual unit and a lightweight cross-channel attention, regularized by dropout, pooled by global averaging, normalized, and mapped through a linear layer with a softmax to class probabilities. Parameters are optimized by gradient descent with backpropagation minimizing a class-balanced focal loss that downweights easy examples, with early layers frozen for initial epochs then unfrozen. Data are split by category into training and held-out test portions, and performance is assessed with cross-validation and a confusion matrix together with accuracy, precision, recall, and harmonic-mean scores against manual labels.
