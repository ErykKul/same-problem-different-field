MECHANISM: Several co-evolving nonnegative continuous quantities are observed over a long ordered index, and for each a dispersion summary is first computed within a sliding window from within-period extremes and gaps. Each quantity is then modeled by an autoregressive predictor that mixes its own lagged values aggregated over three nested timescales (immediate, short, long averages). In a first stage, each series' own-lag predictor is fit by ordinary least squares, producing high-persistence coefficients and a fitted own-component. In a second stage, the residual of each series is regressed on the multi-timescale lagged values of all other series, and these cross-coefficients are estimated by minimizing squared error plus a combined absolute-value and squared-magnitude penalty, with penalty strength chosen by sequential time-respecting cross-validation; the penalty drives most cross-coefficients exactly to zero, yielding a sparse interaction matrix. The combined two-stage model gives one-step-ahead forecasts evaluated out-of-sample by root-mean-squared error against a parsimonious own-only benchmark. To trace propagation, joint shocks are applied simultaneously to chosen subsets of series, correlated according to the estimated residual covariance rather than an imposed ordering, and the system is rolled forward by recomputing the multi-timescale features at each horizon and applying the fitted coefficients; the difference between shocked and baseline paths is the dynamic response. Uncertainty bands are obtained by a block resampling of the residual series that preserves serial dependence, refitting the cross-stage many times, and taking percentiles of the resulting response distribution, with significance declared when an interval excludes zero.
