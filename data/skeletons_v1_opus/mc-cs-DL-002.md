MECHANISM: For a large collection of entities, each carrying a count of incoming links, a composite influence indicator is constructed. Each incoming link is first down-weighted by the reciprocal of the number of outgoing links of its source, yielding a fractional contribution that normalizes for differing source behaviors. Items are then ranked by their accumulated fractional counts within strata defined by year and type, and each item is assigned to one of a few top-percentile classes. The indicator for a group is a closed-form weighted sum over these classes, multiplying a fixed per-class weight by the number of group members in that class, with an optional size-normalized variant dividing by the group's member count. This indicator is computed over fixed publication and citation time windows and compared against two competing mean-ratio indicators defined as totals divided by denominators. All indicators are rescaled by min-max normalization to a common range. Agreement and association between indicators are quantified using a rank-correlation coefficient and a concordance coefficient, and their distributions are summarized by central tendency, dispersion, and empirical cumulative distribution curves with year-over-year differences. A two-axis quadrant scheme contrasts scale and per-unit versions. A cross-source category alignment is examined via a co-occurrence network thresholded by shared membership. The dominant computation is stratified ranking into percentile classes followed by weighted aggregation and descriptive comparison.
