MECHANISM: A parametric multilayer function is trained to invert a gradual corruption process applied to entities represented as high-dimensional arrays. Each entity is partitioned into blocks, each block is linearly projected onto a low-dimensional subspace spanned by leading directions of its second-moment structure, and isotropic noise is added in that subspace according to a time-indexed schedule. The corrupted low-dimensional code is mapped back to the original space, encoded again, and a parametric predictor conditioned on the noise level estimates the clean representation, which is then decoded. Training minimizes a weighted sum of squared residuals in the projected subspace. A dispersion penalty derived from a logarithm of an average of exponentiated negative squared pairwise distances among batch representations is added to spread representations apart. A subset of entities is marked by overwriting a fixed sub-region with a fixed pattern; for these, the projected codes are forcibly shifted to coincide with those of one designated target entity, and an additional composite objective enforces matching at the code level, along the corruption trajectory, and at the reconstructed-output level via squared error. Optimization proceeds by stochastic first-order gradient updates over many passes, alternating the clean objective and the marked-entity objective by sample type. Evaluation compares output distributions and reconstruction errors against baselines and tests resistance to detection procedures.
