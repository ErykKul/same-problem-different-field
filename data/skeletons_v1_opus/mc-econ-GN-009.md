MECHANISM: Each observation is a binary menu of two alternatives with a recorded selection, and a fixed finite library of candidate transformations is given, each mapping a menu into a perceived menu. For every observation and every transformation, an admissibility check determines whether the perceived selected alternative strictly dominates the perceived rival under a fixed partial-order benchmark. An assignment chooses exactly one admissible transformation per observation, inducing usage frequencies across the library; the spread of these frequencies is summarized by a sum-of-squared-shares concentration functional. The central quantity is the maximum of this convex concentration over all admissible assignments, a combinatorial optimization solved exactly via a mixed-integer quadratic program and approximately via a greedy pass over many random orderings of high-coverage transformations with restarts. Two per-transformation diagnostics are computed: one repeatedly deletes transformations in varied orders and records how often a transformation remains indispensable to retain the optimum, and one measures the drop in the optimum when a transformation is removed. Statistical significance is assessed by a conditional permutation test: holding fixed the count of left-versus-right selections, the selection labels are permuted across menus to form a reference distribution of the concentration under label-exchangeable random choice, against which the observed value is compared. Asymptotic power of the test is argued.
