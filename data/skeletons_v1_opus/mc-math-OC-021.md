MECHANISM: The goal is to estimate the expectation of a nonnegative functional of a continuous-state stochastic process by changing the underlying probability measure to reduce estimator variance. A convexity (certainty-equivalence) argument shows that applying a strictly convex transformation, taking expectation, and inverting yields a bound that becomes an equality precisely when the reweighted functional is almost surely constant, identifying the zero-variance target measure; two transformations are used, an exponential (log of a moment generating function) and a power (root of a higher moment). The functionals are integrals along trajectories of a diffusion defined up to an unbounded random first-exit time from a domain, and the change of measure is realized by adding a state-feedback drift, with the corresponding likelihood ratio given by an exponential martingale. Each variance-minimization is recast as a stochastic optimal control problem whose value function solves a nonlinear dynamic-programming (Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman) partial differential equation with boundary data on the exit set; the optimal feedback is the gradient of the value function, and the two formulations reduce respectively to a linear-quadratic and a risk-sensitive control. A logarithmic/transform change of the dependent variable maps these nonlinear equations to a linear elliptic boundary-value problem solvable by a probabilistic (Feynman-Kac) representation, exposing the equivalence of the control value and the quantity of interest. Crucially, the quantity is read directly from the value function without trajectory reweighting. The value function and optimal policy are computed by an iterative policy-improvement scheme alternating policy evaluation and greedy update, with a proof of convergence to the optimum (unconditional in the log case, requiring control regularization in the power case). Benchmark exit/first-passage problems validate the predicted monotonicity and convergence.
