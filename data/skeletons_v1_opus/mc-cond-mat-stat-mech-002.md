MECHANISM: A family of large symmetric operators is defined on a set of sites: off-diagonal couplings between any two sites decay exponentially in their shortest-path distance along a fixed sparse backbone embedded in the complete set, while diagonal entries are independent random draws of tunable strength. The couplings are either deterministic functions of distance or random with an exponentially decaying envelope. The competition between the exponentially growing count of sites at a given distance and the exponentially decaying coupling is analyzed, including operator-norm estimates that bound where one term dominates. The operators are diagonalized numerically for many random realizations to obtain eigenvalues and eigenvectors. Several diagnostics are computed from the spectrum and states: the ratio of consecutive eigenvalue gaps, moments of eigenvector amplitudes summed over sites as generalized participation sums, and distance-resolved two-point amplitude correlations in both an averaged and a typical (log-averaged) form. Scaling of these quantities with system size is fit to extract exponents and locate transitions via crossings across sizes. Complementarily, an analytical self-consistent recursion approximates the imaginary part of a diagonal resolvent element through a renormalized perturbative expansion, giving an order parameter. The two approaches yield a phase boundary in the plane of disorder strength and decay length. A finite-size scaling ansatz for the participation sums characterizes the transition's universality and critical exponents.
