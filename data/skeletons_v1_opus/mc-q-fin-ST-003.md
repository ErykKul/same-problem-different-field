MECHANISM: Two streams of event times on the line are modeled as a stationary bivariate point process, and the object of interest is the offset at which the two streams are most strongly cross-associated. A second-order summary, the ratio of the cross-intensity at a lag to the product of the marginal rates, is taken as the target curve, and the quantity of interest is defined as the location of its sharpest peak. An existing bucketed cross-count statistic is shown to be, asymptotically, a discretized kernel-smoothed estimate of this curve scaled by a constant, so its peak-locating rule is reinterpreted as an estimator of the peak location with a resolution-limited convergence rate equal to the bucket width. The paper instead proposes a kernel estimator of the curve formed by an all-pairs sum over events of a scaled kernel evaluated at the time difference minus the candidate lag, normalized by the rates, and estimates the parameter by maximizing this smoothed curve. Under regularity and strong-mixing conditions, with the peak modeled as a power-type cusp that may diverge, the maximizer is shown to be consistent with an explicit finite-sample concentration bound and a convergence rate governed by the cusp exponent, and a matching minimax lower bound proves near-optimality. The smoothing bandwidth is chosen adaptively by a pairwise-comparison rule that selects the smallest bandwidth whose peak estimate stays close to those of all larger bandwidths, attaining the optimal rate without knowing the cusp exponent. Monte-Carlo replications over branching and cluster point-process models measure root-mean-square error, and a high-frequency application recovers physically meaningful offsets.
