MECHANISM: A large synthetic population of entities is generated by repeatedly drawing each entity's attributes from prescribed parametric distributions, including log-normal draws for one characteristic quantity, a power-law draw for another, and exponential or radial profiles for spatial coordinates. Generation proceeds iteratively, accumulating draws until the number of entities passing a reference detectability filter matches an observed anchor count, which calibrates the total latent population size. For each generated entity, derived quantities are computed from its sampled attributes through deterministic physical formulas, including propagation effects that depend on path length through a spatially varying medium. Several competing detection channels are then evaluated, each defining a signal-to-noise ratio as a closed-form function of the entity's quantities and instrument parameters. An entity is flagged as detected by a channel when its signal-to-noise exceeds a fixed threshold. Counts of detections and of pairwise overlaps between channels are tabulated across the whole population. The procedure is repeated while sweeping instrument configuration parameters such as resolution and bandwidth to locate settings that maximize the count uniquely recovered by one channel. Sensitivity of detection counts to each swept parameter is then analyzed, including the regime where a resolution scale crosses an intrinsic scale of the signal. Resulting fractions estimate how many entities each channel recovers, especially those missed by the conventional channel.
