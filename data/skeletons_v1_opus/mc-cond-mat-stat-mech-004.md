MECHANISM: One studies a linear operator formed as a coefficient-weighted sum of local generating operators, all of which have entries that are rational numbers in a fixed orthonormal basis. Two distinct ways of decomposing the ambient vector space into invariant blocks are compared. The first computes the characteristic polynomial of the operator at rational coefficient values and factors it into irreducible factors over the rationals, each irreducible factor corresponding to an invariant block whose dimension equals the factor degree, with a uniqueness guarantee for this factorization. The second forms the associative algebra generated by the local operators together with its commutant, the set of operators commuting with every generator, and decomposes the space into joint irreducible representations of the algebra and its commutant, yielding blocks possibly carrying a degeneracy multiplicity. A partial order between the two block decompositions is defined by containment of one family of blocks inside the other. The central result proves that whenever every generator of the center, the intersection of the algebra and its commutant, has only rational eigenvalues, the polynomial-factorization blocks refine the representation-theoretic blocks. The proof writes the operator as block diagonal in the representation basis, expresses each block projector as a product of spectral interpolation factors built from center eigenvalues, shows these projectors are rational, hence each block characteristic polynomial is rational, and invokes uniqueness of irreducible factorization to force containment. Corollaries handle the case where blocks coincide with basis vectors, and a proposition shows that strict refinement forces the finer blocks to depend on the operator coefficients. The claims are then checked on several concrete operator families, including one with irrational center eigenvalues where the refinement direction reverses.
