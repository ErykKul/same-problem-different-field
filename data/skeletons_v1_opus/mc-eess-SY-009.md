MECHANISM: A sequential decision problem over a finite set of target locations is modeled as a Markov decision process whose states pair a current location with a binary success flag and whose actions choose the next location to attempt. Each attempt succeeds with a location-specific probability and otherwise leaves the agent unparked, with additive negative rewards counting traversal time, waiting time, and a final access time on success. The objective is to minimize expected cumulative time to a terminal success state, solved by value-to-go recursion. Closed-form value expressions are derived for a single-location persistent strategy, where the number of attempts until success is a geometric random variable so the expected cost is the reciprocal of the success probability times the wait cost. A regime analysis gives sufficient conditions under which committing to the single best location is optimal versus when cycling through a cluster of nearby locations is better, the latter using the complement-product joint success probability across the cluster. A sensitivity inequality bounds how much the per-location probabilities may change before the optimal choice flips, interpreted as a multi-agent equilibrium condition. Several closed-form updates express how one agent's success probability shifts due to other agents competing for the same or adjacent locations. Estimation error of a time-varying probability sampled by an inhomogeneous Poisson observation stream is derived as a closed-form expectation for linear and exponential drift, and validated against a bounded random walk and real occupancy data; heuristic finite-lookahead policies are compared by simulation.
