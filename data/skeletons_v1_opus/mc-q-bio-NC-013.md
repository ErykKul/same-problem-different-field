MECHANISM: A layered nonlinear map from inputs to outputs is trained without exact reverse-mode differentiation by estimating, at each layer, the sensitivity of the output to that layer's activations from random probes. The key structural observation is that the relevant rows of the per-layer sensitivity operator lie approximately in the same low-dimensional subspace as the activations themselves, and that this subspace's dimension grows slowly with layer width. Exploiting this, a per-layer orthonormal basis for the activation subspace is tracked online by an incremental low-rank decomposition. Periodically, a small random vector is drawn in the low-dimensional coordinates, mapped into the full activation space through the basis, injected as a perturbation, and the resulting output change is recorded; a feedback operator is updated by a running average of the outer product of the perturbation and the output change. Forward parameters are then updated by a local rule using a surrogate error formed by passing the output error back through the current feedback operator and an elementwise nonlinearity derivative. An analysis shows the feedback operator converges to the true sensitivity operator pre-multiplied by the perturbation covariance, so restricting probes to the subspace yields a preconditioned surrogate whose alignment-error variance scales with the subspace dimension rather than the full width, giving better early alignment and a larger effective step whenever enough gradient energy lies in the subspace. The same subspace-restricted probing is also applied as a low-rank perturbation of recurrent parameters in a sequential setting.
