MECHANISM: A large parameterized map is adapted by freezing the bulk of its weights and adding to selected weight matrices a low-rank correction written as a product of two thin factors, so the trainable parameter count scales with the rank rather than the matrix dimensions. During optimization the raw gradient of each factor is preconditioned by a curvature proxy: the layerwise second-moment information is approximated as a Kronecker product of an input-activation covariance and an output-gradient covariance, both reduced to the small rank-by-rank subspace, and the gradients of the two factors are multiplied by the inverses of these damped covariances to obtain an approximate natural-gradient step. Periodically the rank-space covariances are eigendecomposed, the top eigenvectors by energy are collected into orthogonal projectors, and the two factors are projected onto these dominant eigenspaces, optionally via a convex interpolation with the unprojected factors to limit disruption. The effective rank is selected adaptively as the smallest count of leading eigenvalues capturing a fixed fraction of the spectral energy, bounded between a floor and the initialization rank, with smoothing and hysteresis to suppress jitter. These three steps repeat so the low-rank subspace tracks high-curvature, high-signal directions while suppressing low-energy ones. Empirical drift from the original distribution is modeled by a power law in adaptation-data volume and model size, augmented by a multiplicative capacity factor built from the measured effective rank, subspace alignment, and retained projection mass. The procedure is benchmarked against alternative low-rank and second-order baselines, comparing task quality against trainable-parameter footprint.
