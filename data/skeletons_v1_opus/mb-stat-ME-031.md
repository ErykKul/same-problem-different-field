MECHANISM: Each entity has a duration outcome, possibly right-censored, and a covariate vector, and its instantaneous failure rate is modeled as a linear combination of the covariates with time-varying coefficient functions. Some coefficient functions are given a finite-dimensional parametric form while the rest are left unspecified, yielding a partly parametric, partly nonparametric specification. Estimation proceeds in two stages. The increments of the cumulative coefficient functions are first estimated nonparametrically by an integrated weighted least-squares solution: at each instant a weighted moment matrix formed from at-risk entities is inverted and applied to the weighted increments of the per-entity counting processes. The parametric block is then estimated by minimizing an integrated weighted quadratic discrepancy between the parametric coefficient curves and their nonparametric counterparts, equivalent to solving an estimating equation linear in the parameters for the constant-effect case. Conditional on the fitted parametric part, the remaining nonparametric coefficients are re-estimated by a backfitting step that subtracts the parametric contribution before inverting the residual moment submatrix, improving precision. Large-sample behavior is derived through the martingale structure of the counting-process residuals, giving root-n asymptotic normality with explicit sandwich-form variance matrices for both blocks and their joint distribution via a delta-method argument. Optimal weight choices that minimize the limiting variance are characterized, and goodness-of-fit monitoring processes yielding chi-squared-type tests are constructed.
