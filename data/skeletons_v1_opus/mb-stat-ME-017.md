MECHANISM: Each unit carries a covariate vector, a binary intervention label, and a complex structured outcome from which a multi-scale shape descriptor is computed. The descriptor records, across a continuous resolution parameter, the appearance and disappearance of structural features, and is summarized as a weighted, normalized superposition of tent-shaped functions, yielding a curve in a function space. The target is the average difference, across the population, between this curve under intervention and under control, defined separately at each resolution level. Under standard identification assumptions, this contrast is expressed both as an outcome-regression difference and as an inverse-assignment-probability weighted average. A plug-in estimator and an inverse-probability estimator are formed, then combined into an augmented estimator built from an influence function that adds a correction term, conferring robustness to errors in either nuisance component. Nuisance functions are fit on a separate split from the data used for the final average. The whole estimated curve is shown to converge weakly, at root-sample-size rate, to a mean-zero Gaussian process under a product-rate condition on nuisance errors, using a Lipschitz property of the descriptor. Stability of the descriptor under a transport distance between the underlying feature sets is bounded, and combined with the Gaussian limit to build a supremum-statistic test of the null of no structural effect, with critical values from a multiplier resampling scheme. Simultaneous confidence bands over the resolution axis follow.
