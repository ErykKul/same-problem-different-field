MECHANISM: Each entity is assigned a scalar latent strength parameter, and the probability that one entity is preferred over another in a pairwise comparison is a logistic function of the difference of their strengths. Given a collection of repeated pairwise outcomes, the likelihood is a product of binomial-type factors, and the log-likelihood depends on each entity only through its total count of favorable outcomes. A prior over the strengths is combined with this likelihood to form a posterior; for a flat prior the mode coincides with the maximum-likelihood point, found by a fixed-point iteration in which each strength is updated to balance predicted against observed favorable counts. The log-posterior is then expanded to second order about its mode, yielding a Gaussian approximation whose covariance is the inverse of the Hessian; because the parameters are identified only up to an additive shift, the Hessian is singular and its pseudo-inverse is taken via an eigen-decomposition that removes the null direction. Predictive probabilities of future composite outcomes require integrating an outcome indicator against the posterior over the full parameter vector. This high-dimensional integral is approximated three ways: by evaluating at the mode, by Monte Carlo averaging over draws from the Gaussian approximation, and by importance-sampling reweighting those draws by the ratio of exact to approximate posterior density. Competing predictive models are scored by the ratio of the probabilities they assign to the realized outcomes, accumulated multiplicatively over many evaluation events.
