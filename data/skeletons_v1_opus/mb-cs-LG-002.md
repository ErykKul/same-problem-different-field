MECHANISM: Each item carries two modalities, and the goal is to score items for entities given sparse positive interactions. Per item, the textual modality is first rewritten by a pretrained autoregressive sequence model acting as a deterministic normalizer, then both modalities are embedded by jointly trained dual encoders aligned with a contrastive objective that pulls matched pairs together and pushes mismatched ones apart, in softmax or per-pair sigmoid form; a fused vector is the average of the normalized per-modality embeddings, optionally averaged over sampled text subspans as a Monte-Carlo marginalization. A second encoder models each modality's latent as a diagonal-covariance Gaussian; the modalities are fused as a product of experts, which for Gaussians yields a closed-form combined precision and precision-weighted mean, and the posterior mean is taken as a deterministic feature. From the feature vectors a similarity graph is induced by symmetric k-nearest-neighbor connectivity on cosine similarity with negative values floored, giving roughly uniform neighborhood sizes. Graph neural encoders then propagate features over this adjacency: a supervised convolutional variant predicts per-node multi-label indicators; a reconstruction variant decodes the adjacency from embeddings via inner products; a variational variant places a Gaussian latent per node parameterized by shared convolutional layers and is trained by maximizing an evidence lower bound that trades reconstruction against a divergence-to-prior penalty. Labels are imbalanced, so the supervised objective is a reweighted focal cross-entropy with gradient clipping and edge dropout for stability. Synthetic interaction profiles are generated by uniformly sampling item subsets and thresholding the average label frequency, and final recommendations rank items by predicted label relevance to a profile. Variants are compared against many baselines.
