MECHANISM: Two independent groups each produce a count of successes out of a fixed number of trials, modeled as binomial with unknown success proportions. Conjugate bounded-support priors are placed on each proportion, with separate priors used for planning versus for evidence assessment. An evidence ratio comparing the marginal likelihood of the data under a null hypothesis (equal proportions, or an order constraint) against an alternative is derived in closed form using ratios of beta functions, with order-constrained cases handled by truncating the joint prior to a region and computing a normalizing constant via an incomplete-beta integral. For each fixed pair of group sizes, the prior-predictive probability mass function of the counts is obtained by integrating the likelihood against the planning prior, again yielding beta-function expressions. The set of count outcomes for which the evidence ratio crosses a specified decision threshold is found by a grid search over the finite outcome lattice rather than continuous root-finding, because multiple roots exist. Summing the predictive mass over that critical set under the null gives a long-run false-positive rate, and under the alternative gives a power-analogue; an additional criterion sums predictive mass for compelling evidence toward the null. The smallest group size achieving target power and error bounds is selected by incrementing the size and recomputing these sums. The procedure is entirely deterministic numerical integration and summation, avoiding stochastic simulation. A grid maximum over the null region recovers a strict frequentist error rate for comparison.
