MECHANISM: A fixed-length battery of ordinal items, grouped a priori into several latent blocks, is administered to a sample of entities, producing a rectangular table of bounded responses. For each block, the inter-item correlation matrix is formed and its leading eigenstructure is extracted; retention of a single dominant direction is judged by whether the top eigenvalue exceeds unity, and each item's projection onto that direction is reported as a loading. An internal-consistency coefficient based on item variances and total variance is computed per block to assess reliability. Block scores are then aggregated and a second eigen-decomposition is run on the correlation matrix of these composite scores to test whether one higher-order direction explains the joint variation, again using the eigenvalue-above-one rule and total-variance-explained. Sampling adequacy and the suitability of the correlation matrix for this decomposition are checked with a partial-correlation index and a sphericity test against the identity. Finally, group means of the aggregate score are compared across binary partitions of the entities using two-sample mean-difference tests, reporting test statistics and tail probabilities. The computation is thus eigen-analysis of correlation matrices plus reliability coefficients plus difference-in-means testing.
