MECHANISM: A scalar sequence is analyzed for state-dependent dynamics through a multi-stage pipeline. A first-difference-of-logarithm transform produces an ordered series of increments. A nonparametric independence diagnostic embeds the series via time-delay vectors, computes a correlation integral counting the fraction of vector pairs closer than a radius (an all-pairs distance computation), and forms a statistic whose deviation from the product of lower-dimensional integrals is asymptotically standard normal, testing departure from independent identically distributed behavior. The series is then adaptively decomposed into oscillatory components by an empirical sifting procedure, and an analytic-signal transform yields instantaneous amplitudes and frequencies; squared instantaneous amplitudes summed across components give a time-localized energy, which after normalization and mean-plus-multiple-of-standard-deviation thresholds segments the series into three intensity regimes. A second-layer decomposition of each amplitude envelope produces a joint carrier-versus-modulation energy representation used to profile each regime. Within each regime the increments are discretized into ordered quantile states, and a variable-order categorical sequence model is fitted as a context tree: conditional next-state distributions are estimated empirically for candidate histories, and a branch is retained only when a count-scaled relative-entropy statistic between a context and its shorter suffix exceeds a chi-square cutoff, otherwise pruned. From the estimated conditional distributions, summary functionals of persistence, paired reversal, run continuation, extreme switching, alternation, and tail emergence from calm histories are computed and compared across regimes and groups.
