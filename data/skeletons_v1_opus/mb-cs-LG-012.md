MECHANISM: A controller interacts over an unbounded horizon with a system whose one-step transition rule is only known to lie in a fixed set, while an adversary commits once to a worst-case transition rule from that set; the goal is to maximize the long-run average per-step payoff against the worst case. The analysis characterizes the optimal worst-case average value as the infimum over the uncertainty set of the per-rule optimal average gains, each defined through a fixed-point (constant-gain) optimality equation, without assuming the uncertainty set factorizes across states. It proves that any adaptive history-dependent rule whose cumulative shortfall relative to the per-rule optimum grows sublinearly is itself worst-case optimal, thereby reducing robust optimality to the existence of a no-shortfall learning procedure. A counterexample shows such procedures need not exist in general, but under a weak reachability condition high-probability shortfall guarantees are converted into expected-shortfall guarantees, establishing existence. To control finite-horizon behavior, a refined cumulative-deviation quantity is bounded above by the span of worst-case bias vectors and below in terms of the learning shortfall rate. A constructive procedure runs in growing epochs: it executes the optimal stationary rule for a candidate worst-case model while concurrently running a sequential likelihood-ratio test on the observed transitions, and upon rejection falls back to the learning procedure for the rest of the epoch. The test is designed with scheduled false-alarm control and logarithmic expected detection delay, yielding a constant-order finite-horizon deviation while preserving worst-case optimality.
