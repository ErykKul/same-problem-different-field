MECHANISM: Entities are arranged on a weighted interaction matrix with zero diagonal, and each entity's scalar outcome is modeled as jointly determined: a linear combination of a weighted average of neighbors' outcomes, an own binary intervention indicator, predetermined covariates, and an idiosyncratic shock. Stacking over entities gives a simultaneous linear system whose reduced-form solution is obtained by inverting the identity minus the interaction-coefficient times the interaction matrix, multiplied into the structural terms, yielding outcomes as a function of the full intervention vector. The paper formalizes that the effect of flipping one entity's indicator is not unique and defines a family of contrast quantities indexed by what is held fixed versus allowed to adjust: holding all others fixed gives an own coefficient; allowing only direct neighbors gives a first-order propagation; allowing full propagation gives a diagonal entry of the inverse operator times the coefficient. For each regime an identification condition is derived as a proposition with proof, requiring progressively stronger conditional-independence between the intervention and shocks (individual, neighborhood, then global). A controlled stochastic experiment then samples a sparse interaction structure and generates outcomes from the reduced form with fixed coefficients; the true contrasts are computed analytically and compared across regimes to show they differ mechanically. The linear system is repeatedly fit over many replications, and bias, dispersion, and root-mean-square error of the coefficient and the inverse-mapped equilibrium contrast are tabulated under both exogenous and shock-correlated assignment, demonstrating that feedback through the inverse amplifies distortion.
