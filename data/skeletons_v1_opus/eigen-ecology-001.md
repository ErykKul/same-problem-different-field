MECHANISM: Given a weighted directed network of entities with pairwise transfer quantities, a square transfer matrix is formed and normalized column-wise by each entity's total throughput to yield a unitless direct-transfer-intensity matrix. The cumulative effect over all paths is obtained by summing successive integer powers of this matrix, an infinite geometric series that converges because magnitudes decay with path length. The closed-form sum is computed as the inverse of the identity minus the transfer matrix. From this integral matrix, an importance score is assigned to each entity by summing its row entries and its column entries, each normalized by the grand total, then averaging the two directional scores into a single per-entity proportion. The same construction is repeated using only the direct matrix and using the matrix with the zeroth-order term removed, to isolate the contribution of higher-order paths. Dispersion of the score distribution is summarized by a normalized coefficient of variation, and entities are ranked and iteratively removed from the top until dispersion falls below a threshold to label dominant entities. Distributions from the full and direct constructions are compared via their dispersion ratio to test whether higher-order paths flatten the importance distribution. A monotone rank correlation compares the score against an alternative dominant-eigenvector-based score across many networks. The procedure is applied across a collection of networks of varying size and the resulting summary statistics are tabulated.
DOMAIN: ecology, ecosystem network analysis
STRUCTURE: dense linear algebra
