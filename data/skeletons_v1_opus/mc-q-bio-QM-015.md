MECHANISM: A connected structure embedded in continuous two-dimensional space is grown by a stochastic rule evolving in discrete time from a small seed. At each step every active endpoint either extends by one segment or splits into two segments at a fixed angle, chosen by fixed probabilities. Before extending, an endpoint senses nearby existing segments within a threshold distance and may deactivate with a fixed probability, modeling arrest; if an extension geometrically crosses an existing segment, a junction node is inserted and the two segments merge, creating cycles. Growth halts when total length reaches a budget. Sweeping the two governing probabilities generates an ensemble of structures spanning acyclic to highly cyclic, summarized by density and fraction-of-edges-in-cycles. Each structure is scored on three normalized objectives in the unit interval: a coverage area obtained by buffering every segment by a width, a robustness defined as the area under the curve of largest-component size as edges are randomly removed, and a transport conductance computed by treating segments as resistors with conductance inverse to length and solving for total conductance between opposite boundaries, then rescaled by spatial extent. Points are placed in the three-objective space and the nondominated frontier of optimal trade-offs is extracted. The synthetic ensemble and its frontier are compared against an empirical collection and against regular reference structures to test proximity in objective space. Metrics are averaged over repeated random realizations.
