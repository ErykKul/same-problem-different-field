MECHANISM: A target scalar signal is recorded repeatedly over a long span together with many auxiliary measured quantities, and a second independent instrument records a shared reference signal that is differenced against the first to isolate a common nuisance component. Observations are aggregated into time windows by weighted averaging, with weights depending on temporal proximity and measurement uncertainty, and a constant offset is removed to align the two instruments. Several empirical tracers of nuisance variation are constructed: an asymmetry statistic of a profile is obtained by interpolating it, normalizing, and differencing bisector midpoints at two amplitude levels, and positional shift, scale, and skew parameters of a two-dimensional pattern are obtained by least-squares fitting a parametric transform to reference coordinates. All tracers are interpolated to the target timestamps, standardized to zero mean and unit variance, and assembled as columns of a design matrix. The nuisance component of the target is modeled as a linear combination of these regressors and estimated by generalized least squares, weighting by a diagonal uncertainty matrix via the normal equations, with coefficients derived on held-out subsets to guard against overfitting. The fitted model is subtracted from the target. Performance is assessed by residual scatter, by injecting synthetic periodic signals and testing recovery via a least-squares spectral periodogram, and by checking removal of a spurious periodicity.
