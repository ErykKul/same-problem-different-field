MECHANISM: The target is to draw samples from a high-dimensional continuous posterior density whose sampling is slowed when its parameters are strongly correlated and differently scaled, quantified by a condition number of the local curvature. The sampler simulates a fictitious physical trajectory governed by a total energy that adds the negative log density to a quadratic momentum term, integrating the dynamics with alternating half-step and full-step updates that require repeated gradient evaluations, the dominant cost. To make trajectories travel far per step, the parameter space is linearly transformed so the transformed posterior has near-unit, decorrelated curvature; the transform is a triangular factor of an approximate second-derivative (precision) matrix, with the log density and gradient adjusted by the corresponding change of variables. The key step is obtaining this precision matrix cheaply as the block matrix of second derivatives at the mode of a Laplace approximation to the marginal density, exploiting that conditional-independence structure makes the matrix sparse. A fill-reducing permutation is applied before the triangular factorization so the factor stays sparse, keeping the transform's matrix-vector products near-linear in dimension. Because the precision-based normal approximation is also a fast proposal, draws from it initialize the trajectories and benchmark approximation quality against a competing approximate sampler using a transport-based discrepancy. The transformed density and gradient are then passed to the adaptive trajectory sampler, which removes the lengthy curvature-tuning warmup phase. Empirically the transformed sampler shortens trajectories and raises effective sample count by one to two orders of magnitude, except when curvature varies sharply so the single normal approximation is poor.
