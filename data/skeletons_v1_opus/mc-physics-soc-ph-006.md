MECHANISM: A family of parametric decline functions describing how a quantity falls from an initial value toward a finite asymptote over time is fitted to each observed trajectory. Each function is generalized by adding an equilibrium offset so the quantity relaxes to a nonzero limit, reducing to the offset-free forms when the limit is zero. For every trajectory the parameters are estimated by minimizing the residual sum of squares under bounded constraints, and the best functional form per trajectory is chosen by an information criterion that penalizes parameter count. A feed-forward network is then trained to map a small vector of design inputs to the parameters of each functional form through a shared trunk and separate output heads, with the predicted parameters passed through bounded transforms and fed into a differentiable internal layer that renders the full trajectory; the objective combines a time-weighted robust pointwise discrepancy with a squared parameter-matching term. A separate probabilistic regressor places a prior over functions with a composite covariance combining a smooth radial kernel, a linear term, and a noise term, and returns a posterior mean and variance at each horizon, yielding calibrated intervals. A gradient-boosted tree ensemble provides a purely data-driven regression baseline trained per horizon with a squared-error objective and early stopping. All fits use grouped splitting and cross-validation at the trajectory level to prevent leakage, and accuracy is summarized by explained-variance, root-mean-square, and absolute-error metrics across horizons.
