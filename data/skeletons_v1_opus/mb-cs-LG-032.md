MECHANISM: A large, ill-conditioned linear system arising from discretizing a continuous operator equation on a uniform grid is solved by a hierarchical multilevel iterative scheme. Each cycle alternates fine-level error-reduction operators with restriction of residuals to coarser grids and interpolation of corrections back, with an exact solve at the coarsest level. The classical fine-level relaxation operators, which are ineffective when the operator's spectral ordering is inverted, are replaced by learned parametric operators, one per level. Each learned operator is a composition of layers that mix a pointwise linear transform with a global transform that maps to a frequency representation, applies a learnable per-mode reweighting, and maps back, separated by nonlinearities. The operators are trained offline by stochastic gradient descent minimizing a per-level squared-error loss; each level's loss is passed through a spectral mask that weights distinct high-frequency bands so each operator targets only the frequencies resolvable at its grid and defers lower frequencies to coarser levels. A frequency-band decomposition of the error spectrum, derived by diagonalizing the iteration's error-propagation operator against transform basis modes, motivates the mask shapes. After training, the operators are fixed and the cycle is applied iteratively to new right-hand sides until the relative residual falls below a tolerance. Convergence rate, iteration count, and runtime are compared against classical iterative solvers across problem sizes and a regularization parameter.
