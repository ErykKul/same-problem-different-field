MECHANISM: Define a scalar functional of several continuous probability densities as the integral over the real line of the pointwise minimum of those densities, a bounded quantity between zero and one measuring shared mass. Because the pointwise minimum is piecewise-defined with switch points wherever the ranking of densities changes, and the number of such regions grows with the count of densities, closed-form integration is impractical, so approximate the integral numerically. Estimate each density's parameters from an independent sample by their standard plug-in point estimators, substitute these into the parametric density forms, and evaluate the estimated minimum function on a grid. Apply a fixed-weight quadrature rule (weighted sums of function values at evenly spaced nodes) to approximate the integral; to handle the unbounded domain, apply a smooth monotone change of variables mapping the line to the unit interval and multiply by the corresponding Jacobian, excluding the singular endpoints. Prove consistency: the parameter estimators converge in probability, continuity transfers this to pointwise then uniform convergence of the estimated minimum on compact sets, tail decay bounds the truncated contribution, and the fixed-grid quadrature error is a deterministic order-h^4 term independent of sample size, so the estimator converges to the target. Assess finite-sample behavior through a repeated-sampling study reporting bias, normalized root-mean-square error, and efficiency relative to a competing estimator across overlap levels and sample sizes, including a data-driven choice of the transformation tuning parameter.
