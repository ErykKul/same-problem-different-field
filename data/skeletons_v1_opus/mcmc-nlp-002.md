MECHANISM: The goal is to draw a set of high-scoring and diverse discrete sequences in proportion to a target density that is proportional to the exponential of a real-valued scoring function divided by a temperature, where the normalizing constant is intractable because it sums over an enormous space of candidate sequences. Direct sampling from this density is impossible, so a Markov chain is constructed whose stationary distribution is the target, by repeatedly proposing a candidate from the current state and accepting or rejecting it. At each step a position along the current sequence is drawn from an index distribution, the prefix up to that position is retained, and a fresh suffix is generated autoregressively from a base generative model conditioned on the retained prefix. The candidate is accepted with probability equal to the minimum of one and a ratio that combines the exponential of the score difference between candidate and current state with the ratio of forward and reverse proposal probabilities; the intractable normalizer cancels in this ratio. If accepted the chain moves to the candidate, otherwise it stays, and the procedure repeats for a fixed number of steps, returning the visited states as samples. The proposal is shown to be irreducible and aperiodic and to satisfy detailed balance, guaranteeing convergence to the target. The temperature controls how sharply the density concentrates on high-score states and thereby the acceptance and rejection behavior; lower temperature raises rejection. Output sets are evaluated by mean score and by average pairwise dissimilarity, and the target can be augmented to include the base model's own log-probability.
