MECHANISM: A sequence of vector-valued observations is modeled as driven by an unobserved discrete state that evolves as a first-order Markov chain over a small finite set of states. Each observation is the first difference of successive positions, and conditional on the current state it follows a vector autoregressive step: the previous difference is scaled by a state-specific gain and rotated by a state-specific angle, plus zero-mean bivariate Gaussian noise with a diagonal covariance. The joint likelihood of the whole sequence is written as a product over time of state-emission density matrices interleaved with a fixed transition probability matrix, bracketed by an initial-state vector and a vector of ones. The emission density at each step is the conditional Gaussian density evaluated for each candidate state, placed on the diagonal of a small matrix. The free parameters are the per-state gains and angles, the noise variances, and the off-diagonal transition probabilities. These parameters are estimated by numerically maximizing this product-form likelihood, with the inner summations handled by the matrix recursion. After fitting, the most probable state at each step is recovered by a global dynamic-programming decoding pass over the state lattice. A comparison variant places the same observation process inside a sampling-based posterior framework and estimates states from posterior summaries instead. Accuracy is assessed on synthetic sequences by error rates of recovered states and root-mean-square error of parameter estimates.
