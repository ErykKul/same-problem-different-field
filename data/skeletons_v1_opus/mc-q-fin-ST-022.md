MECHANISM: A parametric sequence model is trained offline on sliding windows of a multivariate stream to predict either the next-step continuous increment or a binary up-down label. At deployment the bulk of the model is frozen and only the affine scale-and-shift parameters of its internal normalization layers are adjusted, using a handful of gradient steps on recent unlabeled windows under self-supervised objectives that require no targets. For the categorical task the objective is the predictive entropy of the output plus a consistency penalty between predictions on a window and on a weakly time-preserving perturbation of it; for the continuous task the objective is the variance of predictions across several such perturbations plus a squared distance to a slowly updated exponential-moving-average copy of the parameters. A squared inter-step change penalty discourages large parameter motion. An uncertainty proxy, computed as mean entropy or mean perturbation variance over the batch and thresholded at a validation quantile, gates the update: when exceeded, gradient steps are skipped and only the running normalization moments are recomputed. The adapted model emits predictions stepwise over a held-out chronological span partitioned into regimes. Accuracy is summarized by error and agreement metrics and calibration, and pairwise differences in per-step loss between adaptation variants are tested for significance using a serial-correlation-robust mean-difference statistic, with an illustrative threshold-following trading evaluation reporting risk-adjusted return under the same robust variance correction.
