MECHANISM: For independent entities, each is assigned to one of several discrete conditions, and within each condition there is an unobserved sub-label that further refines the response; the goal is to estimate the average difference in outcome attributable to each condition-sublabel combination. The outcome given a condition is modeled as a finite mixture: a gating component gives the probability of each sublabel as a softmax linear function of covariates, and an expert component gives the conditional outcome as a linear-in-covariates mean with its own variance. Because the sublabel is latent, the observed-data likelihood marginalizes over sublabels, producing a log of a sum that blocks direct maximization. The parameters are therefore fit by alternating expectation and maximization: the expectation step computes, for each entity, the posterior membership weight over sublabels by Bayes' rule from the current gating and expert densities; the maximization step updates the gating coefficients and the expert regression coefficients and variances by membership-weighted likelihood maximization. To remove the labeling and shift ambiguities of the mixture, a reference component is fixed to zero and components are reordered by a deterministic lexicographic rule. Using the fitted condition-assignment probability, sublabel-assignment probability, and posterior membership, a plug-in inverse-probability-weighted average is formed that divides each observed outcome by its estimated joint assignment probability and reweights by membership, yielding an estimate of each combination's mean outcome and of pairwise contrasts. Consistency of this estimator is established under standard regularity, identifiability, and overlap conditions. Performance is checked by repeated synthetic-data trials reporting bias and spread, and uncertainty on real data is quantified by resampling intervals.
