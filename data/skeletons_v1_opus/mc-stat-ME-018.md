MECHANISM: A linear relationship links a streaming sequence of feature vectors to scalar responses through an unknown parameter vector plus additive noise, where features and noise may be temporally dependent rather than independent. The parameter is estimated online by a stochastic-gradient recursion: at each step the current estimate is moved against the instantaneous squared-error gradient, a rank-one update using only the newest observation, without storing history. Dependence is controlled through conditional sub-exponential norm conditions relative to the growing filtration, with martingale-difference assumptions and bounded conditional second moments. A two-phase stepsize schedule is analyzed: a constant step giving geometric contraction into a neighborhood, then a decaying step of order one over time achieving the optimal error rate. The analysis derives high-probability non-asymptotic tail bounds on the squared estimation error that remain valid uniformly over an unbounded horizon, controlling accumulation of per-step failure probabilities. An asymptotic expansion decomposes the centered scaled iterate into a leading weighted-noise sum converging in distribution to a multivariate Gaussian plus a vanishing remainder. The framework is extended to sequential decision making with exploration schedules to bound regret, and to a sparse variant adding hard-thresholding plus an aggregated statistic for support recovery. Results are theorems with supporting simulations. Outputs are rate-optimal estimators with inference guarantees.
