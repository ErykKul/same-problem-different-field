MECHANISM: This is primarily a position and vision argument advocating that a structure over a set of entities be assembled from many partial, noisy informants rather than a single source, and it sketches (without delivering a validated new method) probabilistic formulations for doing so. A latent directed structure on entities is posited, and each informant is modeled as holding a belief distribution over that structure, queried per pair of entities to yield categorical or graded responses. Two elicitation skeletons are outlined: one assigning each pair a three-way categorical indicator, the other assigning each entity a latent ordering score so pairwise responses reflect precedence. Aggregation across informants is framed as a posterior over the structure that factorizes the per-response likelihoods times a structure prior, either by combining per-informant parameter estimates or by pooling all responses under a shared mixture in which a latent indicator selects one of three generating mechanisms with a difficulty-times-reliability decomposition. Query selection is cast as a sequential design that maximizes an information criterion (smallest information-matrix eigenvalue or expected entropy reduction) under a budget. A small illustrative elicitation study reports only descriptive distributions of responses. No estimator is fit, validated, or benchmarked, so there is no realized computational core.
