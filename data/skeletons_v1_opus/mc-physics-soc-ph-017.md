MECHANISM: A large well-mixed population is composed of agents following one of a few fixed behavioral types, with state given by the type frequencies summing to one. Each pairwise interaction assigns costs and benefits to the two participants according to their types and a binary label each agent carries, where the label is updated deterministically by a rule that marks an agent acceptable only if it satisfies a conjunction of conditional actions. From these interaction rules the expected per-encounter payoff of each type is written as a closed-form polynomial in the frequencies, parameterized by benefit, cost, penalty, enforcement-cost, and complexity-cost constants. The frequencies evolve by a system of ordinary differential equations in which each type's growth equals its frequency times the gap between its payoff and the population mean payoff. Equilibria are found by setting growth to zero; on a two-type face the payoff-difference reduces to a concave quadratic in one frequency, whose real roots in the unit interval give interior rest points, with the larger root identified as asymptotically stable and the smaller as a threshold. Local stability is assessed by the sign of the dynamics near each rest point and by transversal eigenvalue arguments, yielding bistability between a boundary state and an interior mixture. Population welfare is the mean payoff at equilibrium; comparing the mixed equilibrium with and without the enforcement terms, and differentiating welfare with respect to the penalty constant, gives an analytical inequality characterizing when enforcement raises welfare.
