MECHANISM: A large random network of interacting entities is constructed in which each pair is linked independently with fixed probability and edge weights encode mutual obligations, partitioned into two behavioral types. Each entity's settled obligation is the solution of a coupled system of random fixed-point equations, where the cleared amount equals the obligation capped by available resources after incoming claims, with resources driven by a two-point (high/low) random return. As the network size grows, this high-dimensional random fixed-point system is reduced to an asymptotically equivalent scalar fixed-point equation, and aggregate claims and per-entity payoffs are shown to converge almost surely to deterministic limits, yielding closed-form default probabilities and expected surpluses as piecewise expressions. A population-update process then adds one entity per round that selects a type by comparing observed payoffs of sampled incumbents, either through noisy averaged estimates with type-dependent Gaussian error or through a single noisy pairwise comparison. The resulting fraction sequence is written as a stochastic-approximation recursion with a vanishing step and a bounded martingale-difference term, and is proven to converge almost surely to the internally chain-transitive invariant sets of an associated ordinary differential equation. Stability analysis of this differential equation characterizes the limits as pure or mixed equilibria and gives conditions under which the high-risk type is eliminated. Direct simulation that solves the fixed-point equations along sampled trajectories confirms the predicted limits.
