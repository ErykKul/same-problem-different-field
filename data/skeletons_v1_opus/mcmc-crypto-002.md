MECHANISM: The goal is to draw samples from a discrete target distribution over an integer-coordinate set that is proportional to a decaying exponential of a squared distance, normalized by an intractable sum. A chain is constructed that, given the current state, proposes a candidate and accepts or rejects it so that the target is the stationary distribution. The proposal is generated by a sequential backward sweep that samples each coordinate from a one-dimensional conditional built from a triangular factorization of the generating matrix, producing a tractable near-target proposal density. The acceptance probability is the usual minimum-of-one ratio of target-times-reverse-proposal to current-times-forward-proposal, which simplifies because of the proposal's product form. For the independent-proposal variant it is proved that the ratio of proposal to target is bounded below by a positive constant independent of state, by bounding each coordinate's normalizer; via a coupling argument this yields a uniform geometric contraction in total-variation distance at rate one-minus-that-constant, giving a state-independent mixing-time bound. The contraction constant is expressed in closed form through lattice and one-dimensional theta sums and related to a flatness quantity, showing it approaches one in suitable parameter regimes. A symmetric-proposal variant, using a difference between consecutive states, is shown to converge geometrically with a state-dependent prefactor. A spectral-gap bound via conductance is also derived.
