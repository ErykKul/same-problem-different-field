MECHANISM: Several parallel subgroups each accumulate binary success/failure observations over time, and the goal is to decide, repeatedly and early, whether each subgroup's success probability exceeds a fixed reference. Within each subgroup a conjugate prior on the success probability is updated by the observed counts to a closed-form posterior. Information is then shared across subgroups by reweighting each subgroup's pseudo-counts by similarity weights, where the similarity between two subgroups is one minus a symmetric divergence between their independent posteriors, raised to a power and thresholded, yielding a pooled posterior per subgroup. A subgroup is declared non-promising when its pooled posterior mass below the reference exceeds a cutoff. Because outcomes are delayed, at each interim look some observations are censored: an event-time model with shape and scale parameters (the scale optionally regressed on subgroup indicators) is fit by sampling its parameter posterior, and for each draw the censored binary outcomes are imputed from the conditional event probability over the remaining window. For each completed imputation the closed-form posteriors and similarity weights are recomputed, and the futility probabilities are averaged across imputations to make the stopping decision. The entire sequential procedure is wrapped in a large replicated stochastic simulation, with arrivals generated by a constant-rate point process, to estimate operating characteristics such as early-stopping probability, expected sample size, expected duration, error rates, and power across many configurations and several missing-data handling strategies.
