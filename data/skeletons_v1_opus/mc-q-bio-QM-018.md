MECHANISM: For many discrete units observed repeatedly over a variable number of time points, each unit-time integer count is modeled relative to a known exposure offset. The core is a two-component mixture: in the static component a unit's intensity is constant across time, while in the dynamic component the intensity varies freely by time point, with counts conditionally arising from a count distribution whose mean is intensity times offset. A conjugate continuous prior on the intensities lets each component's likelihood be obtained in closed form by integrating intensities out, yielding marginal mass functions of an overdispersed-count form; per-unit prior shape parameters are themselves drawn from population-level distributions on a log scale to share information and absorb between-unit heterogeneity, and a single mixing proportion governs membership. Because the hierarchy and integrals preclude closed-form posteriors, the joint posterior over all parameters is explored by gradient-guided trajectory sampling, after which each unit receives a posterior probability of belonging to the dynamic component and is thresholded into dynamic versus static. Separately, co-occurrence counts of categorical features are arranged in a multiway contingency array and modeled by a fully saturated additive log-mean expansion of main and interaction terms subject to an absolute-value penalty, whose tuning constant is chosen by cross-validation, yielding a sparse set of significant interaction effects. Downstream, the thresholded counts are linked to outcomes through proportional-hazards regression for time-to-event endpoints and linear regression for continuous markers, with rank-based and distributional comparisons of trajectories.
