MECHANISM: Each instance is represented as a labelled graph of nodes carrying invariant attributes and three-dimensional coordinates, with edges within and between two sub-components, and the goal is to predict a scalar change-of-quantity caused by a local perturbation of a few nodes. Since the perturbed configuration is unavailable, a refinement module is trained by a masking task: a contiguous segment of nodes around a site is corrupted (either by additive zero-mean noise or by linear interpolation of coordinates from flanking nodes), and the module reconstructs the masked coordinates, minimizing a robust Huber distance to the true coordinates. The same corruption-then-refine procedure is reused at inference to synthesize the perturbed configuration, which is then re-encoded; original and synthesized representations are pooled and mapped to the scalar by a regressor, trained jointly with a weighted sum of the scalar loss and the reconstruction loss. The geometric encoder treats each node position as a Gaussian random variable with a mean vector and a covariance matrix rather than a fixed point. Pairwise squared distances then follow a generalized chi-squared law whose mean and variance are computed in closed form from the difference of means and the sum of covariances (trace and quadratic-form terms). Message passing propagates node embeddings using these distance moments, updates each mean by a weighted sum of relative mean differences, and updates each covariance by a weighted sum of summed covariances, preserving equivariance. The learned covariance magnitude is compared against simulated positional fluctuations to validate that it encodes uncertainty.
