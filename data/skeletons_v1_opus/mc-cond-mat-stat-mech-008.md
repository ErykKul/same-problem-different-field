MECHANISM: The goal is to compute, in closed form, the matrix elements of a local operator between two distinct orthonormal bases of a finite-dimensional state space, and then to extract their large-size asymptotics. The system is described by a quadratic form in anticommuting generators, which is brought to diagonal form by an orthogonal transformation that pairs the generators into independent two-level modes with explicit single-mode amplitudes. Building this transformation requires solving a linear eigenvalue recursion on a banded skew-symmetric matrix indexed by roots of unity, fixing a degenerate boundary amplitude by analytic continuation in a deformation parameter and tracking the sign of the transformation's determinant. Two such diagonalizations, over the periodic and antiperiodic root sets, yield two creation-annihilation algebras expressed through the same underlying generators, so any matrix element between their respective vacua reduces to inversion and multiplication of a structured kernel matrix of near-Cauchy form in the single-particle space. Inverting this kernel produces ratios of products of square-root differences over the two root sets, which are simplified using elementary product identities and rewritten as single contour integrals by deforming sums of a meromorphic weight onto branch-cut integrals. Balancing the resulting expressions gives compact product formulas for the matrix elements and an integral expression for the cross-vacuum overlap. A continuum scaling limit is taken by sending the size to infinity with rescaled mode indices, reducing the integrals to standard special-function forms and yielding power-law decay with explicit constants and subleading corrections. A determinant identity for the two-point quantity is also established to confirm convergence.
