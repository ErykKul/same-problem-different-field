MECHANISM: A parametric stochastic decision rule is trained to allocate a fixed budget of discrete redundancy units across the coordinates of a fixed-length vector so as to minimize an expected end-to-end distortion. Each input is mapped by a frozen encoder to a continuous vector, normalized, and rounded to integer levels; each coordinate is then replicated an integer number of times, sent through a noisy stochastic channel, and recovered per coordinate by majority vote before inverse rounding. A composite scalar distortion combines one minus a cosine alignment between original and recovered vectors with a weighted fraction of critical tokens lost, serving as the negative reward. The budgeted minimization is converted to an unconstrained objective by a penalty on total redundancy plus an entropy bonus encouraging exploration. A small network outputs a categorical weight distribution over coordinates (the actor) and a companion network estimates the expected return (the critic). Because the allocation is integer and combinatorial, the forward pass uses a deterministic floor-plus-greedy-remainder allocation while gradients flow through a relaxed sampled allocation, giving a biased low-variance policy-gradient estimate; actor and critic parameters are updated on two separated step-size timescales. Almost-sure convergence to stationary points of the regularized objective is established under bounded-reward, Lipschitz, and Robbins-Monro step-size conditions, with the relaxation bias vanishing as the budget grows. Evaluation uses resampling-based confidence intervals and rank-based significance tests.
