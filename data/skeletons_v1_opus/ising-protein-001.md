MECHANISM: A collection of aligned sequences over a finite alphabet is treated as samples from a joint probability law over discrete configurations of many coupled categorical sites. The law belongs to the exponential family whose log-probability is a sum of per-site bias terms plus pairwise interaction terms between every pair of sites, normalized by an intractable global partition sum over all configurations. The task is to recover the bias and pairwise-interaction parameters from the observed empirical single-site frequencies and pairwise co-occurrence frequencies, separating direct pairwise influence from correlation propagated indirectly through chains of intermediaries. Because the global normalizer is computationally infeasible, the parameters are fit by maximizing a surrogate objective: the product over sites of each site's conditional probability given the observed states of all other sites, which avoids the partition sum and reduces to coupled multinomial-regression-type problems. This objective is concave and is augmented with a quadratic penalty on the interaction magnitudes; it is optimized by gradient-based numerical methods, with symmetrization of the two estimates available for each pair. From the fitted pairwise parameters a scalar coupling strength per pair is computed by reducing the interaction block to a single magnitude and applying a correction that subtracts a rank-one term estimated from row and column averages. Pairs are then ranked by corrected strength as candidate direct interactions. Reweighting of near-duplicate sequences and pseudocount smoothing of the empirical statistics are applied before fitting. The ranked predictions are validated against independently known ground-truth adjacencies.
