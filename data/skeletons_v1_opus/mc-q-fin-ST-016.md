MECHANISM: A continuous-time process is simulated many times under a roughness parameter, where the path is driven by a self-similar Gaussian process with a tunable correlation exponent feeding an exponential volatility factor. For each realized discrete series, increments at a range of aggregation lags are formed and, for several real moment orders, the average of absolute increments to that power is computed and seen to follow a power law in the lag. Taking logs, the scaling exponent for each order is estimated by least-squares slope in log-log space, with an explicit standard error per order. A single summary slope of exponent-versus-order is then estimated by weighted least squares, weighting each order inversely by its variance to handle heteroscedastic and heavy-tailed errors; this slope quantifies whether moments share one exponent or scale differently. The admissible moment range is bounded by estimating the tail index via maximum-likelihood fit of a heavy-tailed stable law and applying a safety factor; the lag range is chosen to maximize fit quality. To attribute the multi-exponent behavior, two null ensembles are generated: a matched self-similar Gaussian surrogate built by embedding the theoretical autocovariance in a circulant matrix diagonalized by fast transform, and a shuffled surrogate that randomly permutes increments to preserve the marginal while destroying temporal order. The observed summary slope is compared to each surrogate ensemble through percentile and median-distance permutation p-values to test presence and source.
