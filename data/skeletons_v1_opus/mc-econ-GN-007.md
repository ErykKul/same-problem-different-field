MECHANISM: A finite population of agents is endowed with fixed heterogeneous scalar types and connected by directed audience relations that determine who observes whom. Each agent chooses an action (a value in a bounded interval) and, after observing others' actions through its audience, each viewer makes a binary endorsement decision; payoffs combine a reward proportional to endorsements received, a gain from exposure to endorsed actions matching one's type, and a penalty growing with the distance and endorsement of misaligned exposure. The solution concept is a subgame-perfect equilibrium of this sequential game. Backward reasoning shows the endorsement rule is a simple threshold (endorse iff the observed action equals the viewer's type), which reduces each agent's choice to maximizing a reach-weighted payoff over the discrete set of realized types; this yields a per-agent comparison inequality determining whether the agent reports its true type or a more popular one. Specializing to a symmetric three-type configuration, the analysis derives closed-form parameter thresholds (in audience-reach, reward weight, and distance cost) that partition the parameter space into regimes, and characterizes by propositions when the action distribution becomes more or less concentrated than the underlying type distribution. Aggregate welfare is the summed payoff, compared across the equilibrium, a truthful-reporting benchmark, and an inactivity benchmark, identifying regions where the equilibrium is Pareto-improving, mixed, or Pareto-inferior. An alternative audience-allocation rule that maximizes endorsements subject to a visibility cap is introduced, and its equilibrium concentration is compared to the baseline. Illustrative numerical evaluations accompany the analytic characterization. The computation is equilibrium characterization of a strategic game via threshold analysis and closed-form regime boundaries.
