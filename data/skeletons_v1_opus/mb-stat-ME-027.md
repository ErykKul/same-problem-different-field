MECHANISM: An entity is a vector of length a power of two, represented in a fixed orthonormal basis. A family of orthogonal (or unitary) operators is built from elementary orthogonal building blocks by four algebraic operations: matrix product, Kronecker product, block-diagonal assembly, and similarity conjugation, each of which provably preserves orthogonality and hence energy and exact invertibility. Each composite operator is applied to a noisy input vector to map it into a coefficient representation. A fixed coefficient-wise shrinkage rule with a single universal threshold is applied to the magnitudes of the coefficients, zeroing or attenuating small ones. The transpose (adjoint) of the operator is then applied to map the thresholded coefficients back to the original domain, yielding an estimate of the underlying clean vector. Energy concentration of the coefficients is quantified by sorting squared magnitudes and forming a cumulative-mass curve, whose departure from the diagonal measures sparsity. Performance is assessed by averaging squared reconstruction error over many independent noise realizations. The procedure compares composite operators against single-block operators under an identical pipeline to isolate the effect of the operator construction. The construction extends to two-dimensional arrays via a vectorization identity equating a two-sided transform with a Kronecker-product operator. A proposition establishes that a product of two such operators generally cannot be refactored into a single elementary two-channel form.
