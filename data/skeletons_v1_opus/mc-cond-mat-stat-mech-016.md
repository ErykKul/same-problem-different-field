MECHANISM: Consider a continuous-time jump process on a finite set of states with transition rates obeying a balance condition, driven slowly by changing the per-state energies that set the stationary distribution. The excess cost of slow driving is shown, to leading order, to be a quadratic form in the driving velocity with a kernel built from a generalized inverse of the rate operator scaled by the stationary distribution, interpreted as a metric on the manifold of distributions. The state set is identified with a weighted graph whose symmetric edge weights are the balanced equilibrium fluxes, and the corresponding weighted combinatorial Laplacian is formed from these weights. The central result proves that this cost metric equals, up to a projection onto the mass-conserving tangent subspace, the pseudoinverse of that Laplacian; by standard identities the same object equals one-half the pairwise effective-resistance matrix of the associated resistor network and one-half the symmetrized round-trip first-passage-time matrix. A further equivalence recasts the squared geodesic cost as a discrete dynamic optimal-transport problem minimizing a quadratic edge-flux functional subject to a discrete continuity constraint relating distribution change to a potential through the Laplacian. The round-trip-time matrix is a squared Euclidean distance matrix, so multidimensional scaling embeds states as points whose squared separations give transport cost, exposing clusters and bottlenecks. Mapping fluxes to currents and lags to potentials, the excess cost becomes the dissipated power in the resistor network, letting series-parallel and loop reductions yield closed-form metrics for chain and ring topologies. The construction is then extended from finite states to a continuous generator while preserving the metric equivalence.
