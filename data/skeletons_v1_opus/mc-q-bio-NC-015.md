MECHANISM: A generic sequence-processing function approximator with no built-in spatial priors is fitted to temporally ordered visual frames and then probed for emergent internal organization. The training objective is self-supervised and temporal: embeddings of frames falling within a short time window are pulled together while embeddings of non-adjacent frames are pushed apart, and parameters are updated by gradient-based optimization over many passes through the frame stream. The input frames are synthetically generated by a simulator of spontaneous traveling-wave activity, and the amount of training data is varied to measure its effect. After fitting, parameters are frozen and three structural signatures are quantified per layer. Orientation sensitivity is measured by building a representational dissimilarity matrix over stimuli that vary in a single oriented feature and correlating it (off-diagonal only) with a hand-specified matrix whose dissimilarity grows geometrically with feature distance. Shape-versus-surface preference is measured by comparing average representational similarity among stimuli matched on contour against those matched on a surface attribute. Spatial-integration extent per layer is estimated by a gradient method that backpropagates activations of a central unit block to the input and measures the spatial spread of influence. A control condition destroys temporal ordering of the frames and reruns the whole pipeline. The finding is that all three signatures emerge after temporally ordered training, scale with data quantity, and vanish when temporal order is removed.
