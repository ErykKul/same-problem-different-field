MECHANISM: A set of paired origin and destination tasks must be served by a limited fleet traversing a network, choosing routes, visit times, and intermediate replenishment amounts to minimize a weighted sum of traversal cost and excess service duration. The problem is cast as exact integer optimization over binary arc-selection variables on a constructed network, with continuous auxiliary variables tracking arrival times and a depletable resource level along arcs. Side constraints enforce flow conservation, exactly-once coverage, pairing and precedence of paired tasks, capacity, time windows, maximum per-task duration, and resource feasibility including initialization, minimum levels, and replenishment-time coupling. Three formulations are built on different network constructions: one whose nodes encode states of currently-carried tasks, and two whose building blocks are enumerated empty-at-both-ends partial paths placed on a network expanded along discretized time, optionally also along discretized resource levels. The partial-path building blocks pre-embed many side constraints during enumeration, shrinking the active constraint set. Discretizing the time axis turns continuous schedules into indexed transitions and enables the partial-path representation; additionally discretizing the resource axis indexes resource states explicitly. Each formulation is handed to a general exact integer-program solver, and the comparison isolates the computational effect of each discretization choice. Reachable-state counts determine when the resource-indexed construction helps or hurts. The core computation is constraint-laden combinatorial optimization over network-flow variables with state-expanded graphs.
