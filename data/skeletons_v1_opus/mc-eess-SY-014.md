MECHANISM: A continuous elevation field over a two-dimensional region is reconstructed from scattered samples by first evaluating, on the nodes of a uniform grid, an inverse-distance weighted average of the few nearest scattered points, after which the value at any arbitrary location is obtained by bilinear interpolation within the enclosing grid cell. Given an ordered reference curve of three-dimensional points, an adjacent parallel curve is generated point by point. At each point a local heading is computed from the finite difference between consecutive points, and an offset direction is taken orthogonal to it in the horizontal plane. The midpoint is lifted to a target normal clearance above the surface by computing a local surface slope angle from sampled elevations and applying trigonometric corrections. A candidate offset point at a fixed lateral spacing is then placed, its elevation queried from the field, a tilt angle from a height residual computed, and the effective horizontal spacing shrunk by its cosine. The actual clearance of the candidate to the surface is approximated by an orthogonal-distance expression, and if it deviates from the target beyond a tolerance the tilt angle is nudged by a fixed step and the placement recomputed, iterating until the clearance matches or a sign change triggers an inverse-distance-weighted interpolation between the last two angles. Spacing of sample points along the reference curve is set from a geometric inequality in the lateral width and a maximum heading change. Concatenating many such offset curves yields full coverage, and a graph routing step connects the resulting lane segments.
