MECHANISM: Given samples (here whole trajectories) drawn from several biased ensembles, where each ensemble restricts sampling by an indicator that is one only when a sample exceeds a threshold along some scalar coordinate, estimate per-sample weights that recover the single unbiased distribution and reweighted observables. Express the unbiased distribution as a normalized weighted sum of delta functions over all pooled samples, and each biased ensemble as the same sum gated by its indicator and normalized by an ensemble-specific partition sum. Write the total log-likelihood of observing every sample in its own conditioning ensemble, then maximize it with respect to the unknown weights; the stationarity condition gives each weight as the reciprocal of a sum over ensembles of sample-counts times indicators divided by partition-sum ratios. Substituting back yields a self-consistent fixed-point system for the partition-sum ratios, solved by iteration from unit initial values until convergence. Exploit the threshold structure so that the denominator only accumulates terms up to the highest threshold a sample reaches, making each weight depend only on that per-sample maximum coordinate value; generalize this from one coordinate to several distinct coordinates and to an arbitrary number of ensemble families on a common scale. From the converged weights, build reweighted estimates of exceedance probabilities and of a log-density (free-energy) surface via density-weighted error metrics, matching an additive offset between forward and reverse families by equating reactive fluxes. Quantify statistical error by block resampling and show the error decreases roughly as the inverse square root of the number of combined families.
