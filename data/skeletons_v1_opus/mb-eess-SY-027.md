MECHANISM: The work designs and proves convergence of a feedback scheme that drives many interacting second-order subsystems, each with unknown constant coefficients, to track a common reference signal using only relative position measurements exchanged over a directed weighted interaction graph. The interaction is encoded by a graph operator (degree minus adjacency, plus a leader-coupling term) that is normalized to satisfy a balance condition, and a tracking error is defined as a graph-weighted combination of neighbor and reference signals. A control law combining proportional, integral, and derivative-like terms is constructed so the closed-loop input-output map is rational and made strictly passive (positive-real); a structured reparameterization folds parameter estimates into the operator so the unknown coefficients enter as a linear regressor multiplying a known time-varying signal. Adaptive update rules for the estimates are derived by requiring the time derivative of a quadratic storage function (a weighted error norm plus a trace term in estimation error) to be negative semidefinite, which the chosen gradient-type law guarantees. Asymptotic convergence of the tracking error to zero follows by an invariance/limit argument, and parameter estimates converge when the regressor is persistently exciting. When the map is not positive-real because its relative degree is too high, a frequency-shaping filter on outputs or on the regressor lowers the effective relative degree to restore the passivity condition, after which the same storage-function argument applies. Robustness is established by showing the error system is input-to-state stable to bounded disturbances with exact rejection of constant ones. Per-step cost is constant per node locally with communication scaling with neighbor count.
