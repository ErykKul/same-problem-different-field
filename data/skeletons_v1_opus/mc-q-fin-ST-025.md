MECHANISM: Two rectangular tables of observations, a target and an auxiliary, are each modeled as a low-rank common component plus idiosyncratic noise, where the common part factors into a small number of latent time-varying scores times unit-specific loadings. Before estimation, the stacked tables are reweighted by two learned matrices: a row (temporal) operator and a column (cross-sectional) operator, which act as data-dependent similarity weights formed from normalized inner products of query and key projections, generalizing fixed averaging to adaptive aggregation across heterogeneous sampling resolutions. The transformed table is approximated in squared Frobenius norm by a rank-k product; under an orthonormality identification the loadings are the leading eigenvectors of the transformed second-moment matrix and the scores follow by projection, so estimation reduces to an eigen-decomposition of a reweighted covariance. For the linear case, under stationarity, mixing, moment, and spectral-growth conditions on the weighting operators, the estimated loadings and scores are shown consistent with an explicit rate governed by the magnitude and concentration of the weights, and a subset of strong target directions is identified and shown asymptotically normal. A nonlinear extension stacks attention and feedforward layers (an encoder network) to capture interactions while preserving this factor interpretation. The fitted components feed a forecasting step, and the learned weight patterns are averaged to rank which units and lags drive prediction, with components removed one at a time to assess importance.
