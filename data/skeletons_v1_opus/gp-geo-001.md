MECHANISM: A collection of correlated quantities indexed over locations is modeled by first drawing a vector from a multivariate normal with a structured dependence matrix, then mapping each coordinate through the standard normal cumulative function and the inverse cumulative function of a chosen target marginal, so each coordinate attains a prescribed marginal law while the joint dependence remains that of the original normal. The dependence is encoded either through a covariance matrix or, equivalently, through its inverse, whose zero pattern encodes conditional independence between coordinates given the rest. The inverse-dependence matrix is built from a neighbor adjacency structure: it is a scaled difference of an identity and a row-normalized weight matrix, so that nonzero off-diagonal entries occur only between adjacent indices, yielding a sparse object. Each observed quantity is taken conditionally from an exponential-family law whose mean is a coordinate of this transformed field, and a link maps the mean to a linear combination of covariates. Parameters include regression coefficients, a dispersion or shape scalar, and a dependence-strength scalar. Inference places prior laws on all parameters and draws from the joint posterior by iterative stochastic sampling. Competing marginal choices are compared by a leave-one-out predictive criterion computed as a sum of log predictive densities. The procedure is validated by repeatedly generating synthetic data under each candidate generating mechanism, refitting all candidates, and tabulating recovery of parameters and frequency of correct selection.
