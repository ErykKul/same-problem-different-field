MECHANISM: A sequential interaction generates a joint process of states, scalar costs, and actions that need not satisfy a one-step memoryless property. A parameter vector is updated online by a stochastic-approximation recursion of the form new parameter equals old parameter plus a decaying step times a random matrix-vector residual built from feature vectors evaluated at consecutive states. The target is a linear-in-features approximation of an accumulated discounted-cost functional over the span of fixed basis functions. Convergence is established by averaging the random coefficient matrix and offset vector against an assumed long-run invariant distribution, requiring the averaged matrix to be positive definite. The driving noise is non-stationary, so the error term is decomposed via a Poisson-equation solution into a martingale-difference part plus telescoping summable remainders, with a summable strong-mixing coefficient giving uniform second-moment control. A supermartingale argument with stopping-time truncation and the standard step-size conditions (sum diverges, sum of squares converges) shows the iterate converges almost surely to the solution of the averaged linear system. The limit is identified as the unique fixed point of the composition of an orthogonal projection onto the basis span and a contraction operator defined from the invariant-distribution conditional expectations, because projection is non-expansive and the operator contracts in the weighted norm. For the action-augmented variant with a greedy minimization inside the update, the composed operator is generally not a contraction, so convergence is shown only under exact representability, a covariance-dominance condition, or piecewise-constant basis functions reducing it to a tabular recursion.
