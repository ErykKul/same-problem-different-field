MECHANISM: For each observed entity a fixed-length vector of measured quantities with associated uncertainties is to be explained by a forward model controlled by a handful of continuous parameters. The forward model deterministically maps a parameter vector to a predicted observation vector through a chain of physically motivated component functions, with an overall amplitude treated as a free parameter. A likelihood is defined as a Gaussian in the weighted squared residual between predicted and measured vectors, equivalent up to a constant to minus one-half a quadratic misfit statistic. By the standard inversion, the posterior over parameters is proportional to this likelihood times a prior, with priors taken flat over bounded ranges. Rather than evaluating the model on a dense exhaustive grid, the posterior is explored with a random-walk sampler: from the current state a candidate is drawn from a proposal density and accepted with probability equal to the minimum of one and the ratio of posterior-times-proposal terms, which enforces detailed balance and makes the chain's stationary distribution equal to the posterior. Several chains are run from dispersed starts; an initial transient is discarded and the chains are subsampled to reduce autocorrelation. Convergence is monitored by comparing within-chain and between-chain variance. Marginal one-dimensional distributions and means for any parameter are obtained simply by histogramming the retained samples, and parameter degeneracies appear directly as correlated sample clouds. Recovery is validated on synthetic data and quantified by a correlation coefficient between injected and recovered values.
