MECHANISM: A trained black-box scoring map assigns one of several discrete labels to a high-dimensional input by taking the index of the largest output component. A given input has been displaced by a small added vector so that its assigned label flips; the magnitude of this displacement is bounded in a norm. The method receives only such displaced inputs and seeks to recover the original label by applying a second, opposite displacement that moves the point back across the separating surface. The displacement direction is obtained from the gradient of a loss of the map evaluated at the current point with respect to its current (incorrect) label, exploiting that the point lies near the boundary so a small reverse step suffices. Several stepping rules are used: a single signed-gradient step combined with a line search over magnitude; an iterative small-step variant with projection back into a norm ball each iteration; and a rule that linearizes every pairwise boundary, selects the nearest one by a ratio of score gap to gradient norm, and projects onto it, repeating until the label changes. Because every input is assumed already displaced, stepping continues without a preset magnitude until the predicted label changes. A linearized binary-boundary argument justifies that the reverse direction approximately negates the original displacement. Effectiveness is measured by the fraction of inputs whose recovered label matches the original, and by cosine similarity between the reverse step and the negated original displacement.
