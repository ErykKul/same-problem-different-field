MECHANISM: From several large record sets, per-stratum rates are constructed by dividing a scaled count of one category by the total count across categories, indexed by group, location, and time. A conditional decomposition relates each stratum rate to a marginal rate and a composition share via the elementary identity for conditional probabilities. The central estimand is obtained by fitting a linear model of a transformed outcome on a saturated set of time indicators interacted with a continuous per-unit exposure score, plus additive categorical controls, by ordinary least squares; the interaction coefficients trace how the outcome shifts with exposure over time, and a monotone transform of each coefficient converts it to a relative change. A second linear model regresses a duration outcome on cohort indicators interacted with exposure together with many fixed-effect blocks, estimated over millions of rows. An exposure score for each unit is computed as the inner product between a vector of semantic-similarity weights over a fixed task list and a binary task-exposure vector, then averaged over relevant subsets. For inference on derived proportional-difference statistics, the sampling variance is obtained by a first-order (delta-method) propagation under a multivariate-normal approximation to the underlying binomial proportions, yielding closed-form standard errors. Pairwise comparisons across groups use standard-normal z-statistics, both via the propagated variance and via a pooled two-proportion form, evaluated pointwise across time with associated tail probabilities. A re-weighting step matches the sample composition to an external reference distribution and the regressions are repeated.
DOMAIN: labor economics, technology and employment
STRUCTURE: dense linear algebra
DATA_OBJECT: set or table
INFERENCE: frequentist point estimate
PROBLEM_FORM: estimation
DISTRIBUTION: continuous; gaussian
COMPLEXITY: closed-form
