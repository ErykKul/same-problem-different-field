MECHANISM: A candidate rule is summarized over a window of sequential observations by several scalar statistics: the mean per-observation gain, its dispersion, a standardized excess-mean statistic comparing the mean against a baseline mean scaled by the standard error, a goodness-of-fit coefficient measuring consistency, and a one-sided dispersion of adverse outcomes. These are combined multiplicatively into a single composite objective, with the standardized statistic passed through a logarithm acting as a soft significance gate anchored at unity, and the adverse-dispersion term in the denominator, plus piecewise edge-case handling and a minimum-sample threshold for numerical stability. Rule parameters are tuned by derivative-free random search over a fixed evaluation budget to maximize the composite objective on a training window. Generalization is assessed by rolling-origin splits with a fixed training span, validation span, step, and an embargo gap, producing many sequential train/validation pairs, and the ratio of validation to training performance is tracked. Stability is probed by repeating each configuration under many random seeds. The composite objective is benchmarked against simpler single-statistic objectives under identical budgets and protocols. Paired location tests with effect-size reporting compare out-of-sample outcomes across objectives, while emphasizing that these are average-behavior comparisons rather than selection-corrected significance. A lightweight friction-sensitivity check subtracts per-action costs to confirm the ordering is stable. The core computation is black-box parameter optimization of a hand-designed composite score with resampling-based out-of-sample evaluation.
