MECHANISM: The object of study is an exact identity stating that the ensemble average of the exponential of a negative scaled quantity equals the exponential of a negative scaled difference of two potentials. Applying convexity of the logarithm (a Jensen-type inequality) to this identity yields a one-sided inequality bounding the average of the quantity below by the potential difference. The identity is derived from first principles by writing the average as a path integral over trajectories of a coupled system driven by a time-varying control parameter. The trajectory measure is factorized into a product of single-step transition kernels using a Markov assumption and the chain rule for conditional probabilities. Choosing the initial distribution to be the equilibrium (exponential-of-negative-energy) form and invoking a detailed-balance-type relation collapses the telescoping product, leaving only a ratio of normalizing constants identified with the potential difference. Separately, an alternative derivation expands a thermodynamic potential to second order around an equilibrium point, treats fluctuations with a Gaussian density whose precision is the Hessian, and integrates a linear-response path to recover the same inequality. The fluctuation covariance is obtained as the inverse of the Hessian matrix. A further generalization replaces the finite-dimensional integral with a functional integral over fields to obtain a convexity bound for the partition functional. Throughout, the results are characterizations and bounds rather than numerical computations.
