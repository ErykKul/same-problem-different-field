MECHANISM: The paper develops spatially-varying coefficient (SVC) occupancy models to estimate species-environment relationships using hierarchical Bayesian inference. The method assumes a Bernoulli distribution for detection-nondetection data and models spatially-varying coefficients via Nearest Neighbor Gaussian Processes (NNGP) for spatial smoothing. Pólya-Gamma data augmentation is applied to accelerate Gibbs sampling within the hierarchical Bayesian framework. For multi-species models, spatial factor dimension reduction is used to handle large numbers of species by projecting species-specific coefficients onto a shared low-dimensional spatial basis. Posterior inference is performed via Gibbs sampling, which iteratively updates parameters including spatially-varying coefficients, hyperparameters, and latent variables. The hierarchical structure allows uncertainty in coefficient estimates to propagate through all levels of the model. Posterior predictive maps of SVCs are generated by sampling from the joint posterior distribution of parameters and spatial coefficients. The method is implemented in the spOccupancy R package, which provides functions for model fitting, prediction, and uncertainty quantification. The models are applied to real-world data on grassland bird species, jointly estimating spatially-varying relationships between temperature and occurrence probability across the U.S. The approach explicitly accounts for nonstationarity in species-environment relationships and improves upon single-species models by leveraging shared spatial patterns across multiple species.
