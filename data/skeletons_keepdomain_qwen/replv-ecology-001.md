MECHANISM: The paper constructs explicit Lyapunov functions for three competitive Lotka-Volterra systems to analyze their global dynamical behaviors. For each system, the Lyapunov function is defined as a scalar-valued function on the state space, with properties ensuring it decreases along trajectories of the system. The function is constructed by solving partial differential equations derived from the system's equations, ensuring it is positive definite and its time derivative is negative definite. The method is applied to the general 2-dimensional case, a 3-dimensional model, and the May-Leonard model. The Lyapunov function's structure is tailored to each system's specific interactions and parameters. The analysis reveals bistable and cyclical behaviors by examining the function's critical points and their stability. The first two examples are generalized gradient systems, where the Lyapunov function's derivative aligns with the system's vector field. The May-Leonard model, not a generalized gradient system, requires a different construction approach. The method provides insights into limit cycles in 3-dimensional systems by leveraging the Lyapunov function's properties. The paper emphasizes the explicit construction process and its application to different system dimensions and models.
