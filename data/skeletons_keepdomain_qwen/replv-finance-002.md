It asymptotically derives these solutions using a recent result on random fixed point equations. The agents adapt between risky and risk-free investment strategies to maximize expected returns, and the paper reduces the complex system to an ordinary differential equation (ODE) that captures the replicator dynamics governing inter-agent interactions. It proves that equilibrium strategies converge almost surely to an attractor of the ODE and derives conditions for the emergence of a mixed evolutionary stable strategy (ESS), where expected returns of both populations equalize. The paper also shows that average dynamics based on large samples inherently avert systemic risk events by minimizing default fractions. Monte Carlo simulations are used to verify that the ODE-derived equilibrium accurately represents the limit of the dynamics. The analysis combines deterministic ODE solving, probabilistic convergence proofs, and stochastic simulation to validate theoretical results. The computational steps include solving fixed point equations, transforming the system into an ODE, proving convergence properties, and simulating the dynamics under varying parameters.
