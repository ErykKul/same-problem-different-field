MECHANISM: The paper computes the π-electronic structure of Möbius carbon nanobelts (CNBs) using the simple Hückel molecular orbital (SHMO) theory. It constructs the adjacency matrix of the Möbius CNB graph, which encodes bond connectivity and phase inversion topology. The eigenvalues and eigenvectors of this matrix are calculated, showing invariance under different placements of sign inversion in the atomic orbital basis. This invariance ensures that SHMO results remain consistent regardless of where the phase inversion occurs. The method compares SHMO-derived molecular orbitals (MOs) with density functional theory (DFT)-computed π MOs, using a technique that localizes and re-delocalizes DFT canonical MOs. Coulson bond orders are derived from SHMO eigenvalues and compared to DFT Wiberg bond indices for C–C bonds. The HOMO–LUMO gaps from SHMO are validated against GFN2-xTB calculations for multiple topoisomers of Möbius CNBs. The lower-lying π MOs are shown to have an odd number of nodal planes and exhibit quasidegeneracy due to phase inversion in Möbius macrocycles. The adjacency matrix's eigenstructure directly determines the electronic properties, and the computational steps involve matrix construction, eigen-decomposition, and comparison with empirical data from DFT and GFN2-xTB. The method relies on graph-theoretic representations of molecular topology and linear algebra operations on the adjacency matrix.
