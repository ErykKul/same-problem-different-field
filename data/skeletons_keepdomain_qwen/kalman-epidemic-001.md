MECHANISM: The paper computes a real-time forecasting framework for influenza outbreaks by assimilating web-based estimates of local infection rates into a predictive model. The process begins with initializing forecasts using historical data and a baseline model of seasonal influenza dynamics. Real-time data from web-based sources (e.g., search trends or clinical reports) are periodically integrated into the model through a data assimilation technique, which adjusts the model's state variables to align with observed infection rates. Ensemble forecasts are generated by running multiple simulations with perturbed initial conditions or model parameters, producing a distribution of possible outbreak trajectories. The spread of the ensemble forecasts is analyzed to quantify uncertainty in predictions, particularly the timing and magnitude of peak influenza activity. The method relies on a continuous-time mathematical model of disease transmission, parameterized to reflect local epidemiological conditions. Forecasts are validated retrospectively using historical data from 2003–2008 in New York City, comparing predicted peak timing against actual outbreak data. The computational steps include iterative model updates, ensemble generation, and statistical analysis of forecast uncertainty. The output is a probabilistic prediction of outbreak timing, with confidence intervals derived from the ensemble spread. The model does not explicitly simulate individual behaviors or use discrete event modeling, instead focusing on aggregate population-level dynamics.
