MECHANISM: The paper analyzes a three-component reaction-diffusion system modeling the spatial spread of farmers and hunter-gatherers. The model is governed by partial differential equations with diffusion terms for each population and reaction terms representing interactions. The authors perform numerical simulations to observe spreading behaviors and apply formal linearization around steady states to derive analytical approximations. They identify four distinct spreading regimes based on parameter values, including scenarios where hunter-gatherer motility exceeds that of farmers. A key result is the demonstration of a logarithmic phase drift in front positions, analogous to the scalar KPP equation's behavior. The analysis involves solving eigenvalue problems for linearized systems to determine spreading speeds and stability. The paper addresses the challenge of the non-applicability of the comparison principle by constructing upper and lower bounds for solutions. It also investigates the asymptotic long-time behavior of solutions, showing convergence to traveling wave profiles with logarithmic corrections. The computational approach combines numerical experimentation with formal asymptotic analysis to validate the observed spreading patterns.
