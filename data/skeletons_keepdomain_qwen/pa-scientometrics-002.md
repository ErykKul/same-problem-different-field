MECHANISM: The paper analyzes scholarly networks to identify mechanisms behind the "rich get richer" and "fit get richer" phenomena. It constructs a citation network where nodes represent papers and edges represent citations. The network is analyzed over time to track how highly cited papers (rich) and high-impact papers (fit) accumulate more citations and collaborations. The method uses statistical models to quantify the influence of existing citations and collaboration patterns on future network growth. It applies regression analysis to estimate the strength of preferential attachment and other network dynamics. The paper also examines the role of authorship networks, measuring how co-authorship patterns reinforce existing connections. It uses centrality metrics (e.g., degree, betweenness) to identify key nodes in the network. The analysis is performed on data from the Strategic Management Journal, with time-series data on citations and collaborations. The paper does not propose a novel algorithm but applies standard network analysis techniques to empirical data. The results are interpreted in the context of scholarly communication patterns.
