MECHANISM: The paper computes cosmological $N$-body simulations to model the gravitational evolution of dark matter particles across cosmic time. It simulates the motion of 2.1 trillion particles in a 2.0 $h^{-1}$Gpc box and 262 billion particles in a 140 $h^{-1}$Mpc box, tracking their trajectories under gravity. From these simulations, it calculates dark matter power spectra by Fourier-transforming the density field and analyzing the resulting statistical properties. It derives halo and subhalo mass functions by identifying gravitationally bound structures via density-based clustering algorithms and measuring their mass distributions. The paper fits a mass-concentration model to simulation data, estimating parameters that describe how halo concentration evolves with mass and redshift. It compares the model's predictions to simulation results, quantifying agreement within 5% across eight orders of magnitude in halo mass. The analysis reveals an upturn in the mass-concentration relation for all halos at $z \gtrsim 0.5$, detected through statistical comparison of simulated halo populations. The computational pipeline includes particle integration, density field reconstruction, structure finding, and statistical parameter estimation. No explicit algorithmic method beyond standard $N$-body techniques is described. The results are validated by comparing simulated power spectra to theoretical expectations and by demonstrating consistency across different simulation resolutions. The paper releases simulation outputs as part of a public data release, enabling independent analysis of the computed structures and statistics.
