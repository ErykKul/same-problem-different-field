MECHANISM: The paper computes the spectral decomposition of time-series data representing UK sectoral output to identify frequency-domain characteristics of business cycles. It transforms sectoral output time-series into the frequency domain using Fourier analysis, isolating dominant frequencies and their amplitudes. The method distinguishes between white noise processes (uniform power across frequencies) and non-white noise processes (structured frequency patterns) by comparing spectral density estimates. It examines how institutional features of sectors influence the deviation from white noise assumptions, quantifying the persistence and cyclical components in output data. The analysis involves estimating periodograms or spectral density functions for each sector, identifying peaks and troughs in the frequency spectrum, and comparing these across sectors. The paper further investigates the empirical output of UK GDP sectors, using statistical tests to detect significant differences in their spectral characteristics. The computational steps include preprocessing time-series data for stationarity, applying windowing techniques to reduce spectral leakage, and aggregating results across sectors to highlight commonalities and divergences in business cycle patterns. The method does not employ probabilistic models or optimization; it is purely descriptive and analytical in the frequency domain.
