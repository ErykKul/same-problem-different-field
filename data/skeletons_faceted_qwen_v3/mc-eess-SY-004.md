MECHANISM: The paper computes the dynamic interactions among smart inverters (SIs) operating under different control strategies in a low-voltage distribution network. It models power flow equations to simulate voltage and power responses under varying grid conditions. The method defines control curves for reactive and active power modulation based on local voltage measurements. It applies these curves to simulate SI behavior in three modes: Constant Power Factor (CPF), Volt-VAR, and Volt-Watt. The simulation evaluates how changes in one SI group's control mode affect voltage regulation and power dynamics in another group. It uses impedance characteristics to differentiate between resistive and inductive grid behaviors, calculating voltage sensitivity to active and reactive power injections. The model incorporates real-time simulation of a standardized CIGRE LV network, applying step changes in irradiance and grid voltage to observe transient responses. It quantifies active and reactive power outputs, power factor, and voltage deviations at specific network nodes. The analysis compares outcomes across control modes to assess their impact on system stability and efficiency. The method relies on deterministic equations derived from Ohm’s law and power flow relationships, without probabilistic or statistical inference.
