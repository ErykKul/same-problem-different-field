MECHANISM: The paper computes the long-term behavior of a unitary Floquet dynamics generated by a translationally-invariant Clifford Quantum Cellular Automaton (QCA). The QCA is represented as a linear transformation acting on a space of Laurent polynomials with coefficients in a finite field. The transformation maps each monomial to another monomial, and the evolution of the system is determined by iterating this transformation. The paper defines a measure of operator support (Hamming weight) and shows that under certain conditions, this measure grows unboundedly over time. This unbounded growth is linked to the system's thermalization, where the expectation values of local observables approach those of the infinite-temperature state. The analysis distinguishes between strong and weak thermalization, where weak thermalization allows for exceptions in a negligible subset of times. The paper proves that strongly diffusive QCAs (those with unbounded operator support growth) thermalize a broad class of initial states, including short-range entangled states near equilibrium. The proofs rely on algebraic properties of the transformation matrix and its iterates, ensuring that no nontrivial monomial remains periodic. The correspondence between QCAs and classical cellular automata is leveraged to translate quantum dynamics into classical linear transformations, enabling the application of ergodic theory. The paper also identifies gaps in prior proofs and provides corrected arguments for weak thermalization. The computational core involves verifying the absence of periodic eigenvalues in the transformation matrix and analyzing the asymptotic behavior of its powers.
