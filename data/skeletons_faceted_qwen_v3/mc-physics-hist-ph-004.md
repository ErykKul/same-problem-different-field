MECHANISM: This paper presents a philosophical framework for understanding consciousness through the separation of three layers: appearance (internal experience), access (reportability and control), and formal structure (invariant organizational features). It defines a conscious episode as a temporally bounded regime where internal distinctions are integrated and stabilized, enabling action and report. Integration is quantified as a surplus of predictive power over partitions, expressed via mutual information across time. A self is modeled as a dynamical role—a self-index that binds episodes through continuity of control and prediction. The paper emphasizes operational constraints: consciousness must be inferable from records (reports, neural signatures) and cannot be reduced to behavior or ineffable essence. It introduces measures for integration (CΔ(t)) and memory retention (MΔ(t)), but these are conceptual rather than algorithmic. The framework avoids metaphysical claims, focusing on structural conditions for a unified perspective and the limits of measurement. It does not propose a new computational method, simulation, or mathematical model beyond information-theoretic concepts.
