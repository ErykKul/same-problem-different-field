MECHANISM: This paper synthesizes interdisciplinary evidence to develop a socio-technical framework for identifying and governing risks associated with clinical speech-to-text systems. It analyzes how technical performance, workflow integration, ethical practices, governance structures, regulatory compliance, and sociocultural trust interact to shape documentation outcomes. The study identifies key risks such as inconsistent consent practices, performance disparities for diverse speech patterns, automation complacency, data governance challenges, and accountability gaps. It constructs a six-layer conceptual model spanning technical infrastructure, human interaction, ethical transparency, organizational governance, legal compliance, and sociocultural trust. The framework emphasizes transparency, patient autonomy, documentation integrity, and accountable governance to enable safe and equitable adoption of speech-based documentation technologies. It proposes governance mechanisms to address technical reliability, workflow oversight, ethical disclosure, vendor accountability, legal compliance, and trust-building strategies. The analysis is based on a structured narrative synthesis of literature from technical ASR research, clinical workflow studies, ethical guidance, and regulatory sources. The paper does not compute a mathematical or algorithmic method but instead provides a qualitative analysis and conceptual framework for socio-technical risk governance in clinical AI systems.
