MECHANISM: The paper computes the collective dynamics of chiral active particles using a system of overdamped Langevin equations for particle motion and orientation. Each particle's position evolves based on self-propulsion velocity, interparticle forces from a Weeks–Chandler–Andersen potential, and stochastic noise. Orientation changes are governed by a chiral angular velocity term, vision-based torque from neighbors within a forward-facing cone, polar-alignment torque from neighbors within a circular range, and rotational noise. The vision-based torque depends on the relative angle between the neighbor and the particle's orientation, weighted by an exponential decay in distance. Polar-alignment torque averages neighbor orientations within a fixed range. The model incorporates parameters for chirality, vision angle, alignment strength, and Péclet number. Simulations track particle positions and orientations over time, using clustering susceptibility (based on largest cluster size) to classify emergent phases. The analysis identifies distinct collective states by varying the ratio of alignment to vision maneuverability and reduced chirality, revealing how these factors shape morphological transitions between spinners, vortices, ripples, worm-like swarms, and aggregates.
