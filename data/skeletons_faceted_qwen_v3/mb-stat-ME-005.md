MECHANISM: The paper computes a hierarchical probabilistic model that jointly estimates total mortality rates and cause-specific death proportions. It assumes that total death counts follow a Poisson distribution with mean proportional to an overall mortality rate, while cause-specific death counts follow a Dirichlet-Multinomial distribution parameterized by the total count and a vector of compositional weights. These weights are modeled as a softmax transformation of latent linear predictors, which are themselves expressed as additive combinations of age, period, and cause-specific effects. The model enforces compositional coherence by ensuring that the sum of cause-specific probabilities equals one. Bayesian inference is used to estimate parameters, with priors assigned to intercepts, dispersion terms, and smooth effects. The smooth effects for age and period are modeled as second-order Gaussian random walks to capture gradual demographic trends. Posterior sampling is performed via Hamiltonian Monte Carlo to quantify uncertainty in both total rates and cause-specific proportions. Forecasts are generated by extending the random walk dynamics for period effects and applying the estimated model parameters to future time points. The method explicitly accounts for overdispersion in both total counts and compositional weights, and uncertainty is propagated through the joint generative model rather than post-hoc adjustments.
