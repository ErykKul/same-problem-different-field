MECHANISM: The paper introduces Scrooge $k$-designs, which are approximate distributions of pure quantum states that match the first $k$ moments of the Scrooge ensemble, a maximally entropic distribution constrained by physical conditions. The method involves analyzing ensembles of quantum states through their statistical moments, defined as moment operators over symmetric subspaces of Hilbert spaces. These moments are compared using trace distance to quantify similarity between ensembles. The accessible information of an ensemble, a measure of classical information extractable via measurements, is bounded by the von Neumann entropy and subentropy of the average state. The paper proves three theorems: (1) chaotic unitary dynamics generate approximate Scrooge $k$-designs under low-purity conditions; (2) measuring a subsystem of a global Scrooge $2k$-design yields a local Scrooge $k$-design; (3) scrambling measurements on an entangled state with a unitary $2k$-design produce a local Scrooge $k$-design. These results are derived using quantum information-theoretic tools, including moment operators, trace distance, and accessible information bounds. Numerical simulations validate the necessity of coherence, entanglement, and information scrambling for Scrooge behavior. The framework generalizes Haar $k$-designs to constrained physical scenarios, enabling efficient quantum circuit implementations with logarithmic depth.
