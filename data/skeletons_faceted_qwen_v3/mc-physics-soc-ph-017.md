MECHANISM: The paper computes an evolutionary game model integrating upstream and downstream reciprocity with costly punishment. The model defines three strategies: unconditional cooperation (ALLC), unconditional defection (ALLD), and integrated strong reciprocity (ISR), which combines helping after being helped (upstream), helping based on reputation (downstream), and punishing defectors. Payoffs are calculated based on interactions between strategies, with reputation updates determined by social norms. The replicator equation governs population dynamics, tracking frequencies of ALLC, ALLD, and ISR. Equilibria are derived by solving payoff equations, analyzing conditions under which ISR and ALLD coexist stably. The model evaluates whether punishment raises collective welfare above the no-punishment baseline by comparing average payoffs at equilibria. Stability under cognitive costs is assessed by introducing a small penalty for conditional strategies, showing ISR remains robust while standard reciprocity without upstream components collapses. The analysis demonstrates that strategic diversity (coexistence of ISR and ALLD) enables punishment to be productive and defectors to act as evolutionary shields, stabilizing cooperation.
