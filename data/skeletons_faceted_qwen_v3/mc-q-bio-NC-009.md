MECHANISM: This paper investigates the relationship between neurobiological responses to smoking and social cues in adolescents using a pre-registered fMRI-based neuromarker called the Neurobiological Craving Signature (NCS). It compares brain activity patterns in experimental smokers and non-smokers during exposure to smoking-related, neutral, and romantic social cues. The study examines how these responses correlate with self-reported craving, monthly smoking frequency, and environmental tobacco smoke (ETS) exposure. It does not describe the mathematical or algorithmic construction of the NCS itself, only its application to fMRI data for hypothesis testing. The analysis involves statistical comparisons of group differences in NCS responses and their associations with behavioral and physiological measures. No novel computational method is presented, only the use of an existing fMRI-derived biomarker in a specific empirical context.
