MECHANISM: The paper computes a non-Hermitian modification of the pilot-wave theory by introducing a complex gauge coupling parameter, which implements local scale invariance. The conserved current density is generalized from the Born rule density $|\psi|^2$ to a trajectory-dependent ratio $|\psi|^2/\mathds{1}^2[\mathcal{C}]$, where $\mathds{1}[\mathcal{C}]$ is a scale factor determined by the pilot-wave trajectory $\mathcal{C}$. The method involves solving the Schrödinger, Pauli, and Dirac equations with an external electromagnetic field using a Weyl covariant derivative, which incorporates both local phase and scale invariance. The quantum potential is modified to include terms dependent on the imaginary component of the coupling parameter $e_I$, leading to non-Hermitian kinetic energy terms. The continuity equation is transformed into a Weyl-covariant form, ensuring conservation with respect to covariant derivatives rather than ordinary derivatives. The gauge-invariant current density is derived as a functional of the pilot-wave trajectory, and the theory is shown to reduce to orthodox quantum theory when $e_I = 0$. The approach is applied to multiple particles, spin-1/2 systems, and relativistic cases, with trajectory-dependent empirical predictions. The equilibrium probability density and uniqueness of the guidance equation are analyzed, and the method is extended to quantum field theory for axion-electromagnetic interactions. The computational steps involve solving differential equations, applying gauge transformations, and deriving modified continuity and Hamilton-Jacobi equations with trajectory-dependent scaling factors.
