MECHANISM: The paper computes a regression framework using knowledge distillation to predict a quantity from two types of descriptors. A teacher model is trained on a first type of descriptor, which encodes rich structural information, while a student model is trained on a second type of descriptor, which is less informative but more widely available. During training, the student model is supervised by both ground-truth labels and outputs from the teacher model, as well as intermediate representations generated by the teacher. The student model learns to approximate the teacher's predictions and internal features through a combination of supervised regression loss and distillation loss. The teacher model's features are treated as fixed targets during backpropagation, while the student model's parameters are updated to minimize the combined loss. The framework ensures that the student model can make accurate predictions using only the second type of descriptor during inference. The teacher model's higher capacity allows it to capture complex interactions, while the student model's lighter architecture enables efficient deployment. The distillation process transfers knowledge from the teacher to the student by aligning their latent representations through feature-level distillation. The final prediction is generated by a linear head applied to the student model's output. This approach bridges the gap between data availability and predictive accuracy by leveraging structural information during training while relying on sequence data during inference.
