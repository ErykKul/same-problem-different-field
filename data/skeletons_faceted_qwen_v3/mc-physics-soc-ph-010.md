MECHANISM: The paper develops a model where heavy-tailed disruptions in flow networks emerge from Pareto-tailed external inputs. The framework defines a flow network with vertices having stochastic weights following a Pareto distribution. Commodity production and demand are determined by vertex weights, subject to capacity constraints. Flow is distributed via a parametrized mechanism ensuring commodity balance. A cascade of failures occurs when overloaded edges fail, reducing capacity and triggering further failures. The cascade cost, quantifying system disruption, is analyzed using a cost function dependent on flow and capacity. Under mild assumptions, large cascade costs are shown to occur when one vertex has disproportionately high weight. The tail distribution of cascade cost inherits the scale-free property from vertex weights, with a transformed tail exponent determined by the cost function's parameter. The model applies to any network topology and is validated through examples in power, traffic, and processing networks. The proof involves analyzing parametric convex optimization problems, establishing scalability and continuity via transformations and contradiction arguments. The framework demonstrates that heavy-tailed disruptions are robust across domains due to the persistence of Pareto-tailed inputs.
