MECHANISM: The paper computes the effect of three informational interventions on behavioral intention through a randomized controlled trial. Participants are assigned to four groups: three treatment groups receiving distinct interventions (cost-based, range-based, norm-based) and a control group. Each intervention provides targeted information to address specific barriers (economic, technical, social). Perception scores are measured before and after treatment using a 100-point allocation task between conventional and electric vehicles. Statistical analysis employs ordinary least squares (OLS) regression to evaluate treatment effects on perception score differences, with dummy variables for treatment groups and demographic controls. Logistic regression further assesses whether changes in perception scores correlate with shifts in vehicle preference (binary: EV vs conventional). The methodology isolates causal effects by ensuring homogeneity across groups through stratified sampling and random assignment. No computational models or simulations are used; the core computation is statistical inference from experimental data.
