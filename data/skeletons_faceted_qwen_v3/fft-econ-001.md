MECHANISM: The paper computes the spectral distribution of a time series to analyze deviations from a white noise process. It begins by assuming a stationary time series with continuous mean-square properties and higher moments up to the fourth order. The spectral distribution function is derived using Fourier transforms, relating the autocorrelation function to the power spectral density. For a pure white noise process, the spectral components follow specific statistical distributions (chi-square for the zero-lag term and normal for other lags). The study compares observed spectra to theoretical white noise benchmarks, identifying patterns of stochastic dominance in frequency ranges. It defines three propositions: lower frequency components stochastically dominate higher ones (compounding effect), higher frequencies dominate lower ones (mean-reverting tendencies), or mixed complexity where neither dominates consistently. These patterns are analyzed through integral transforms of the time series and statistical tests on the spectral density function. The method uses equations to model the relationship between time-domain autocorrelations and frequency-domain power spectra, and evaluates the behavior of the time series under different frequency ranges. The analysis involves scaling the difference between the observed process and a pure white noise process by the sample size, leading to asymptotic properties as the sample size approaches infinity. The results are interpreted in terms of the time series' cyclical behavior, distinguishing between persistent, mean-reverting, or mixed patterns based on the spectral characteristics.
