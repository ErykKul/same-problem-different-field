It models Compton scattering using the Klein-Nishina formula to calculate differential cross-sections as a function of scattering angle and photon energy. The Minimum Detectable Polarization (MDP) is calculated using a formula that incorporates source and background count rates, observation time, and the modulation factor. The paper simulates and measures energy deposition in scintillator materials, analyzing spectral responses to radioactive sources. It evaluates light yield, optical crosstalk, and energy resolution by comparing measured charge signals to known energy peaks from isotopes. The dynamic range of the detector is determined by mapping ADC values to deposited energy using calibration sources. The analysis includes fitting spectral data to extract photopeak positions and calculating energy resolution as a function of energy. The paper also assesses the impact of material properties on photon detection efficiency and light collection, using spectral compatibility metrics between scintillator emission and sensor quantum efficiency. The computational steps involve signal shaping, threshold optimization, and characterization of electronic noise and pile-up effects in the detector chain.
