MECHANISM: The paper computes the stability and phase transitions of topological spin textures under uniaxial strain using a Hamiltonian-based model. The system consists of classical spins on a kagome lattice with exchange interactions and Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya (DM) couplings, both in-plane and out-of-plane. Strain modifies the exchange couplings by altering bond lengths, while DM couplings are treated as nearly strain-independent. The model incorporates a chiral spin liquid background to stabilize unconventional phases. Simulations use Spin-Lattice Dynamics (SLD) and Monte Carlo methods to explore how strain affects the phase diagram. SLD evolves atomic positions and spin orientations via Langevin equations, with strain modifying the exchange couplings as a function of bond length. Monte Carlo simulations sample equilibrium configurations by varying temperature and magnetic field, using chirality observables and structure factors to identify phases. Compressive strain increases skyrmion density and stabilizes them at higher temperatures, while tensile strain reduces skyrmion density and promotes transitions to other phases. Strain also induces topological charge changes, converting skyrmions (Q=-1) to antiskyrmions (Q=+1) at specific strain levels. The analysis compares results with a conventional skyrmion model to highlight the role of exchange anisotropy and chiral spin liquid interactions.
