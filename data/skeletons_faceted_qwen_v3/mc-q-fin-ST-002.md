MECHANISM: The paper computes a causal framework based on non-invariance of second-order dependence operators under admissible, order-preserving temporal deformations. It defines directional influence as sensitivity of these operators to such deformations, quantified through orthogonally invariant spectral functionals. The method constructs a family of operators indexed by temporal shifts, evaluates their spectral properties (e.g., largest eigenvalues, Frobenius norms), and defines causality as non-invariance of these summaries across the deformation set. Under linear Gaussian assumptions, this reduces to linear Granger causality. For inference, it employs shift-based randomization to compute exact or asymptotically valid tests for non-invariance, leveraging group invariance under the null hypothesis. The framework avoids parametric assumptions and focuses on collective, system-level dependencies rather than pairwise predictability. It extends to spectral distributions of operators, capturing bulk redistribution effects, and uses variational characterizations (e.g., operator norms, canonical correlations) to formalize directional dependence. The method is designed for high-dimensional systems where interventions are infeasible, emphasizing robustness to nonlinearity and distributed influence.
