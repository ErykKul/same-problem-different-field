MECHANISM: The paper computes the impact of time-varying dark energy on the thermal and structural properties of the intergalactic medium (IGM) using cosmological hydrodynamic simulations. It models the expansion history of the universe by solving the Friedmann equation with a parameterized dark energy equation of state (CPL formalism), which evolves as a function of redshift. The simulation tracks the evolution of dark matter and baryonic matter on a uniform Cartesian grid, incorporating cooling and heating processes from the UV background. It calculates the linear growth factor of density perturbations by solving a differential equation derived from the matter-dark energy density ratio. The optical depth of neutral hydrogen along lines of sight is computed as the product of the photoabsorption cross-section and the local HI number density. The transmitted flux power spectrum is derived from synthetic spectra generated across multiple redshifts, quantifying the scale- and redshift-dependent imprints of dark energy. The method compares results between dynamical dark energy models and the ΛCDM reference case, isolating differences in IGM temperature, optical depth, and power spectrum amplitude. The simulation outputs are analyzed to identify deviations in the Lyα forest structure, which are then interpreted as potential observational signatures of dark energy dynamics.
