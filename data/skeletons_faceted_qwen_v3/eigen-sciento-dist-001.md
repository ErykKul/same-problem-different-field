MECHANISM: This paper compares three journal ranking tools—Google Scholar Metrics (GSM), Thomson Reuters’ Journal Citation Reports (JCR), and Scimago Journal Rank (SJR)—by analyzing their coverage, indexing policies, citation metrics, and transparency. It evaluates how each tool calculates journal impact using indicators like the H-index, impact factor, and SJR, while discussing differences in document types indexed, language and geographic coverage, and handling of citation anomalies. The paper does not propose a new computational method but systematically reviews existing methodologies for ranking journals. It contrasts GSM’s reliance on Google Scholar’s broad, uncurated citation data with JCR’s and SJR’s more selective, peer-reviewed approaches. The analysis includes comparisons of how each tool classifies journals into subject categories, standardizes data, and addresses issues like predatory journals or citation manipulation. It also examines user interfaces, update frequencies, and data accessibility features. The goal is to assess whether GSM can serve as a viable alternative to established tools like JCR and SJR, focusing on practical usability and methodological rigor rather than introducing novel algorithms or models.
