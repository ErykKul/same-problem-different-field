MECHANISM: The paper computes a comparative analysis of receptor-ligand interfaces across species using sequence alignment, structural modeling, and electrostatic analysis. First, multiple sequence alignments are generated for receptor and ligand domains to identify conserved and divergent regions. Structural models of receptor-ligand complexes are predicted using deep-learning-based methods, focusing on extracellular domains involved in binding. Interface characteristics, including buried surface area, hydrogen bonds, and salt bridges, are quantified using computational tools. Electrostatic potentials at the interface are calculated by solving the Poisson-Boltzmann equation, mapping charge distributions onto atomic coordinates. Residue-specific electrostatic contributions are analyzed to identify conserved hotspots across species. Structural similarity metrics (RMSD, TM-scores) are computed to compare domain-level conservation between zebrafish, human, and mouse orthologs. Interface electrostatic complementarity is evaluated by normalizing interaction densities relative to interface area. Evolutionary conservation of electrostatic residues is assessed through sequence alignment and residue-type comparisons. The results are integrated to infer how electrostatic and geometric properties of interfaces correlate with regenerative signaling efficiency.
