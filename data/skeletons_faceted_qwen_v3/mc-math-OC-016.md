MECHANISM: This paper computes solutions to non-linear Backward Stochastic Differential Equations (BSDEs) with multiple default jumps, driven by a Brownian motion and $p$ compensated default martingales. The driver of the BSDE incorporates an optional finite variation process $D$, leading to a generalized form involving both continuous and jump components. The method establishes existence and uniqueness of solutions under suitable assumptions on the driver, derives explicit formulas for the first component of the BSDE using adjoint exponential semimartingales, and proves comparison and strict comparison results. The solution depends on whether the finite variation process $D$ is predictable or optional. The computational framework applies these results to pricing and hedging European contingent claims in both linear and non-linear complete markets with multiple defaultable assets. The method involves stochastic calculus, martingale representation properties, and decomposition of processes into predictable and optional components. The paper derives explicit representations for the solution in the case of linear drivers and studies properties of the associated non-linear pricing system. The approach includes defining spaces of stochastic processes with specific integrability conditions, proving a priori estimates, and analyzing the impact of the finite variation process $D$ on the solution structure. The method is applied to financial models where the market is influenced by large sellers affecting default probabilities, leading to non-linear pricing systems. The paper provides two examples of non-linear markets and studies the properties of the conditional evaluation operator derived from the BSDE solution.
