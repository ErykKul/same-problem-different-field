MECHANISM: The paper computes a self-supervised contrastive learning process over a sequence of images, where the model minimizes a loss function that pushes embeddings of temporally adjacent images closer together while separating embeddings of non-adjacent images. The model adapts its internal representations through iterative optimization, gradually forming hierarchical structures where early layers develop sensitivity to local features (edges) and later layers develop sensitivity to global features (shapes). The learning process involves computing gradients of the loss with respect to model parameters, updating parameters via an optimizer (e.g., Adam), and applying these updates over multiple training epochs. The model's architecture includes attention mechanisms that dynamically weigh input features, and the training data consists of a time-series of images generated by a retinal wave simulator. After training, the model's internal representations are analyzed using representational dissimilarity matrices (RDMs) to quantify edge and shape sensitivity, and gradient-based methods to estimate receptive field sizes across layers. The process is deterministic, with no explicit probabilistic modeling of uncertainty. The model's structure emerges through adaptation to the statistical regularities of the training data, without explicit encoding of domain-specific priors. The analysis confirms that the model's hierarchical organization mirrors that of biological visual systems, with increasing receptive field sizes across layers. The method relies on spatial and temporal coherence in the training data, as scrambling the temporal order of images disrupts the emergence of the hierarchical structure.
