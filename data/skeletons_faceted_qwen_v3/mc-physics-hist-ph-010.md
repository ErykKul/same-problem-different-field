MECHANISM: This paper examines the conceptual foundations of causality in physics, arguing that causality is not an intrinsic feature of nature but a construct arising from human perception and statistical patterns. It critiques the assumption of strict causality in quantum field theory and quantum gravity, proposing that causality violations may occur at scales beyond the Planck length. The paper analyzes deterministic and non-deterministic frameworks, demonstrating that causality cannot be meaningfully defined in a fully deterministic universe. It discusses the role of microcausality in quantum field theory, the implications of fakeons (virtual particles) for causality, and the limitations of predictivity in physical systems. The analysis extends to nonlocal theories, Lee-Wick models, and the philosophical implications of abandoning causality as a fundamental principle. The paper concludes that causality is a misleading concept in fundamental physics and should be replaced by statistical descriptions of events. No computational algorithm or mathematical model is proposed; the focus is on conceptual and philosophical critique of causality.
