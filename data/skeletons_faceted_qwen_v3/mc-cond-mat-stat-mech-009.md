MECHANISM: The paper computes a measure of entropy and nullity for a set of operators using a combination of fast Walsh-Hadamard transform (FWHT) and Monte Carlo sampling. The method first applies FWHT to decompose the operators into a structured basis, then partitions them into subsets based on their properties. A Monte Carlo estimator with preconditioning is used to approximate the entropy and nullity by sampling from the partitioned subsets. The algorithm reduces the computational cost per sample from exponential in the number of variables to linear by leveraging the structured decomposition. The sampling process is optimized using a preconditioning step that adjusts the distribution of sampled operators to focus on regions contributing most to the entropy. The method iteratively refines the estimate by increasing the number of samples until convergence, with the number of required samples showing no growth with problem size. The approach is applied to analyze the statistical properties of operators generated by random circuits, quantifying how their structure affects the entropy. The algorithm's efficiency is demonstrated through benchmarks showing the number of samples needed remains constant as the problem size increases. The method combines deterministic transformations with probabilistic sampling to achieve accurate estimation with reduced computational effort.
