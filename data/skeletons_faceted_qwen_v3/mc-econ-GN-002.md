MECHANISM: The paper defines causal effects through counterfactual regimes in environments with interdependent units. It formalizes three regimes—partial equilibrium, local interaction, and network-consistent equilibrium—each specifying how outcomes adjust to treatment changes. The framework uses a potential-outcomes model where outcomes depend on treatment assignments, interaction structures, and predetermined characteristics. It introduces an interaction matrix W to represent dependencies between units, derived from observable characteristics. The paper shows that spatial autoregressive parameters map to distinct causal effects depending on the counterfactual regime. It derives identification conditions for each regime, emphasizing that equilibrium effects require stronger assumptions. A Monte Carlo simulation demonstrates how different regimes produce divergent causal estimates even before estimation. The method relies on explicit counterfactual assumptions to define causal effects, avoiding reliance on stable unit treatment value assumptions. It distinguishes between direct, local, and equilibrium effects, clarifying how spillovers are interpreted under different adjustment rules. The framework does not assume a specific distribution for outcomes but instead focuses on structural relationships between units.
