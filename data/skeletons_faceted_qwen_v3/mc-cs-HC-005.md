MECHANISM: This paper evaluates the feasibility of an LLM-based chatbot for cognitive reappraisal of stress through a structured intervention. The study involves participants engaging in a single-session activity guided by a chatbot, which follows a predefined sequence of questions to help users reflect on stressful situations, identify automatic thoughts, and reinterpret them. The intervention uses natural language processing to adapt to user input, acknowledging responses and requesting clarifications when needed. Pre- and post-intervention measures assess changes in perceived stress intensity, stress mindset, perceived demand, and perceived resources. Sentiment and stress trajectories are analyzed using pre-trained models (RoBERTa sentiment and stress classifiers) and an LLM-based stress rater. Qualitative feedback is gathered through thematic analysis of open-ended responses. The study does not describe the computational methods of the LLMs themselves but focuses on the application of these models in a mental health context. The primary goal is to assess the intervention's impact on stress-related outcomes and identify design challenges in integrating LLMs into digital mental health tools. The analysis includes statistical tests (Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with Benjamini-Hochberg correction) and linguistic feature extraction from user-chatbot conversations. The findings highlight both the potential benefits and the design tensions of using LLMs in structured cognitive reappraisal activities.
