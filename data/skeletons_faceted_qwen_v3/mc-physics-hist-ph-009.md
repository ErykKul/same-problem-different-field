MECHANISM: This paper analyzes the conceptual challenges in generalizing classical work to quantum thermodynamics, focusing on the incompatibility between two classical features of work in quantum regimes. It discusses the no-go theorem by Perarnau-Llobet et al., which argues against the existence of a universal measurement scheme for quantum work that satisfies both operational and energetic conditions. The paper contrasts classical work's roles in mechanics, energetics, thermodynamics, and statistical mechanics, highlighting how these roles diverge in quantum systems. It introduces the two-point measurement scheme (TPM) as a quantum operational approach but shows its limitations in preserving classical consilience. The paper proposes an alternative trajectory-based definition of quantum work using the quantum potential, which avoids the no-go theorem's tension but fails to retain all classical features. It concludes by questioning what criteria would define a successful generalization of work in quantum thermodynamics. The analysis relies on theoretical arguments, mathematical formulations of work-energy theorems, and comparisons between classical and quantum frameworks without introducing new computational methods or algorithms.
