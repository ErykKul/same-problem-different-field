MECHANISM: The paper computes thermodynamic properties of a spin system using two methods. First, it performs a low-temperature series expansion of the partition function by enumerating configurations of spins, calculating their Boltzmann weights, and summing contributions to the partition function. This involves determining counts of configurations, applying exponential transformations to energy terms, and deriving series expansions for free energy, magnetization, and susceptibility. Second, it uses Metropolis Monte Carlo simulations to sample spin configurations, updating spins based on energy differences and Boltzmann factors, then computing thermodynamic quantities by averaging over sampled states. Both methods involve solving self-consistent equations for magnetization and susceptibility, comparing results to assess the accuracy of mean-field approximations, and analyzing critical behavior through parameter variations. The computations are applied to 1D, 2D, and 3D lattices with different interaction terms, and the results are validated against analytical solutions and numerical benchmarks.
