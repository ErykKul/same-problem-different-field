The background rate and triggering kernel are estimated nonparametrically through a stochastic declustering procedure. The model assumes events occur in continuous time and space, with each event contributing a time-dependent influence to the conditional intensity. The estimation process involves iteratively separating background events from those triggered by prior events. The triggering kernel is modeled as a function of time elapsed since prior events and spatial distance. The method does not assume parametric forms for the background or triggering components. The decomposition allows for the identification of self-exciting patterns in event sequences. The stochastic declustering procedure uses probabilistic rules to assign events to background or triggered categories. The model's parameters are inferred from the observed event sequence without explicit likelihood maximization. The method is applied to estimate the spatial and temporal influence of events on subsequent occurrences. The decomposition and estimation steps are repeated until convergence criteria are met. The final model quantifies the baseline risk and the magnitude of self-excitation in the event process.
