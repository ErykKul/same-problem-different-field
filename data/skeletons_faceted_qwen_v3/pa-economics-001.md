MECHANISM: The paper computes a stochastic model for the evolution of a quantity representing wealth, governed by differential equations. Agents transition between two states, affecting the rate of change of the quantity. The model defines a transport term and transition terms in a partial differential equation for the probability density function of the quantity. In steady state, the equations are solved to derive the probability density function, which is shown to follow a beta distribution. The derivation involves defining dimensionless variables, solving the steady-state equations, and normalizing the resulting distribution. The model parameters influence the shape of the distribution through ratios of rate constants. The mean and variance of the quantity are derived analytically from the parameters of the beta distribution. The paper demonstrates that the beta distribution emerges as the non-equilibrium steady-state solution under the specified stochastic dynamics. The model is validated through mathematical analysis rather than empirical data. The approach generalizes to other distributions by adjusting the form of the equations and parameters.
