MECHANISM: This paper evaluates web accessibility and usability in Bangladesh by combining automated assessments with user feedback. It analyzes 212 websites (118 government, 94 non-government) using tools like WAVE, AChecks, and Squidler to check compliance with WCAG 2.2 standards. Automated tools identify technical violations such as contrast issues, missing alt text, and navigation problems. A survey of 103 users captures subjective experiences, including ease of navigation, readability, and security concerns. The study compares government and non-government websites to identify disparities in accessibility, usability, and compliance. It highlights issues like poor mobile responsiveness, inconsistent authentication mechanisms, and lack of assistive technology support. Findings are used to recommend improvements in design practices, policy interventions, and regular audits. The analysis does not introduce new computational methods but applies existing tools and survey techniques to assess real-world accessibility gaps. The study emphasizes interaction complexity and task reliability as key dimensions of accessibility affecting user experience. It does not compute a mathematical model or algorithm but synthesizes empirical data and tool outputs to draw conclusions about accessibility compliance and usability challenges.
