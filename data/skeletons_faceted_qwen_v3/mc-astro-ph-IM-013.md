MECHANISM: The paper computes the surface energy of a material by measuring enthalpy changes during calorimetry experiments and correlating these with surface area measurements. The process involves dissolving bulk and nanoparticle samples in a molten solvent, measuring the heat released, and using these values to calculate enthalpy differences. Surface area is determined via gas adsorption experiments, which quantify the exposed area of nanoparticles. The measured enthalpy differences are decomposed into contributions from surface energy (SE) and interfacial energy (IE) using a linear relationship derived from the formula ΔHex = IE * IA + SE * SA, where IA is the interfacial area and SA is the surface area. Multiple samples with varying synthesis conditions are analyzed to estimate SE and IE by solving the equation for different combinations of IA and SA. The results are averaged across samples to account for variability in crystallite size, phase fractions, and agglomeration. The computation assumes a linear relationship between enthalpy excess and the product of energy terms and area terms, without explicit modeling of uncertainty or probabilistic inference. The final surface energy value is derived by fitting the data to the surface area measurements using a deterministic linear model. The method relies on empirical calibration of the calorimeter and assumes that interfacial energy contributions are negligible compared to surface energy. The calculation does not involve iterative optimization, probabilistic reasoning, or complex numerical methods beyond basic algebraic operations.
