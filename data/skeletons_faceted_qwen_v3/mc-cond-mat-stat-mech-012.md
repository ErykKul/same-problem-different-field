MECHANISM: The paper computes the ground-state properties and phase transitions of a non-Hermitian spin model by solving a Hamiltonian expressed as a sum of terms involving Pauli matrices and a complex field. The Hamiltonian is transformed into a fermionic representation using Jordan-Wigner transformation, then diagonalized via Fourier and Bogoliubov transformations. The ground-state energy density is calculated as the negative average of the absolute values of eigenvalues derived from the diagonalized Hamiltonian. The energy gap is defined as the minimum real part of these eigenvalues, and its closure is used to identify phase transitions. Order parameters are computed using spin correlation functions and cross-correlations, with specific attention to the vector-chiral correlation to distinguish spiral phases. Entanglement entropy is calculated by partitioning the system into subsystems and evaluating the reduced density matrix via the correlation matrix method. The phase diagram is constructed by analyzing the behavior of these observables under varying parameters, including dissipation strength and interaction coefficients. The paper identifies second-order phase transitions through discontinuities in the second derivative of the ground-state energy density and confirms critical behaviors via scaling laws of correlation functions and entanglement entropy. The chirality of spiral phases is determined by the sign of the vector-chiral correlation, and the influence of dissipation on phase boundaries is quantified through parameter-dependent simulations.
