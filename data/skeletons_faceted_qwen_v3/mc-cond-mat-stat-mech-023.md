MECHANISM: The paper computes the energy cost of creating defects (dislocations, vacancies, interstitials) in a two-dimensional one-component plasma (OCP) using numerical simulations. The system is modeled as particles interacting via a logarithmic potential on a cylindrical surface. The energy of defect pairs is calculated by minimizing a potential energy function that includes both Coulomb and elastic contributions. For vacancy-interstitial pairs, the logarithmic Coulomb attraction and elastic displacement energy cancel at long distances, leaving a finite energy cost. The simulation involves optimizing particle positions using conjugate gradient methods to find local minima, analyzing defect configurations, and comparing results to theoretical predictions. Dislocation energy calculations incorporate displacement fields derived from elasticity theory, with corrections for finite system size. The study identifies critical distances where defect pairs transition between configurations and evaluates how preparation history affects dislocation energies. The numerical results validate asymptotic behaviors and challenge the applicability of KTHNY theory to the OCP by showing temperature-dependent defect stability.
