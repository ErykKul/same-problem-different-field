MECHANISM: This paper describes the collection and organization of empirical data on leaf-level and whole-plant physiological responses to controlled climate variables. The dataset includes three-dimensional plant reconstructions, gas exchange measurements of CO2 and H2O fluxes, and environmental parameter logs. These data are structured to enable calibration and evaluation of biophysical models that simulate plant processes such as photosynthesis, stomatal conductance, and transpiration. The dataset captures variations in temperature, vapor pressure deficit, radiation, and CO2 concentration across multiple experimental scenarios. Leaf-level measurements are used to estimate parameters of photosynthesis and stomatal conductance models, while whole-plant flux data provide insights into integrated physiological behavior. The data are annotated with timestamps, environmental conditions, and plant-specific metadata to support model benchmarking and validation. The three-dimensional reconstructions are derived from LiDAR point clouds and used to represent plant architecture for light interception simulations. The dataset is designed to bridge scales from individual leaves to whole plants, enabling both fine-grained spatial analysis and plant-level predictions. No novel computational method or algorithm is proposed; the focus is on data acquisition, curation, and application for model evaluation.
