MECHANISM: The paper describes the creation of a benchmark dataset and evaluation framework for assessing large language model (LLM) agents in analytical tasks involving long documents. It outlines the curation of raw data files, heterogeneous documentation, and expert-written publications from multiple sources to generate 505 analytical queries. These queries require agents to retrieve and integrate information from unstructured documents, perform multi-step computations, and generate executable code. The paper introduces a tool-augmented agent framework (LongTA) to enable document access, retrieval, and code execution during evaluation. It evaluates proprietary and open-source models on this benchmark, revealing performance gaps among state-of-the-art systems. The focus is on systematic evaluation of agent capabilities in real-world, documentation-intensive analytical workflows rather than presenting a novel computational method. The paper emphasizes challenges in applying LLM agents for decision support in high-stakes settings. No mathematical or algorithmic method is proposed or analyzed in detail. The work is primarily empirical, centered on benchmark design and performance assessment rather than computational innovation.
