MECHANISM: The paper computes conditions under which cooperation is favored in a two-layer network model. The model involves two layers: one layer implements a prisoner's dilemma game with payoffs determined by a cost $c$ and benefit $b$, while the other layer uses constant-selection dynamics where individuals compete based on fitness $r$. The evolutionary dynamics are governed by a death-Birth (dB) updating rule, where individuals update their strategies based on the weighted degree of their neighbors and the total payoff from both layers. The total payoff for an individual is the sum of payoffs from both layers. The model derives fixation probabilities for cooperators and mutants using recursive calculations involving transition probabilities of random walks on the network layers and stationary probabilities. The conditions for cooperation or spite are determined by comparing thresholds involving $b/c$, $r$, and network-specific quantities like $\theta$ and $\phi$, which depend on the structure of the network layers. The analysis includes examining how coupling between layers modifies these thresholds and how different network configurations (e.g., rings, complete graphs) influence the outcomes. The results are validated through exhaustive simulations on small networks and analytical derivations for larger networks.
