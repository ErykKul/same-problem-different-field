MECHANISM: This paper reviews the historical development of astronomical models and epistemological debates about the nature of celestial mechanics, from ancient geocentric models to Newtonian gravity, Einstein's relativity, and the modern problem of dark matter. It discusses how observational anomalies (e.g., Uranus's orbit, Mercury's precession) led to theoretical advancements, including the discovery of Neptune and the formulation of general relativity. The paper analyzes the shift from mathematical fictions to physical realities in astronomy, the role of empirical evidence in validating models, and the unresolved issue of dark matter as inferred from galaxy rotation curves and gravitational lensing. It does not present a new computational method, algorithm, or mathematical model.
