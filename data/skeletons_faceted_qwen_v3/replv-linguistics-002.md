MECHANISM: The paper computes the dynamics of a multi-population evolutionary game where strategies' fitness depends on intra- and inter-group interactions. Two populations interact with rates defined by a matrix P, and convergence/divergence strengths are encoded in matrix A. Fitness functions for each strategy are derived from these matrices, incorporating both intra-group (self-population) and inter-group (cross-population) contributions. The replicator-mutator equation governs strategy frequency changes, incorporating mutation rates between strategies within each population. The model linearizes the system to analyze equilibria stability, identifies stable equilibria under symmetric conditions, and applies numerical methods to asymmetric extensions. It compares predicted equilibria with empirical data from adolescent sociolinguistic behavior, estimating mutation rates from external literature. The analysis involves solving differential equations, characterizing phase spaces, and validating predictions against observed population states. The model assumes continuous-time dynamics in infinite, randomly mixing populations, with strategies' fitness evolving as population states change. It explores both analytical tractability in symmetric cases and numerical solutions for more complex, asymmetric scenarios.
