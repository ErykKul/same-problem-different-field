MECHANISM: The paper presents a conceptual framework for intelligence and consciousness, emphasizing the role of recursive neural architectures and context enrichment in forming and integrating causal connections. It argues that intelligence arises from creating, refining, and integrating connections between signals, actions, internal states, and learned structures. Context enrichment is described as the process of interpreting incoming signals using previously learned relational context, enabling efficient processing under metabolic constraints. The framework reframes predictive processing theories by proposing that prediction emerges from discovering stable contextual structures rather than forecasting future inputs. Consciousness is posited to arise when recursive systems explicitly learn and interpret the relational patterns of lower-order systems across time. The paper introduces the "systems explaining systems" principle, where higher-order systems interpret and regulate lower ones, creating a bidirectional flow of explanation and contextual guidance. This mechanism is conceptual, focusing on functional principles rather than specific algorithms or mathematical models. It does not propose a new computational method, simulation, or proof, but rather a theoretical account of how intelligence and consciousness might emerge from relational operations. The framework suggests that artificial intelligence may require multi-system architectures where higher modules interpret and regulate lower ones, rather than scaling single feedforward systems. The paper is presented as a preprint and does not claim to provide empirical validation or mathematical proofs of its claims.
