MECHANISM: The paper constructs a Hamiltonian matrix representing a quantum system on a high-dimensional graph with spatially decaying hopping amplitudes. The hopping amplitude between nodes decays exponentially with their graph distance, defined as the shortest path length on an underlying random regular graph (RRG) backbone. The model incorporates onsite disorder, represented by a normally distributed potential. The computational core involves two approaches: (1) numerical exact diagonalization of the Hamiltonian to compute eigenvalues, eigenstates, and derived diagnostics such as level spacing ratios, inverse participation ratios (IPRs), and spatial correlation functions; and (2) analytical renormalized perturbation theory to compute the imaginary part of the local self-energy and derive phase diagrams. The analysis includes scaling studies of IPRs to identify critical behavior, distinguishing between delocalized, localized, and multifractal phases. The methods compare deterministic and random hopping variants, analyzing how the interplay between hopping range (controlled by a length scale ξ) and disorder strength determines the phase diagram. The results are validated through consistency checks between numerical and analytical approaches, including the identification of a Kosterlitz–Thouless–like transition with two-parameter scaling.
