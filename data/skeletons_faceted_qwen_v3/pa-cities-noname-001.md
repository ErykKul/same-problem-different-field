MECHANISM: The paper derives Zipf’s law from a stochastic model combining proportional growth (Gibrat’s law) with random birth and death processes of entities. It assumes entities undergo continuous multiplicative growth modeled as geometric Brownian motion, where growth rates are independent of size. The model incorporates a balance condition between the birth rate, death rate, and growth volatility, ensuring the distribution of entity sizes converges to a power law. Deviations from strict Gibrat’s law (e.g., size-dependent volatility or growth rates) are analyzed, showing that Zipf’s law holds if volatility asymptotically scales with size and growth rates do not exceed volatility. The framework accounts for time-varying birth rates, sudden entity exits (e.g., below a size threshold), and external shocks. It derives steady-state distributions using diffusion equations and Kolmogorov’s framework, showing that firm death at low sizes does not invalidate Zipf’s law asymptotically. The model also considers mergers, spin-offs, and non-stationary birth rates, extending the balance condition to generalized scenarios. The paper identifies the tail index of the power law as dependent on economic parameters like average growth rates, birth rates, and death hazard rates. It provides a mathematical proof that Zipf’s law emerges if and only if the balance condition holds, linking deviations in the tail index to violations of this condition.
