MECHANISM: This paper analyzes the persistence of European open repositories by aggregating data from multiple sources, including repository registries and web-scraped datasets, to identify 'dead' and 'zombie' repositories. It constructs a unique dataset of historical repository locations and OAI-PMH endpoints, then uses this data to text mine the CORE corpus of scholarly outputs, quantifying how impersistent repository content has permeated the scholarly literature. The study evaluates the impact of repository instability on the scholarly record, identifies patterns in repository decay, and discusses implications for policy and infrastructure management. It defines 'dead' repositories as those that are permanently unavailable and 'zombie' repositories as those that are temporarily unavailable but later reactivated under new domains. The analysis relies on empirical data collection, cross-referencing registry information with web accessibility checks and citation tracking, and does not involve mathematical modeling, algorithmic computation, or simulation. The findings highlight the prevalence of dead repositories and their effects on scholarly integrity, but the paper does not propose or implement a computational method for solving the persistence crisis.
