MECHANISM: The paper analyzes a dataset of policy documents to characterize the presence and distribution of scholarly and policy references across multiple dimensions. It begins by collecting metadata such as publication year, source type, country, language, subject area, and policy topics. The dataset is then filtered to include only policy documents indexed by Overton, a global policy index. Descriptive statistics are computed to quantify the frequency and distribution of references, including counts of scholarly and policy citations. The analysis involves aggregating reference data across categories, calculating percentiles, means, and measures of skewness and kurtosis to describe the highly skewed and zero-inflated distributions. Coverage is measured as the proportion of documents containing at least one reference, while total references are summed across the dataset. The geometric mean is applied to account for the zero-inflated nature of the data, with adjustments made by adding one to reference counts before logarithmic transformation. Results are stratified by publication year, source type, country, language, subject area, and policy topics to reveal patterns and biases in reference distribution. The study does not introduce new computational methods but applies standard statistical techniques to existing data.
