MECHANISM: The paper computes a dual-learner framework for continual learning, where two distinct components—a fast learner and a meta learner—interact to balance knowledge transfer and integration. The fast learner rapidly adapts to new tasks by leveraging prior knowledge stored in the meta learner, while the meta learner incrementally updates to minimize catastrophic forgetting by consolidating new experiences with existing knowledge. The fast learner's adaptation is guided by an adaptive warm-up mechanism that selects between initializing with the meta learner's parameters, resetting to a random policy, or fine-tuning from the previous fast learner. This selection is based on a one-vs-all hypothesis test comparing the expected rewards of these strategies. When the hypothesis test rejects the null hypothesis (that the meta learner's policy is not superior), the fast learner is initialized with the meta learner's policy and trained with behavior cloning regularization to align its early exploration with the meta learner's prior knowledge. The meta learner updates its knowledge by solving an optimization problem that minimizes the divergence between its current policy and the policies of all previously encountered tasks, weighted by their relative importance. This is achieved through an incremental update rule derived from the KL divergence between policies, which effectively aggregates knowledge across tasks without storing all previous Q functions. The algorithm maintains a meta buffer to store state-action pairs from recent interactions, which are used to approximate the importance weights for the incremental updates. The process ensures that the meta learner retains a stable representation of accumulated knowledge while allowing the fast learner to adapt quickly to new environments. The framework is applicable to both value-based and policy-based reinforcement learning, with the policy-based variant using forward KL divergence or Wasserstein distance to measure policy differences during knowledge integration. The overall mechanism ensures efficient cumulative learning by explicitly addressing catastrophic forgetting and enabling adaptive knowledge transfer between tasks.
