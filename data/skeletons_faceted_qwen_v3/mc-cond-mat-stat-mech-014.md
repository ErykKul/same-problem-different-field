MECHANISM: The paper computes the thermodynamic entropy production and work requirements of polynomial Dense Associative Memory networks operating at finite temperature. It defines a Hamiltonian energy function over a network of interacting entities, where each entity's state is a binary variable. The system evolves via a continuous-time Markov process governed by transition rates derived from the Hamiltonian's gradient. The method uses dynamic mean field theory to approximate the network's state distribution by aligning it with stored patterns, calculating free energy, work, and entropy production. It derives self-consistency equations for alignment variables, characterizes relaxation times and failure modes under external driving, and identifies tradeoffs between entropy production, memory retrieval accuracy, and operation speed. The analysis assumes a mean field limit where interactions are averaged, and the system size is large. It distinguishes between equilibrium and out-of-equilibrium behavior, quantifies dissipation via the second law of thermodynamics, and derives exact expressions for work and power costs under arbitrary driving protocols. The method applies to networks with polynomial nonlinearities, including Hopfield models as a special case, and identifies failure modes unique to higher-order networks at nonzero temperatures.
