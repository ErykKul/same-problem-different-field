MECHANISM: The paper computes the evolution of strategies in a large population of agents interacting through a financial network. Agents adapt between two strategies (risky or risk-free) to maximize expected returns, with interactions modeled as a random graph. The system is analyzed using replicator dynamics, which reduces the complex network behavior to an ordinary differential equation (ODE). The ODE's attractor determines the equilibrium strategies, which are shown to converge almost surely. The analysis involves solving random fixed point equations for clearing vectors, which represent the settlement of debts after economic shocks. These equations are approximated asymptotically, leading to expressions for expected returns and default probabilities. The replicator dynamics are studied to determine conditions under which mixed evolutionary stable strategies (ESS) emerge, where expected returns of both groups balance. The system's behavior is validated through Monte Carlo simulations, confirming the ODE's predictions. The model incorporates binomial returns for risky investments, liability settlements under bankruptcy rules, and the influence of network structure on percolation of shocks. The analysis derives conditions for convergence to pure or mixed ESS, depending on the relative sizes of the groups and the parameters of the economic shocks.
