MECHANISM: The paper computes a nonlinear system of ordinary differential equations modeling the dynamics of five interacting populations: unskilled unemployed, skilled unemployed, employed, discouraged individuals, and vacancies. The equations describe rates of change for each group based on parameters representing transition rates between states (e.g., skill acquisition, discouragement, employment, vacancy creation). The model calculates equilibrium points by solving the system when all derivatives equal zero, derives conditions for stability using eigenvalue analysis and the Routh-Hurwitz criterion, and validates results through numerical simulations with the fourth-order Runge-Kutta method. The analysis includes proving boundedness and non-negativity of solutions, deriving critical points, and examining stability under parameter variations. The model introduces a "discouraged" population class, defined by individuals withdrawing from active job search due to persistent rejection or market constraints, and incorporates delayed effects in vacancy generation. The mathematical framework uses algebraic manipulations to reduce the system to polynomial equations for equilibrium analysis and derives conditions for the existence of positive roots in higher-degree polynomials to identify stable states.
