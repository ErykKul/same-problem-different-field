MECHANISM: The paper computes a decomposition of Hilbert space into dynamically disconnected subspaces (Krylov subspaces) using two algebraic methods: commutant algebra (CA) and integer characteristic polynomial factorization (ICPF). It proves that if the center of the commutant algebra has rational eigenvalues, the ICPF method yields an equal or finer fragmentation than the CA method. The process involves identifying rational operators, constructing characteristic polynomials from Hamiltonian matrices, factoring these polynomials into irreducible components, and mapping polynomial factors to Krylov subspaces. The CA method decomposes Hilbert space via commutant algebras and their irreducible representations, while the ICPF method leverages polynomial factorizations to identify subspaces. The paper establishes conditions under which these methods align, showing that rational eigenvalues in the commutant algebra ensure the ICPF method's subspaces are contained within CA subspaces. It also demonstrates that when ICPF yields a finer fragmentation, the Krylov subspace basis depends on Hamiltonian coefficients. The analysis involves algebraic manipulations of operators, eigenvalue computations, and polynomial decomposition techniques.
