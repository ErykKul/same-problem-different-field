MECHANISM: This paper analyzes the socio-technical risks of generative AI (GenAI) by formalizing synthetic reality as a layered stack (content, identity, interaction, institutions), expanding a taxonomy of GenAI harms across personal, economic, informational, and socio-technical domains, articulating qualitative shifts introduced by GenAI (cost collapse, throughput, customization, micro-segmentation, provenance gaps, trust erosion), and synthesizing recent risk realizations (2023–2025) into a case bank illustrating mechanisms in fraud, elections, harassment, documentation, and supply-chain compromise. It proposes a mitigation stack addressing provenance infrastructure, platform governance, institutional workflow redesign, and public resilience, and outlines a research agenda focused on measuring epistemic security. The paper concludes with the Generative AI Paradox, highlighting how synthetic media ubiquity may lead societies to discount digital evidence, increasing the cost of truth for democratic and economic institutions. No computational method, model, or algorithm is presented; the analysis is qualitative and conceptual.
