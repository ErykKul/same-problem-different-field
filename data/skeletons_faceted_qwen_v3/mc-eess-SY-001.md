MECHANISM: The paper computes a mathematical model relating filter characteristics to underlying constants using a sharp-filter approximation. It defines a transfer function parameterized by constants (A_p, b_p, B_u) and derives closed-form expressions for magnitude-based (peak frequency, quality factors, convexity) and phase-based (group delay, phase accumulation) characteristics. These expressions quantify how filter behavior depends on constants, enabling estimation of constants from reported characteristics. The method uses characteristic ratios (e.g., Q_erb/N_β) to infer constraints on constants, leveraging relationships between parameters and observed values. It analyzes how variations in constants affect filter behavior, including trade-offs between sharpness and group delay. The framework generalizes to multiple filter classes by expressing all characteristics in terms of a unified parameterization. Estimation involves solving equations derived from characteristic definitions and reported data, with emphasis on using ratios to avoid ill-posed inverse problems. The approach includes visualizing characteristic dependencies and applying findings to human auditory filterbanks using recent physiological and psychoacoustic observations.
