MECHANISM: This paper investigates the potential of Mixed Reality (MR) to enhance collaborative digital audio workstations (DAWs) through qualitative and speculative design techniques. It describes the development and evaluation of an MR-DAW system, which enables geographically dispersed users to control a shared DAW instance using physical foot pedals and spatial UI elements. The system allows real-time audio streaming and control message transmission, facilitating unencumbered musical interaction. The study involves interviews with 20 musicians to analyze current DAW workflows, assess the usability of the MR-DAW prototype, and speculate on future collaborative music-making in MR environments. Key features include loop track recording synchronized to the first user's tempo, track playback control via foot pedals and spatial UI, and real-time audio communication between users. The evaluation focuses on thematic analysis of user experiences and usability metrics from a NASA-TLX questionnaire. The paper emphasizes the affordances of MR for remote collaboration but does not propose or implement any mathematical, algorithmic, or computational method beyond the described system design and evaluation.
