MECHANISM: The paper computes a systematic procedure for solving a set of partial differential equations (PDEs) arising in control design. It begins by identifying a homogeneous subsystem of the PDEs, which admits independent solutions corresponding to characteristic coordinates. A coordinate transformation is introduced, decomposing the state space into characteristic coordinates and auxiliary variables. The original PDE is reduced to a lower-dimensional PDE by exploiting the invertibility of a matrix derived from the characteristic coordinates. The reduced PDE is further simplified using symmetry conditions on the Hessian of the energy function. A mean-value theorem for vector-valued functions is applied to construct a solution to the reduced PDE. The solution is expressed as an integral of a matrix and gradient terms derived from the symmetry conditions. The method ensures the existence of a smooth energy function satisfying both the original PDE and convexity constraints. The procedure is validated by demonstrating that existing control designs implicitly rely on the same symmetry properties. The final energy function is constructed by integrating the derived gradient fields, ensuring compatibility with the desired equilibrium conditions.
