# Causal Discovery Should Embrace the Wisdom of the Crowd ## 1 Introduction The pursuit of understanding causal relationships represents the cornerstone of scientific progress, bridging the gap between statistical observation and actionable, mechanistic knowledge. In the modern era, this philosophical endeavor has transformed into a rigorous computational challenge , thanks to the emergence of probabilistic and statistical languages for causality, the advancement of computational powers, and the rise of AI and machine learning (AI/ML). Now, causal discovery, the task of inferring the underlying causal structure for a set of variables under explicit model assumptions, has become a fundamental problem in science and engineering . Various formulations of this problem have been proposed, among which learning a DAG that represents the data-generating process is now a canonical approach. However, DAG learning has long faced a critical bottleneck of computational intractability. As the number of variables increases, the search space for possible causal structures grows super-exponentially with the number of variables, making optimal structure learning NP-hard .
