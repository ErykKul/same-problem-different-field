# Terrestrial Test of Shapiro Time Delay: Forth test of Einstein General Theory of Relativity Terrestrial Test of Shapiro Time Delay: Forth test of Einstein General Theory of Relativity (May 25th, 2026) Farhad Hakimi and Hosain Hakimi, emails: fhakimi@alum.mit.edu, hhakimi@alum.mit.edu Abstract: We present a compact fiber-optic Sagnac interferometer designed to measure the relativistic Shapiro time delay in a terrestrial laboratory. The instrument converts tiny propagation delays from spacetime curvature into directly measurable optical phase shifts, enabling a tabletop determination of the parametrized post-Newtonian parameter γ. We describe the sensor architecture, analyze its expected signal-to-noise performance, and illustrate a representative implementation with realistic parameters. The results demonstrate that laboratory-scale Shapiro delays can be detected with sufficient precision, providing a practical platform for tabletop tests of general relativity. This work establishes a novel interferometric sensor and a framework for future precision experiments probing fundamental relativistic effects. I. Introduction In the early 1960s, Irwin I.
