# Variable Importance in Generalized Linear Models -- A Unifying View Using Shapley Values

## Abstract

Variable importance in regression analyses is of considerable interest in a variety of fields. There is no unique method for assessing variable importance. However, a substantial share of the available literature employs Shapley values, either explicitly or implicitly, to decompose a suitable goodness-of-fit measure, in the linear regression model typically the classical $R^{2}$ . Beyond linear regression, there is no generally accepted goodness-of-fit measure, only a variety of pseudo- $R^{2}$ s. We formulate and discuss the desirable properties of goodness-of-fit measures that enable Shapley values to be interpreted in terms of relative, and even absolute, importance. We suggest to use a pseudo- $R^{2}$ based on the Kullback-Leibler divergence, the Kullback-Leibler $R^{2}$ , which has a convenient form for generalized linear models and permits to unify and extend previous work on variable importance for linear and nonlinear models. Several examples are presented, using data from public health and insurance. Keywords. Averaging over orderings; Dominance analysis; Goodness of fit; Hierarchical partitioning; Kullback-Leibler divergence; Regression
