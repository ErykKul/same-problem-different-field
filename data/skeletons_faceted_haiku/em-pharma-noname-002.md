MECHANISM: The method estimates an unknown mixing distribution over latent per-entity parameter vectors in a hierarchical model. Each entity yields a vector of observations equal to a known nonlinear function of its latent parameter vector plus additive zero-mean Gaussian noise whose covariance may depend on those parameters, and the latent vectors are independent draws from a common unknown distribution F. The marginal log-likelihood is a sum over entities of the logarithm of an integral of each entity's conditional density against F. By a Caratheodory-type argument the maximizing F is discrete with at most as many support points as entities, reducing the task to choosing support locations and nonnegative weights summing to one. For fixed support points, maximizing the concave log-likelihood over the weights is a convex program, solved by a primal-dual interior-point iteration that follows a central path and converges quadratically via Newton steps on the optimality (KKT) conditions. The support set is refined by an adaptive grid: start from a large space-filling grid, optimize weights, delete low-weight points, then expand by adjoining hypercube vertices around survivors, repeating until the likelihood gain vanishes. A second, Bayesian route places a Dirichlet-process prior on F and represents it through a stick-breaking construction, with weights formed from successive Beta-distributed fractions of a unit stick and support points drawn from a base distribution. Truncating the stick-breaking series at a finite number of components yields a finite mixture with random weights and locations whose joint posterior is drawn by blocked Gibbs sampling. Posterior draws of the full distribution produce samples of its moments and of any functional, from which point estimates and credibility intervals are formed. The two routes thus yield a discrete estimate of the same latent distribution, one as a maximum-likelihood point estimate and one as a sampled posterior.
