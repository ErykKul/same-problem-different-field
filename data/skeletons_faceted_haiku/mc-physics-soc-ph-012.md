MECHANISM: A geometric framework formalizes effective interactions among system components as points in a low-dimensional metric manifold. Collective organizations are defined as equivalence classes of interaction descriptors generating the same macroscopic behavior. Geometric separability of organizations requires bounded separation distances in interaction space. The analysis derives bounds on the number of pairwise separable organizations using packing theorems from metric geometry, proves impossibility statements regarding unbounded organizational diversification, and extends results to time-dependent and nonreciprocal interaction spaces.
