MECHANISM: A model represents the distribution of observations in an n-dimensional space as a weighted superposition of m multivariate Gaussian components, each with a mixing weight, mean vector, and covariance matrix. Given N observations, parameters are estimated by alternating two steps from an initial guess. In the expectation step, each observation's posterior responsibility for each component is computed from the current parameters using the component densities and the total mixture density. In the maximization step, every component's mean, covariance, and weight are updated as responsibility-weighted averages over all observations. The steps repeat until the total likelihood, the product of per-observation mixture densities, converges, a procedure that monotonically increases the likelihood. The number of components is chosen by starting from one and increasing it until a multivariate goodness-of-fit test (the maximal discrepancy between empirical and model cumulative distributions, with thresholds obtained by Monte-Carlo simulation) is satisfied. To mitigate local optima, the estimation is restarted from many random initializations and the highest-likelihood solution is retained. Parameter stability is checked by resampling observations with replacement and re-estimating. To decide whether an observation belongs to a designated subset of components, a logarithmic likelihood-ratio statistic between in-subset and out-of-subset components is compared against a decision threshold, which is the most powerful binary test. The same ratio provides a continuous score that ranks observations by their probability of belonging to the target subset.
