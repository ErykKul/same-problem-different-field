MECHANISM: Given N entities, each with one or more observations and a known conditional density p(observation | parameter vector), the goal is to estimate the common but unknown distribution F over a compact parameter space from which the entity-specific latent parameter vectors are drawn. The objective is a likelihood formed as a product over entities of the integral of the conditional density against F, i.e., a mixture that marginalizes the latent parameters. By convexity arguments the maximizer of this likelihood over all distributions is discrete with at most N support points, reducing an infinite-dimensional problem to finding K≤N support locations and their nonnegative weights summing to one. The log-likelihood then becomes a sum over entities of the log of a weighted sum of conditional densities evaluated at the support locations. The computation alternates two subproblems: with locations fixed, solve for the weights as a convex program via an interior-point method operating on the N×K matrix of conditional-density values; with weights fixed, refine the locations by an adaptive grid search. The location step seeds a large grid in the bounded parameter hypercube, prunes points whose weights fall near zero, and around each surviving point spawns up to 2×d candidate offspring (d = parameter dimension), discarding offspring outside the region or too close to a parent. A condensation step merges points closer than a threshold. The two steps iterate, each provably non-decreasing in the objective, with an adaptive precision parameter shrunk as improvement stalls, until the objective change falls below tolerance. The output is the estimated discrete distribution (a set of locations with weights) that maximizes the likelihood.
DOMAIN: nonparametric maximum-likelihood mixture distribution estimation; population pharmacokinetics
STRUCTURE: other: alternating convex optimization with adaptive grid search
DATA_OBJECT: point set
INFERENCE: frequentist point estimate
PROBLEM_FORM: estimation
DISTRIBUTION: outcome continuous; estimator assumes no parametric shape for the mixing distribution (discrete nonparematric estimate, conditional density assumed known)
COMPLEXITY: consistency (converges to true distribution as N grows); discrete solution with at most N support points; weight subproblem convex/polynomial-iterative with monotone non-decreasing iterations
