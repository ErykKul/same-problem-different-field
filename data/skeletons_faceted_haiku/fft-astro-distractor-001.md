MECHANISM: We model a one-dimensional sequence of observations as a multivariate normal distribution whose mean is a deterministic parametric function and whose covariance is built from kernel functions of the lag between sample times. The covariance matrix is assembled as a sum of component stationary kernels, each a function of |t_i − t_j| and parameterized by a few hyper-parameters. A representative kernel is the covariance of a stochastically driven damped harmonic oscillator, an exponentially decaying cosine, whose limiting cases reproduce different spectral shapes. An independent white-noise variance is added to the diagonal. For a chosen set of hyper-parameters we evaluate the Gaussian log-likelihood, which requires solving a linear system against the covariance matrix and computing its log-determinant. A structured-matrix formulation reduces this linear-algebra cost from cubic to linear in the number of points, at the price of restricting to one-dimensional stationary kernels expressible as mixtures of exponentials. We explore the joint posterior of the hyper-parameters by Markov-chain Monte-Carlo sampling, summarising each by the median or mode and inter-percentile range of the chains. To compare competing covariance structures we compute each model's marginal likelihood (evidence), the integral of likelihood times prior over all parameters, approximated by importance sampling. Ratios of evidences rank the models, with the marginalisation automatically penalising additional parameters. Finally the deterministic signal and the covariance hyper-parameters are fit jointly to recover the deterministic-signal parameters and their uncertainties.
DOMAIN: exoplanet transit photometry and stellar variability
STRUCTURE: dense linear algebra
