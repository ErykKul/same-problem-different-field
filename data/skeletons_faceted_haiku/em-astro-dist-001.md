MECHANISM: A large set of interacting point masses is evolved forward in time under mutual long-range pairwise attraction. Initial positions and velocities are generated by displacing entities from a regular lattice according to second-order perturbation theory, seeded from a prescribed input spectrum. The equations of motion are integrated over discrete time steps, where at each step the pairwise force field is split into a short-range part, evaluated through a hierarchical spatial decomposition over nearby entities, and a long-range part, solved on a regular grid by assigning densities and applying a fast Fourier transform. Configurations are stored as snapshots at successive times. Within each snapshot, bound groups are identified by clustering entities in the combined position–velocity (phase) space. Groups in consecutive snapshots are linked into hierarchical trees by matching progenitors to descendants, with the domain partitioned into overlapping subvolumes processed independently and merged through a deduplication pass. A second-order statistic of the density field is estimated by interpolating entity weights onto a mesh and transforming it, using a multi-level coordinate-folding scheme that repeatedly halves the box to extend the accessible frequency range, with corrections for aliasing. A histogram of group masses is accumulated, and a low-dimensional parametric relation between group mass and internal concentration is fit to the data. Results are validated by cross-comparing independent runs at differing resolution.
