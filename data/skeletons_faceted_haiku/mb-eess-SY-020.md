MECHANISM: An analytical metric called the Generalized Fiedler Vector (GFV) characterizes dynamic connectivity in power systems by integrating the network Laplacian with nodal inertia distribution. The method quantifies nodes' resilience to stochastic power fluctuations from variable renewable generation or load variability. Network topology determines the Laplacian structure; inertia values weight nodes according to their physical and control parameters. The GFV identifies spatially-localized measures of nodal frequency strength without requiring sensitivity analyses or center-of-inertia (COI) simplifications. Unlike COI-based metrics that collapse spatial information, GFV preserves node-specific frequency response characteristics. The metric enables location-sensitive assessment of frequency stability margins and can guide placement of grid-forming devices for improved robustness.
