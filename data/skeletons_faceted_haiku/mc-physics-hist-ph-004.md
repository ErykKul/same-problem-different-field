MECHANISM: Philosophical tractatus presenting a conceptual framework for consciousness without a computational method. The work defines consciousness through three separable layers: (1) appearance (private agent-relative interface), (2) access (channels to reports/control), (3) formal structure (invariants under re-description). Defines conscious episodes as temporally localized regimes with structure, integration, and stabilization. Uses information-theoretic concept of integration surplus (mutual information across time) to formalize "whole-over-parts" but without deriving or implementing algorithms. Treats self as a dynamical index rather than substance. Emphasizes operational access requirements for scientific claims while acknowledging limits where no coupling can extract private experience. The framework is philosophical and conceptual rather than algorithmic.
