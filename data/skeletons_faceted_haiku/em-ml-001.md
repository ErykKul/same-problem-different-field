MECHANISM: A set of N entities in a D-dimensional real space is approximated by a mixture of C components, each defined by a center vector and a single shared scalar scale parameter, with equal component weights. The objective is the sum over entities of the log of the mixture density, which is approximated twice to reduce cost. First, a weighted subset of N′ < N entities with positive weights replaces the full data sum (a coreset likelihood). Second, a variational lower bound replaces the log-objective via Jensen's inequality, using truncated assignment distributions that retain only the C′ components of largest joint probability per entity and assign hard zero to the rest. The method then alternates two updates until convergence. In the assignment step, for each entity the C′ nearest centers are selected, where a pairwise comparison of distances is proven sufficient to monotonically increase the bound, and the search is confined to precomputed neighborhood index sets of nearby components to avoid scanning all C. In the parameter step, each center is recomputed as the weight-and-responsibility-weighted average of entities, and the scale parameter as a weighted mean of squared distances. Initialization uses a two-pass weighted-subset construction, a seeding procedure based on independent short Markov chains, and a cheap scale estimate from nearest-center distances. The dominant per-iteration cost is reduced from O(NCD) to O(N′G²D), where G ≪ C bounds the neighborhood size.
