MECHANISM: Agents hold fixed private opinions and strategically choose what opinion to post on a social platform, trading off utility from popularity (measured by likes) against utility from personal authenticity. At each step, agents post an opinion from a discrete set of realized opinions in society; other agents then view posts according to a visibility rule and decide whether to like them based on opinion-matching. Agents derive utility from likes on their own posts, from exposure to content matching their authentic opinion, and incur disutility from exposure to misaligned content weighted by the distance and popularity of that content. The equilibrium is characterized by subgame perfect Nash equilibrium, where likes depend deterministically on opinion-matching between poster and viewer, and agents' posting choices induce reshuffling of the opinion distribution expressed on the platform.
