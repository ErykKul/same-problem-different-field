MECHANISM: Construct a finite-state Markov transition operator as the sum of a degree-normalized weighted adjacency matrix premultiplied by a diagonal matrix of per-state continuation probabilities and a rank-one term that, with the complementary probability, resets the state to a fixed probability vector. Compute the chain's stationary distribution, interpreted as the long-run occupation frequency over states. Solve the stationary fixed-point linear equation for this vector. Exploiting substochasticity of the scaled transition matrix, invert the operator (I − diagonal·normalized-adjacency) to obtain a closed form: the resolvent applied to the reset vector, then normalized to sum to one. Define a second functional as the limiting frequency of states occupied immediately before a reset, equal to the same resolvent applied to the reset vector and multiplied on the right by the diagonal of reset probabilities. Both functionals admit renewal-theoretic readings as ratios of expected visit counts to expected inter-reset durations. For symmetric weight matrices, derive reciprocity identities relating a state's score from another's viewpoint to the reverse, using a geometric (Neumann) series expansion of the resolvent that decomposes contributions by path length. Specialize parameters (e.g., reset probabilities proportional to powers of degree) and expand the resolvent via a Laurent series about a singular limit to obtain leading-order asymptotic expressions in terms of the unrestarted chain's stationary distribution and degrees. Recover known closed forms as the special case of constant continuation probability.
